## Why it matters for you

You don't have to choose between your mind and your faith. If you have ever looked at the night sky, or at the elegance of a mathematical law, and felt wonder, you have already felt what drove many great scientists to their work. Seeing the universe as made by a rational God can make science more meaningful, not less.

## The short answer

Science asks how the universe works; faith asks why there is a universe at all, and what it is for. They are different questions. Many founders of modern science, including Kepler, Newton, Boyle and Faraday, were Christians who saw their work as reading the book of nature.

## What Christians believe

John's Gospel begins: "In the beginning was the Word", in Greek the *Logos*: reason, order, meaning. Christians believe the universe is intelligible because it was made by a rational God. That conviction, that nature has laws we can discover, is part of the soil modern science grew from.

## The great teachers

**Augustine** warned, around 400 AD, that Christians should not read Genesis in ways that contradict what can be known about the natural world, or they would make the faith look foolish.

**Aquinas** held that faith and reason cannot truly contradict each other, because both come from the same God. An apparent clash means one of them has been misread.

## The modern debate
tag: Strongest objections included

In 1927 **Georges Lemaître**, a Belgian Catholic priest and physicist, proposed that the universe is expanding, the idea that became the Big Bang theory. Today believers point to the universe having a beginning, and to its apparent fine-tuning for life.

Critics answer that a multiverse or future physics may explain both, and that the Galileo affair shows the Church has resisted science before. Both points deserve a fair hearing.

The geneticist **Francis Collins**, who led the Human Genome Project, is one of many working scientists who see no conflict between their science and their faith.

## The sources

Augustine, *The Literal Meaning of Genesis* I.19; Aquinas, *Summa contra Gentiles* I.7; Lemaître's 1927 paper on the expanding universe; Francis Collins, *The Language of God* (2006). From the other side, read Richard Dawkins, *The God Delusion* (2006), alongside.
