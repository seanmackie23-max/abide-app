## The short answer

Walk into a great cathedral, or hear a choir sing Allegri's Miserere, and something opens in you that ordinary life keeps closed. Christians believe that is not just a feeling. Beauty is a real glimpse of God, who is beauty itself, breaking through into the world.

## What Christians believe

When God made the world, he saw that it was "very good" (Genesis 1:31). In Christ, God took on a body, so matter itself can carry holiness. That is why Christians have poured their best into stone, glass, paint and music. The old tradition says truth, goodness and beauty are one in God, three ways the same light reaches us.

## The great teachers

Before Christ, **Plato** described a ladder of love that climbs from beautiful bodies to beautiful souls to Beauty itself.

**Augustine** met God as beauty: "Late have I loved you, O Beauty ever ancient, ever new, late have I loved you!"

**Aquinas** said beauty needs wholeness, proportion and radiance. And in the 1140s **Abbot Suger** rebuilt the abbey church of Saint-Denis near Paris to be filled with coloured light, believing that material beauty can lift the mind to God. Gothic architecture was born.

## The modern voices
tag: Strongest objections included

Dostoevsky gave one of his characters the line that "beauty will save the world". The theologian **Hans Urs von Balthasar** built a whole theology on beauty, warning that a world which loses beauty soon loses the power to pray and to love.

The philosopher **Roger Scruton**, in his BBC film *Why Beauty Matters* (2009), argued that modern art and architecture turned away from beauty, and that our souls are starved as a result. He saw the experience of beauty as a call to the sacred.

There are real objections. Many say beauty is in the eye of the beholder, shaped by culture. Within Christianity, too, people have feared beauty could become an idol: the iconoclasts of 8th-century Byzantium destroyed images, and the Scottish Reformation stripped churches bare to keep worship centred on the Word. That tension is part of the story.
