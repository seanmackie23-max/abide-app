## Why it matters for you

As technology does more of what we once did, it is easy to feel replaceable. Christianity says you are not your output. You were "fearfully and wonderfully made", known and loved before you achieved anything. That truth matters more, not less, in an age of artificial intelligence.

## The short answer

As machines learn to write, talk and reason like us, an old question returns with new force: what is a human being? Christianity answers that we are more than what we can do. A person is not valuable because of their intelligence or output, but because they are made in the image of God, for love.

## What Christians believe

"God created man in his own image" (Genesis 1:27). Christians believe a human is a unity of body and soul, able to know truth, to love, to choose, to worship and to be known by God. Intelligence is part of that, but not the whole of it, and not its foundation.

## The great teachers

**Augustine** marvelled at the depths of human memory and self-awareness, and found God there, "more inward than my most inward part".

**Aquinas** taught that the soul is the *form* of the body, what makes this living body a person, and that our intellect reaches beyond matter to grasp universal truths.

**Pascal** wrote: "Man is but a reed, the most feeble thing in nature; but he is a thinking reed." Our greatness lies not in power but in knowing.

## The modern debate
tag: Strongest objections included

**Alan Turing** proposed in 1950 that if a machine's conversation can't be told apart from a human's, we should call it intelligent. The philosopher **John Searle** replied with his "Chinese Room" (1980): manipulating symbols is not the same as understanding them. **David Chalmers** named the "hard problem" of consciousness: why there is inner experience at all.

In 2025 the Vatican published *Antiqua et Nova*, a document on AI and human intelligence, arguing that AI should serve human dignity rather than replace human relationships.

Many philosophers and scientists disagree with the Christian view. They hold that the mind is simply what the brain does, so a sufficiently complex machine could one day be conscious too. That debate is open.
