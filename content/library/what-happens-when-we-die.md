## The short answer

Christianity takes death seriously: it is real, and it is an enemy, which is why we grieve. But Christians believe it is not the end. Because Jesus rose from the dead, they hope that those who die are held safe by God, and that one day God will raise the dead and make all things new.

## What Christians believe

Jesus said: "I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies" (John 11:25). Christian hope is not mainly about souls floating away to the clouds. It is about the resurrection of the body and a renewed creation, where God "will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (Revelation 21:4).

## The great teachers

**Augustine** described the final hope as rest in God: "you have made us for yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you."

**Aquinas** described heaven as the *beatific vision*: seeing God as he is, the fulfilment of every human longing to know and to love.

The poet **John Donne** (17th century) wrote to death itself: "Death, be not proud ... Death, thou shalt die."

## The modern debate
tag: Strongest objections included

The New Testament scholar **N.T. Wright**, in *Surprised by Hope* (2008), argues that many Christians have misunderstood their own hope: heaven is not the final destination; resurrection and new creation are.

The strongest objection comes from neuroscience: our thoughts, memories and personality seem to depend completely on the brain, so naturalists conclude that death is simply the end. Christians answer that the resurrection is precisely not a ghostly survival but God's re-creation of the whole person.
