## The short answer

John's Gospel opens: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." The Greek word is *Logos*: word, reason, meaning, the ordering principle of everything. Christianity makes an astonishing claim: that the meaning at the heart of reality became a human being.

## What Christians believe

Through the Logos "all things were made" (John 1:3), and "the Word became flesh, and lived among us" (John 1:14). So the universe is not random noise but something spoken, with an order we can understand. And that order is personal: the reason behind everything has a face, Jesus Christ.

## The great teachers

Long before Christ, the Greek philosopher **Heraclitus** (around 500 BC) spoke of the Logos as the rational principle running through all things, and the Stoics developed the idea.

**Justin Martyr** (2nd century), a philosopher who became a Christian, taught that "seeds of the Logos" were scattered through all human wisdom, so that whatever the philosophers said truly belonged to Christ.

**Augustine** and **Aquinas** built on this: because the world is made through divine reason, faith and reason belong together.

## The modern readings
tag: Includes thinkers outside the tradition

**Pope Benedict XVI**, in his 2006 lecture at Regensburg, argued that acting against reason is contrary to God's nature, and that faith cut off from reason, or reason cut off from faith, both go wrong.

Scientists have long marvelled that the universe can be understood at all. Albert Einstein remarked that the most incomprehensible thing about the universe is that it is comprehensible.

**Jordan Peterson** describes the Logos as truthful speech that brings order out of chaos, as God speaks creation into being in Genesis 1. **Jonathan Pageau** explores how the Logos joins heaven and earth, meaning and matter.
