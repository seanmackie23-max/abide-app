## The short answer

Christianity doesn't explain suffering away. Whole books of the Bible, like Job and many of the Psalms, are people protesting to God. The Christian claim is not that suffering makes sense, but that God did not stay distant from it: in Jesus, God suffered and died alongside us.

## What Christians believe

A world with real love needs real freedom, and freedom can be misused. Creation is not yet finished. God is with those who suffer, and the resurrection promises that suffering will not have the last word: God will "wipe away every tear" (Revelation 21:4).

## The great teachers

**Augustine** argued that evil is not a thing in itself but the absence or corruption of good, as blindness is the absence of sight.

**Aquinas** held that God permits evil only because he can bring a greater good even out of it.

**Julian of Norwich**, an English mystic of the 14th century, received in a vision the words: "All shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well."

## The modern voices
tag: Strongest objections included

The strongest protest comes in Dostoevsky's *The Brothers Karamazov*, where Ivan refuses any heaven built on the suffering of a child. Dostoevsky, a believer, gave the objection its full force.

**C.S. Lewis** wrote *The Problem of Pain* as a thinker, then *A Grief Observed* after his wife died, a far rawer book. **Simone Weil** wrote of "affliction" as the place where God can seem most absent, and is most near.
