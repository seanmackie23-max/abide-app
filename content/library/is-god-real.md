## Why it matters for you

This is the question beneath every other question. If God is real, then your life is not an accident: you were wanted, you are known, and there is meaning to what you do. If you are unsure, that's fine. Honest searching is where faith often begins, and Jesus promised that those who seek will find.

## The short answer

Thoughtful people have argued both ways for thousands of years, so there is no shame in asking. Christians point to three kinds of reasons: the world itself (why is there anything at all, and why is it so ordered?), the moral sense in every person, and the life, death and reported resurrection of Jesus. Many would add a fourth: their own experience of God in prayer.

## What Christians believe

God is not one more thing inside the universe, like a very powerful person somewhere in space. God is the reason anything exists at all: the source of being, goodness and truth. That is why Christians say God can't be found with a telescope, but can be known, through creation, through conscience, and above all through Jesus, who "has declared him" (John 1:18).

## The great teachers

**Anselm** (11th century) defined God as "that than which nothing greater can be thought", and argued that such a being must exist. Philosophers still debate whether the argument works.

**Thomas Aquinas** set out his famous *Five Ways*: arguments from change, cause, contingency, degrees of goodness and the order of nature, each leading to a first source of everything.

**Blaise Pascal**, a mathematician, warned that proofs alone rarely move anyone: "The heart has its reasons, which reason does not know."

## The modern debate
tag: Strongest objections included

Modern defenders of belief argue from the universe's beginning, its fine-tuning for life, the existence of objective moral truths, and the historical evidence for the resurrection.

The strongest objections are serious. The problem of evil asks how a good God permits so much suffering. The problem of divine hiddenness asks why God, if real, isn't more obvious. Naturalists argue that science explains the world without needing God, and David Hume and, more recently, Richard Dawkins have argued that the design argument fails.

Abide's view is that you should meet the best of both sides, and decide for yourself.

## The sources

Anselm, *Proslogion*, chapters 2–4; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.2 a.3; Pascal, *Pensées*; C.S. Lewis, *Mere Christianity*, Book One. Against: David Hume, *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*; Richard Dawkins, *The God Delusion*. Many public debates between believers and atheists are freely available online.

- **Go deeper:** Timothy Keller, *The Reason for God* (2008), written for sceptics.
- **Go deeper:** Edward Feser, *Five Proofs of the Existence of God* (2017).
- **For the scholar:** Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.2; David Hume, *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*.
