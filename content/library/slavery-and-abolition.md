## Why it matters for you

Slavery existed in almost every civilisation for thousands of years. That it was ever abolished is one of the great moral turns in human history. Its story shows both the worst and best of religion, and how a single conscience, joined to others, can change the world.

## The short answer

The story is not simple. Christians owned slaves and quoted the Bible to defend slavery. But the movement that finally abolished the slave trade in Britain was led largely by Christians: Quakers, evangelicals like William Wilberforce, and formerly enslaved Christians like Olaudah Equiano. Britain banned the trade in 1807 and slavery across most of its empire in 1833.

## What Christians believe

Every person bears God's image, so no person can be property. The Exodus, where God hears the cry of slaves in Egypt and sets them free, became the defining story for enslaved Christians themselves, as the spirituals sung on American plantations show.

## The great teachers

Around 379, **Gregory of Nyssa** condemned slavery as an institution. In the 16th century, the Spanish friar **Bartolomé de las Casas** defended the indigenous peoples of the Americas against enslavement.

In 1787 a committee of Quakers and Anglicans, including **Thomas Clarkson** and **Granville Sharp**, founded the Society for Effecting the Abolition of the Slave Trade. **John Newton**, a former slave-ship captain who wrote "Amazing Grace", published his testimony against the trade in 1788.

## The modern debate
tag: Strongest objections included

The most searching Christian critic was **Frederick Douglass**, who escaped slavery and wrote in 1845 that he loved "the pure, peaceable, and impartial Christianity of Christ" and hated the "slaveholding religion" of America. His distinction still matters.

Historians debate how much faith drove abolition. In *Capitalism and Slavery* (1944), **Eric Williams** argued that economics mattered more than morality. Others reply that abolition cost Britain dearly. Critics also note that the Bible itself never commands the end of slavery. Christians answer that its deepest logic does.
