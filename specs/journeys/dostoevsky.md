**Follows:** a paper in nineteenth-century literature and philosophy of religion.

**Frame:** the novelist as philosopher: Dostoevsky tests the idea 'if there is no God, everything is permitted' in characters who live it: the underground man (freedom without reason), Raskolnikov (murder for an idea), Myshkin (goodness that cannot save), Ivan (rebellion and the Inquisitor), and the answer he gives not in argument but in Zosima and Alyosha.

**Real questions:** Who was Dostoevsky? Did Dostoevsky say 'if God does not exist, everything is permitted'? What is the Grand Inquisitor? What is Crime and Punishment about? Why did Raskolnikov kill? Was Dostoevsky a Christian? What is Notes from Underground? What did Dostoevsky think of the West? Why is he still read? What is the answer to Ivan?

**Builds on:** *The death of God?*, *Bearing suffering* (Ivan's 'Rebellion', read). **Read elsewhere:** *Brothers Karamazov* V.4 (*Bearing suffering*); John 11 (*Death and hope*).

## Sessions

1. **The mock execution.** *Finding:* In 1849 Dostoevsky was led out to be shot, reprieved at the last moment, and sent to Siberia with a New Testament he read for four years: everything he wrote comes from that. *Read:* *The Idiot* I.5 (Myshkin describes an execution). *Compare:* his letter to Fonvizina (1854: 'if someone proved to me that Christ is outside the truth', our own words). *Lecture:* life; the Petrashevsky circle; prison; Orthodoxy. *Learn:* new `john-12-24`. *Look:* a winter scene or prisoner. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
2. **Two times two makes four.** *Finding:* The underground man refuses to be a piano key of reason and science: freedom, even freedom to be miserable, is what humans want most. *Read:* *Notes from Underground* I.7–9. *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 7:29. *Lecture:* against utilitarian utopias (the Crystal Palace); existentialism's ancestor. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a lonely room. *Music:* none.
3. **Everything is permitted.** *Finding:* Raskolnikov kills an old pawnbroker to prove that extraordinary men may step over moral law, and finds he cannot live with it. *Read:* *Crime and Punishment* III.5. *Compare:* Genesis 4:9–10 (point back). *Lecture:* the Napoleonic idea; the phrase 'everything is permitted' and its sources in the *Karamazov*. *Learn:* `genesis-4-9`. *Look:* a dim city street. *Music:* none.
4. **Lazarus, come forth.** *Finding:* The prostitute Sonia reads him the raising of Lazarus, and in Siberia, at the end, he begins to be raised. *Read:* *Crime and Punishment* IV.4 and Epilogue II. *Compare:* John 11:38–44 (point back). *Lecture:* suffering and redemption; Sonia as the novel's saint. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Raising of Lazarus (Gozzoli used: another). *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: the Grand Inquisitor.** *Finding:* Ivan's poem has Christ return to Seville and be condemned by a cardinal who says people do not want freedom but bread, miracle and authority; Christ answers with a kiss. *Read:* *Brothers Karamazov* V.5. *Compare:* Matthew 4:1–11 (the three temptations). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: freedom as too heavy a gift; Ivan's case recalled (point back); readings (Berdyaev, D. H. Lawrence's agreement with the Inquisitor, our own words). *Debate:* the Inquisitor; Christ's silence; Lawrence. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a cardinal or Christ before an accuser. *Music:* none.
6. **Each of us is responsible for all.** *Finding:* Dostoevsky's answer is the elder Zosima: love the whole creation, and know that each is responsible for all. *Read:* *Brothers Karamazov* VI.2–3. *Compare:* 1 John 4:20–21. *Lecture:* Optina Pustyn and the elders; Orthodox spirituality; active love. *Learn:* new `zosima-love-all-creation` (Garnett). *Look:* an elder or monk. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
7. **Unless a grain of wheat: the verdict.** *Finding:* Dostoevsky did not refute Ivan; he showed what follows from each answer, and set Alyosha and the boys at the stone as his reply. *Read:* *Brothers Karamazov* Epilogue 3 (the speech at the stone). *Compare:* John 12:24 (the epigraph). *Lecture:* is everything permitted? the philosophical debate (point ahead to *Right and wrong* as the hall); Dostoevsky and the twentieth century (Camus, Solzhenitsyn). *Learn:* `john-12-24`. *Look:* children, or a wheat field. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 5, the Grand Inquisitor (with Ivan's rebellion recalled).
