**Follows:** a paper on biblical eschatology and apocalyptic.

**Frame:** the Bible ends with a city, not a cloud: the journey reads its hope in four stages (the prophets' new creation, Jesus's warnings, Paul's resurrection hope, Revelation's visions), faces the charge that it all failed, and ends with the hall's 'make the case'.

**Real questions:** What does the Bible say about the end of the world? What is the Second Coming? Is Revelation about now? What is the rapture? What is the millennium? Did Jesus think the world would end soon? What is heaven like? What happens to creation? Why are Christians so obsessed with the end times?

**Builds on:** the whole hall; *Death and hope* (personal death; 1 Corinthians 15:35–58). **Read elsewhere:** Revelation 21–22 (*The whole story*, *Light, stone and glass*), Revelation 4–5 (*Light, stone and glass*, *The sound of faith*), 1 Corinthians 15 (*Death and hope*, *The last week*).

## Sessions

1. **New heavens and a new earth.** *Finding:* The prophets hoped not to escape the world but for its renewal: dry bones raised, the wolf with the lamb. *Read:* Isaiah 65:17–25 and Ezekiel 37:1–14. *Compare:* Hesiod's golden age (point back to *In the beginning*: Works and Days). *Lecture:* hope in Israel; resurrection in Daniel 12 and 2 Maccabees 7. *Learn:* new `isaiah-65-17`. *Look:* the Peaceable Kingdom (Hicks is used: another). *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
2. **Apocalypse.** *Finding:* 'Apocalypse' means unveiling: a kind of writing for persecuted people, full of symbols, about who really rules history. *Read:* Daniel 7 point back; read Revelation 1:9–20. *Compare:* 1 Enoch 1 (tr. R. H. Charles, 1917). *Lecture:* apocalyptic literature; symbols and numbers; John on Patmos (Titian used). *Learn:* new `revelation-1-8`. *Look:* St John on Patmos (another painting). *Music:* `quartet-end-of-time`.
3. **Stay awake.** *Finding:* Jesus warned of the Temple's fall and of a coming of the Son of Man; he told his followers to watch, not to calculate. *Read:* Mark 13:1–37. *Compare:* Josephus on the siege (point back to *One root, two branches*). *Lecture:* reading Mark 13: AD 70, the end, or both. *Learn:* new `mark-13-33`. *Look:* the wise and foolish virgins. *Music:* `wachet-auf`.
4. **The hope of the dead.** *Finding:* Paul's hope is resurrection and the renewal of creation, with Christ, not a soul floating free. *Read:* 1 Thessalonians 4:13–5:11, 2 Corinthians 5:1–10 and Romans 8:18–25. *Compare:* Plato's immortality of the soul (point back to *Death and hope*). *Lecture:* the 'rapture' (Darby, 1830s) and why most traditions reject it; heaven and new creation (Wright, our own words). *Learn:* `romans-nothing-can-separate-us`. *Look:* the Last Judgement or the Resurrection of the dead. *Music:* `brahms-requiem`.
5. **The case against: failed prophecy?** *Finding:* Schweitzer argued Jesus expected the end within a generation and was wrong; Christians answer from AD 70, from the 'already and not yet', or admit a genuine puzzle. *Read:* Schweitzer, *The Quest of the Historical Jesus*, ch. XIX–XX (the wheel of the world). *Compare:* Mark 13:30 and 2 Peter 3:3–10. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength in Schweitzer's terms and Bertrand Russell's (*Why I Am Not a Christian*, 1927 lecture; our own words); replies (C. S. Lewis, 'The World's Last Night', our own words; Wright). *Learn:* `isaiah-40-8`. *Look:* a dramatic Last Judgement. *Music:* `mozart-requiem`.
6. **A great multitude.** *Finding:* Revelation's goal is worship by every nation and the healing of the nations; the millennium has been read in three main ways. *Read:* Revelation 7:9–17. *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* XX.7–9 (the millennium is now, the Church's age). *Lecture:* pre-, post- and amillennialism side by side; the end in Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant teaching; end-times movements and their harm. *Learn:* `revelation-21-4`. *Look:* All Saints, or the Adoration of the Lamb. *Music:* `for-all-the-saints`.
7. **The verdict, and making the case.** *Finding:* The Bible's story ends in a renewed creation where God lives with people; it is either the truest story or the greatest wish, and this hall has given you what you need to decide. *Read:* Romans 8:18–25 recalled; Revelation 21–22 point back. *Compare:* Julian of Norwich (point back to *Bearing suffering*) or Hopkins's 'The Wreck of the Deutschland' stanza 35 (public domain). *Lecture:* the hall in one table. **Make the case** (ends Hall II): is this story true, and is it mine? *Learn:* `julian-all-shall-be-well`. *Look:* the Heavenly Jerusalem. *Music:* `messiah`.

**Objection:** session 5, failed prophecy, in Schweitzer's and Russell's terms.

**Traditions:** the millennium, purgatory, judgement and universal hope side by side (Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed, evangelical).

**Voices:** N. T. Wright, *Surprised by Hope*; Richard Bauckham on Revelation; Jürgen Moltmann, *Theology of Hope*; C. S. Lewis, 'The World's Last Night' and *The Great Divorce*; Bertrand Russell.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Did Jesus expect the end soon?'; 'How should Revelation be read?'; 'Heaven or new creation?'. Make the case for Hall II.
