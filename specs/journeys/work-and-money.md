**Follows:** a paper in Christian ethics (work, vocation and economics).

**Frame:** from the garden to the marketplace: work as given before the fall (Genesis 2), cursed after it (Genesis 3), prayed (Benedict), called (Luther), and the question of money in three of Wesley's words: gain all you can, save all you can, give all you can.

**Real questions:** Is work a curse? What is a calling or vocation? What does the Bible say about money? Is it wrong to be rich? How much should I give? Is the love of money the root of all evil? What is the Protestant work ethic? Is ambition wrong? How do I find meaningful work? Should Christians rest?

**Builds on:** *Time made holy* (Sabbath), *The poor* (Ruskin IV, Smith, Deuteronomy 15, Luke 14), *The Reformation* (vocation). **Read elsewhere:** those passages.

## Sessions

1. **To work it and keep it.** *Finding:* In Genesis work comes before the fall: tending the garden is part of being human; the curse makes it toil, not its existence. *Read:* Proverbs 6:6–11 (the ant) and Psalm 127:1–2, with Genesis 2:15 and 3:17–19 pointed back to *Dust that wonders* and *In the beginning*. *Compare:* Hesiod's 'work is no disgrace' (point back to *In the beginning*). *Lecture:* work in the ancient world (slaves and leisure); the dignity of labour. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* peasants at work (Breton used: another). *Music:* none.
2. **Pray and work.** *Finding:* Benedict made manual work part of the monk's day: idleness is the enemy of the soul. *Read:* *Rule* 48 and 57. *Compare:* 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12. *Lecture:* *ora et labora*; Cistercian farming; craft. *Learn:* `herbert-the-elixir`. *Look:* a monk at work. *Music:* none.
3. **Every calling.** *Finding:* Luther said the milkmaid and the cobbler serve God as truly as the monk: every honest work is a vocation. *Read:* Luther (our own words / PD) with Colossians 3:23–24. *Compare:* Herbert's 'The Elixir' (memory, point back). *Lecture:* vocation; Calvin; William Perkins; Sayers's 'Why Work?' (our own words). *Learn:* new `colossians-3-23`. *Look:* a craftsman (Velázquez used: another). *Music:* none.
4. **Neither poverty nor riches.** *Finding:* The Bible is not against money but warns again and again that wealth deceives: 'the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil'. *Read:* Proverbs 30:7–9 and 1 Timothy 6:6–10, 17–19. *Compare:* Luke 12:13–21. *Lecture:* wealth in the Old and New Testaments; the rich young man; usury debates. *Learn:* new `1-timothy-6-6`. *Look:* a money-changer or banker. *Music:* none.
5. **Gain, save, give.** *Finding:* Wesley told Methodists to gain all they can, save all they can and give all they can, and lived on the same income while his earnings rose. *Read:* Wesley, 'The Use of Money'. *Compare:* 2 Corinthians 9:6–8. *Lecture:* tithing; Wesley's own giving; effective altruism (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: the Protestant ethic and the cage.** *Finding:* Weber argued that Protestant duty-to-work built capitalism and then left an 'iron cage' without the spirit; critics say Christian work-talk sanctifies exploitation and burnout. *Read:* Ruskin, *Unto This Last* Essay I. *Compare:* Weber (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Weber; the prosperity gospel; burnout and hustle culture; replies (Sabbath, point back; Pope Leo XIII, *Rerum Novarum* 1891 §§19–20 on the just wage, PD translation). *Debate:* Weber; Ruskin; a free-market Christian (Michael Novak, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Enough: the verdict.** *Finding:* Work is holy when it serves God and neighbour, money is good when it serves rather than rules, and rest is the sign that we are not slaves. *Read:* Matthew 6:19–21 and 24 (point back if read in *Sermon*). *Compare:* *Rerum Novarum* recalled. *Lecture:* a simple rule of life for work and money (giving, rest, contentment). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a household at rest. *Music:* none.
