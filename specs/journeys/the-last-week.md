**Follows:** a first-year paper on the Passion narratives and the historical question of the resurrection.

**Frame:** seven days, read like a historian walking through Jerusalem: the entry and the Temple, the supper, the arrest and trials, the cross, the tomb, the witnesses, and the verdict on Easter.

**Real questions:** What is Holy Week? Why did they kill Jesus? Who was responsible? What was crucifixion really like? Was the tomb empty? Did the disciples see him? Could it have been hallucinations, a stolen body, a swoon? Why do the Gospels' accounts differ? What is Easter about? What do Jews and Muslims say about the cross?

**Builds on:** *Who was Jesus?* (his claims), *Exodus* (Passover). **Read elsewhere:** Gethsemane (*How to pray*), Tacitus (*What is the Bible?*), Matthew 26 (*The sound of faith*), Isaiah 53 (*Kings and prophets*). This journey absorbs the cross and resurrection from the current *Who was Jesus?* and the planned 'Did Jesus rise?'.

## Sessions

1. **Into the city.** *Answers:* What is Holy Week? What happened on Palm Sunday and in the Temple? *Finding:* Jesus entered Jerusalem at Passover as a king on a donkey and attacked the Temple trade: an act no authority could ignore.
   *Read:* Mark 11:1–19. *Compare:* Zechariah 9:9 and Jeremiah 7:9–11.
   *Lecture:* Jerusalem at Passover (Josephus on the crowds); the Temple, its priests and Roman soldiers; Holy Week day by day. *Debate:* a prophetic sign or a political act?
   *Learn:* new `zechariah-9-9` (WEB). *Look:* the Entry into Jerusalem, or the cleansing of the Temple. *Music:* `all-glory-laud`.
2. **The last supper.** *Answers:* Was it a Passover meal? What did he mean by 'this is my body'? Why did he wash their feet? *Finding:* Jesus gave his death its meaning in advance, at a Passover meal: a new covenant in his blood, and a servant's towel.
   *Read:* Mark 14:10–31 and John 13:1–17 (the anointing, 14:3–9, is read in *Light, stone and glass*: point back). *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 (the earliest written account).
   *Lecture:* the dating problem (Mark and John); covenant and Passover (pointing back to *Exodus*); Judas. *Debate:* Passover meal or not?
   *Learn:* `exodus-12-14` recalled; new `1-corinthians-11-24`. *Look:* the Last Supper, or the foot-washing. *Music:* `ave-verum`.
3. **On trial.** *Answers:* Why did they kill him? Who was responsible? *Finding:* The high priests handed him to Pilate as a threat to order, and Rome crucified him as 'King of the Jews'; the Gospels' 'the Jews' has a terrible later history.
   *Read:* John 18:28–19:16. *Compare:* Philo, *Embassy to Gaius* 299–305 on Pilate's character (tr. Yonge, 1855).
   *Lecture:* Jewish and Roman law; Pilate in Philo and Josephus; Matthew 27:25 and its abuse (point ahead to *One root, two branches*); the Catholic Church's *Nostra Aetate* (1965) on collective guilt. *Debate:* who was responsible?
   *Learn:* `psalm-22-1`. *Look:* Christ before Pilate, or Ecce Homo. *Music:* `st-matthew-passion`.
4. **The cross.** *Answers:* What was crucifixion like? Why did he die so quickly? What were his last words? *Finding:* Crucifixion was Rome's most shameful death, meant for slaves and rebels; that the first Christians proclaimed a crucified Messiah is itself evidence they did not invent it.
   *Read:* Mark 15:1–47 (moved from *Who was Jesus?*) and Luke 23:39–49. *Compare:* Cicero, *Against Verres* II.5.162–170 (a Roman citizen crucified; 'the cruellest and most disgusting penalty').
   *Lecture:* the archaeology (the heel bone of Yehohanan, 1968); the seven last words across the Gospels; the criterion of embarrassment. *Debate:* why did it matter that it was a cross?
   *Learn:* `psalm-31-5`. *Look:* the Crucifixion (the Grünewald is already used; choose another). *Music:* `were-you-there`.
5. **The empty tomb.** *Answers:* Was the tomb empty? Why do the accounts differ? What about Mark's ending? *Finding:* The earliest accounts agree that women found the tomb empty, an unlikely detail to invent; they differ in the details as independent witnesses do.
   *Read:* Mark 16:1–8 and Luke 24:1–12. *Compare:* Matthew 28:11–15 (the earliest counter-explanation: the body was stolen).
   *Lecture:* burial customs and Joseph of Arimathea; Mark's short ending; women as witnesses; the Gospels set side by side. *Debate:* empty tomb: history or legend?
   *Learn:* new `luke-24-6` (WEB). *Look:* the holy women at the tomb. *Music:* `christ-the-lord-is-risen`.
6. **The case against: did he rise?** *Answers:* Could it have been hallucinations, a stolen body, a swoon, legend? *Finding:* Each natural explanation explains part of the evidence; the question is which explains all of it best, and the answer depends on whether you allow that God could act.
   *Read:* 1 Corinthians 15:12–20 (moved from *Who was Jesus?*; the list of witnesses, 15:1–11, is read in *What is the Bible?*: point back). *Compare:* Hume and Strauss in our own words, with D. F. Strauss, *The Life of Jesus Critically Examined* (tr. George Eliot, 1846), §§ on the resurrection.
   *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: hallucination (Lüdemann), legend (Ehrman), swoon and theft, and Hume's principle (point ahead to Hall VII, not a session); N. T. Wright's case and its critics (our own words). *Debate:* Strauss/Lüdemann; Wright; Dale Allison.
   *Learn:* `cor-15-3`. *Look:* doubting Thomas, or Christ appearing. *Music:* `mozart-requiem`.
7. **My Lord and my God: the verdict.** *Answers:* What is Easter about? What do Jews and Muslims say? So did he rise? *Finding:* Something happened that turned frightened followers into witnesses ready to die; Christians say it was the resurrection, and this journey has set out why and what else it might be.
   *Read:* John 20:1–29 (moved from *Who was Jesus?*; 20:30–31 is in *What is the Bible?*). *Compare:* the Qur'an, Sura 4:157 (Rodwell): the Muslim denial of the crucifixion.
   *Lecture:* resurrection in Jewish hope (Daniel 12, 2 Maccabees 7); Easter in the traditions; Jewish and Muslim views side by side. *Debate:* the verdict.
   *Learn:* `donne-death-be-not-proud`. *Look:* the Resurrection (the Bergognone is used; choose another). *Music:* `messiah`.

**Objection:** session 6, the natural explanations at full strength in Strauss's and modern sceptics' terms.

**Traditions:** the Eucharist (point ahead to *What happens at church* only as the hall); Easter dating East and West; who killed Jesus (Catholic and Protestant statements, Jewish responses); the Muslim view of the cross.

**Voices:** N. T. Wright, *The Resurrection of the Son of God*; Gerd Lüdemann; Bart Ehrman; Dale Allison; Raymond Brown, *The Death of the Messiah*; Martin Hengel, *Crucifixion*.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Is the empty tomb historical?'; 'Assess the hallucination hypothesis'; 'Why did the first Christians proclaim a crucified Messiah?'.
