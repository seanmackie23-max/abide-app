**Follows:** a paper in theology and philosophy (the problem of evil as moral evil and sin).

**Frame:** Solzhenitsyn's line through every human heart as the frame: evil is not only out there (monsters) but in here; the journey asks where it came from (the fall), what it is (privation), how it divides us (Romans 7, Jekyll), how ordinary it is (Arendt), whether we are born with it (original sin), and whether it can be overcome.

**Real questions:** Where does evil come from? Are people born good or bad? What is original sin? What is sin? Why do I do what I don't want to do? Is evil real or just the absence of good? What is the banality of evil? What is Jung's shadow? Are some people evil? Can evil be overcome?

**Builds on:** *In the beginning* (Genesis 3), *Augustine* (Pelagius), *The twentieth century* (Solzhenitsyn's line). **Read elsewhere:** Genesis 3 (*In the beginning*, *The whole story*); Romans 7 noted in *Augustine* and *Plato* as compare: settle.

## Sessions

1. **East of Eden.** *Finding:* Genesis says evil entered a good world through a choice: not a rival god, not matter, but trust broken. *Read:* point back to Genesis 3; read Genesis 4:6–7 ('sin is crouching at the door'). *Compare:* the Enuma Elish or Zoroastrian dualism (point back). *Lecture:* what the fall says and does not say. *Learn:* `genesis-4-9`. *Look:* the Fall (Goltzius used: another). *Music:* none.
2. **What evil is.** *Finding:* Augustine concluded that evil is not a thing but a lack: good gone wrong, like rot in fruit or blindness in an eye. *Read:* Augustine, *Enchiridion* 10–14 and *City of God* XII.6–7. *Compare:* Plotinus (our own words). *Lecture:* privation theory; Manichaeism; the objection that it trivialises horror. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a decaying still life. *Music:* none.
3. **The good I want.** *Finding:* Paul described a self divided against itself: 'the good which I desire, I don't do'; the experience is universal. *Read:* Romans 7:14–25. *Compare:* Ovid, *Metamorphoses* VII.20–21 ('I see the better and approve it; I follow the worse') (More 1922). *Lecture:* the debate on Romans 7 (Augustine, Luther, Stendahl); akrasia in Aristotle (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a figure in inner struggle. *Music:* none.
4. **Jekyll and Hyde.** *Finding:* Stevenson's story imagines a man who splits his good and evil selves and finds the evil one growing stronger: the Victorian parable of the divided heart. *Read:* *Jekyll and Hyde* ch. 10 (selected). *Compare:* Jung's shadow (our own words). *Lecture:* Stevenson's Calvinist Edinburgh; Jung; evil projected onto others. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a nocturnal city. *Music:* none.
5. **Born in sin?** *Finding:* Augustine taught that we inherit a fallen nature from Adam; the traditions agree we are born into a broken world and disagree on guilt. *Read:* Romans 5:12–21. *Compare:* Kant, *Religion* Book I (the 'radical evil' in human nature). *Lecture:* original sin side by side (Orthodox ancestral sin, Catholic, Reformed total depravity, liberal rejection); G. K. Chesterton's 'the only Christian doctrine that can be proved' (*Orthodoxy* ch. II, PD; point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Adam and Eve. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: monsters, or ordinary people?** *Finding:* Arendt watched Eichmann's trial and found not a monster but a thoughtless bureaucrat; modern psychology (Milgram, Zimbardo) suggests situations, not hearts, make evil, which challenges both 'monsters' and 'sin'. *Read:* Arendt (our own words). *Compare:* Solzhenitsyn's line (point back). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: situationism, determinism, evolutionary psychology (Steven Pinker, our own words, on declining violence); replies. *Debate:* Arendt; Milgram; Solzhenitsyn. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Overcome evil with good: the verdict.** *Finding:* Evil comes from the misuse of freedom and lives in every heart; the Christian answer is not that we can fix it alone but that it has been met and can be overcome, one heart at a time. *Read:* Romans 12:21 (point back if new elsewhere) with Romans 8:1–4. *Compare:* the end of *Les Misérables*' bishop scene (point ahead to *Guilt and forgiveness* in Hall IX as the hall: use another, e.g. Dostoevsky's Zosima point back). *Lecture:* evil and grace; practical: examination of conscience. *Learn:* `psalm-51`. *Look:* the Archangel Michael. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.

**Objection:** session 6.
