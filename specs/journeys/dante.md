**Follows:** a first-year paper on Dante's *Divine Comedy*.

**Frame:** the poem's own road: lost in a dark wood at thirty-five, guided by Virgil down through hell, up the mountain of purgatory, and by Beatrice through the heavens to 'the love that moves the sun and the other stars'. Seven sessions, seven stations on the road.

**Real questions:** What is the Divine Comedy? Who was Dante? Who was Beatrice? What are the circles of hell? Why is Virgil his guide? Who is in Dante's hell? What is purgatory? Is Dante's hell meant literally? What is the last line of the Divine Comedy? Why is Dante important?

**Builds on:** *Rome* (Virgil), *Homer* (Ulysses), *Augustine*. **Read elsewhere:** the memory `dante-midway-upon-the-journey`; *Purgatorio*'s terraces (*The seven deadly sins*, Hall IX).

## Sessions

1. **The dark wood.** *Finding:* Dante begins lost: the poem is the story of one man's rescue, and so of everyone's. *Read:* *Inferno* I. *Compare:* Psalm 23:4 (point back). *Lecture:* Dante's life (Florence, exile 1302); the poem's shape (three, thirty-three, terza rima); Virgil as guide. *Learn:* `dante-midway-upon-the-journey`. *Look:* a dark forest, or Dante (NGA: confirm). *Music:* none.
2. **Abandon all hope.** *Finding:* Hell's gate says it was made by justice and love: Dante's hell is the state of those who chose themselves forever. *Read:* *Inferno* III.1–69 (the gate and the neutrals). *Compare:* Matthew 25:41–46 (point back) and Revelation 3:15–16. *Lecture:* the geography of hell (a table: incontinence, violence, fraud); *contrapasso*. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scene of the underworld. *Music:* none.
3. **Francesca.** *Finding:* The lovers Paolo and Francesca are blown forever on the wind of the passion they gave way to; Dante faints from pity, and the reader must ask whether pity is right. *Read:* *Inferno* V.73–142. *Compare:* Song of Songs 8:6–7 (point back to *Psalms and wisdom*). *Lecture:* courtly love; Francesca's self-excuse; Dante's own susceptibility. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Paolo and Francesca. *Music:* none.
4. **Ulysses' last voyage.** *Finding:* Dante's Ulysses sails beyond the limits for 'virtue and knowledge' and drowns: the noble pagan who trusted only himself. *Read:* *Inferno* XXVI.85–142. *Compare:* Tennyson, 'Ulysses' closing lines (memory, point to Practice). *Lecture:* Ulysses in Homer and Dante (point back); the fraudulent counsellors; curiosity and pride. *Learn:* `tennyson-ulysses`. *Look:* a ship at sea. *Music:* none.
5. **To see the stars.** *Finding:* At the bottom of hell Satan is frozen in ice, chewing traitors; Dante climbs down his body, turns, and comes out to see the stars. *Read:* *Inferno* XXXIV.1–69 and 127–139. *Compare:* Isaiah 14:12–15. *Lecture:* why ice, not fire; treachery as the worst sin; each canticle ends with 'stars'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the night sky. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: a cruel poem?** *Finding:* Readers have called Dante's hell vindictive (he put his enemies there, and even popes) and the whole idea of eternal punishment monstrous; Dante's answer is that hell is what the damned insist on. *Read:* *Inferno* XXXIII.1–90 (Ugolino) (Longfellow). *Compare:* Origen and Gregory of Nyssa on universal restoration (our own words; or NPNF short passage). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Shelley, modern critics; universalism: David Bentley Hart, our own words); replies (C. S. Lewis, *The Great Divorce*, our own words; Dorothy Sayers). *Debate:* a universalist; Dante; Lewis. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Last Judgement. *Music:* `mozart-requiem`.
7. **The love that moves the sun: the verdict.** *Finding:* Through Beatrice's rebuke and the heavens Dante learns that 'in his will is our peace', and ends seeing God as the love that moves the sun and the other stars. *Read:* *Purgatorio* XXX.22–57; *Paradiso* III.70–90 and XXXIII.115–145. *Compare:* 1 John 4:16. *Lecture:* purgatory in Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant teaching side by side; Beatrice; the last canto. *Learn:* new `paradiso-33-145` (Longfellow). *Look:* a vision of heaven, or the Coronation of the Virgin. *Music:* `palestrina-marcelli`.

**Objection:** session 6, the cruelty of hell.

**Traditions:** purgatory and hell (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant; universalism) side by side.
