**Follows:** a first-year paper on Hellenistic philosophy.

**Frame:** two schools, one goal (peace of mind, *ataraxia*): the Epicureans find it by removing fear of the gods and death and living simply; the Stoics by wanting only what is in our power and accepting the rest as the will of a rational universe. The Christians met both in Athens (Acts 17:18) and borrowed from both.

**Real questions:** What is Stoicism? Is Stoicism a religion? What did Epicurus actually teach? Is Epicureanism just pleasure-seeking? What is the dichotomy of control? Why is Stoicism popular today? Who was Seneca? Who was Epictetus? Can a Christian be a Stoic? Is it wrong to fear death?

**Builds on:** the Athens hall; *A good life* (Marcus Book I), *Death and hope* (Lucretius III). **Read elsewhere:** Lucretius III (*Death and hope*), Marcus Book I (*A good life*); memory pieces `within-our-power`, `begin-the-morning`, `retire-into-thyself`, `ten-thousand-years`, `i-have-restored-it` already exist.

## Sessions

1. **The garden.** *Finding:* Epicurus taught that pleasure, understood as freedom from pain and fear, is the goal, and that it is reached by friendship, simplicity and understanding nature. *Read:* *Letter to Menoeceus* (Hicks). *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 9:7–10 (point back). *Lecture:* the Garden; atoms and the swerve; the four-part cure (*tetrapharmakos*). *Learn:* new `epicurus-death-is-nothing` (Hicks). *Look:* friends at a simple meal. *Music:* none.
2. **The case against religion.** *Finding:* Lucretius argued that religion breeds terror and crime, and that knowing nature frees us: the West's first great argument against faith. *Read:* Lucretius I.62–101 (Leonard). *Compare:* Psalm 14:1 ('the fool has said in his heart'). *Lecture:* the poem's rediscovery in 1417 (Poggio; Greenblatt, our own words, with its critics); Epicureanism and the modern world. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the sacrifice of Iphigenia. *Music:* none.
3. **The porch.** *Finding:* The Stoics taught that the world is ordered by divine reason (*logos*), and that virtue, living by it, is the only good. *Read:* Epictetus, *Discourses* I.1 (Long). *Compare:* John 1:1–5 (point back) on *logos*. *Lecture:* Zeno, Chrysippus; Stoic physics and ethics; indifferents. *Learn:* `within-our-power`. *Look:* a philosopher in a portico. *Music:* none.
4. **What is up to us.** *Finding:* Epictetus, born a slave, taught that some things are in our power (our judgements and choices) and the rest are not: peace comes from wanting only the first. *Read:* Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1–5. *Compare:* the Serenity Prayer (Niebuhr, 1930s; our own words, not quoted) and Matthew 6:25–34 (point back). *Lecture:* the dichotomy of control; Stoicism and CBT (Aaron Beck, our own words); James Stockdale in Hanoi. *Learn:* `i-have-restored-it`. *Look:* a prisoner or a slave with dignity. *Music:* none.
5. **A Roman Stoic.** *Finding:* Seneca, rich adviser to Nero, wrote letters on time, death and treating slaves as fellow men, and was forced to kill himself by his pupil. *Read:* Seneca, *Moral Letters* 1 ('on saving time') and 47 ('on master and slave') (Gummere). *Compare:* Philemon (point back) or Ephesians 6:5–9. *Lecture:* Seneca's life and hypocrisy charge; the forged correspondence with Paul; Stoic cosmopolitanism. *Learn:* new `seneca-letter-1` (Gummere). *Look:* the death of Seneca. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: cold comfort?** *Finding:* The Stoic answer to grief, that we should not be disturbed by what we cannot control, can sound like the death of love; Augustine and modern critics said so. *Read:* Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* II.1, IV.3, VI.13 (Long). *Compare:* John 11:33–36 ('Jesus wept', point back to *Death and hope*) and Augustine, *City of God* XIV.9 (against Stoic apathy). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Stoic *apatheia* versus Christian compassion; Nietzsche on Stoicism (our own words); modern 'broicism'. *Debate:* Epictetus; Augustine; a modern Stoic (Massimo Pigliucci, our own words). *Learn:* `begin-the-morning`. *Look:* a weeping figure. *Music:* `brahms-requiem`.
7. **Content in all things: the verdict.** *Finding:* Paul, writing from prison, used the Stoics' word for contentment and gave it a new ground: 'I can do all things through Christ'. Both schools offered peace; Christianity offered peace with someone. *Read:* Philippians 4:4–13. *Compare:* Epicurus, *Principal Doctrines* 1–5 recalled, as a table of the three answers. *Lecture:* Stoicism in Christian thought (Ambrose, Calvin's Seneca commentary, Justus Lipsius); the Epicurean revival in the Enlightenment; the three answers side by side. **Make the case** (ends Hall III). *Learn:* `philippians-4-6`. *Look:* St Paul in prison. *Music:* `it-is-well`.

**Objection:** session 6, Stoic peace as cold comfort, set against the case for Stoicism at its best.
