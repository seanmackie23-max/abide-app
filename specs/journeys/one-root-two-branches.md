**Follows:** a first-year paper on Christian origins and the 'parting of the ways'.

**Frame:** Paul's own picture in Romans 11: one olive tree, with Gentile branches grafted in. The journey follows the tree from Jesus the Jew through the Gentile question and the Temple's fall to the parting, faces the poison that grew from it, and ends with Jews and Christians today. Sessions as set out in the hall plan.

**Real questions:** Was Jesus Jewish? Were the first Christians Jews? Why did Christians stop keeping the Jewish law? When did Christianity and Judaism separate? Why don't Jews believe Jesus was the Messiah? Is the New Testament anti-Semitic? Why do Christians keep the Old Testament? Who was Marcion? What do Jews and Christians think of each other today?

**Builds on:** the Old Testament hall; *First Christians*. **Read elsewhere:** Galatians 2 (*First Christians*); Acts 2–5 (Acts 2 in *The whole story*); Galatians 4 (*Abraham*).

## Sessions

1. **Jesus the Jew.** *Finding:* Jesus was a Jew who kept the festivals, prayed the Shema and taught in synagogues; his quarrels were family quarrels. *Read:* Luke 2:21–52 (circumcised on the eighth day, presented in the Temple, at Passover aged twelve), with Luke 4:16–21 pointed back to *Who was Jesus?*. *Compare:* the Shema and the Amidah's first blessings (Singer's prayer book, 1890). *Lecture:* Second Temple Judaism; Pharisees fairly; Vermes and Levine (our own words). *Learn:* `deuteronomy-6-4`. *Look:* Christ in the synagogue, or the Presentation in the Temple. *Music:* `o-magnum-mysterium`.
2. **A Jewish sect.** *Finding:* For a generation the Jerusalem church under James was a Jewish movement worshipping in the Temple. *Read:* Acts 21:17–26. *Compare:* Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.9.1 (point back: James's death, read in *Who was Jesus?*). *Lecture:* 'the Way', Nazarenes; James the Just (Hegesippus in Eusebius II.23). *Learn:* new `acts-2-46`. *Look:* St James. *Music:* `locus-iste`.
3. **The Gentile question.** *Finding:* The first great Christian argument was whether Gentiles must become Jews; the Council of Jerusalem said no. *Read:* Acts 10:1–48 and Acts 15:1–29. *Compare:* Ignatius, *To the Magnesians* 8–10 (living 'no longer sabbatising'). *Lecture:* God-fearers; circumcision and food laws; the Noahide laws in Jewish thought. *Learn:* new `acts-10-34`. *Look:* Peter and Cornelius, or Peter's vision. *Music:* `all-creatures`.
4. **The Temple falls.** *Finding:* Rome's destruction of the Temple in AD 70 forced both communities to reinvent themselves, rabbinic Judaism at Yavneh and the Church around Christ, and they drew apart. *Read:* Josephus, *The Jewish War* VI.4–5. *Compare:* Mark 13:1–2. *Lecture:* Yohanan ben Zakkai and Yavneh; the *Birkat ha-Minim*; the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135). *Debate:* when did the ways part? (Dunn; Boyarin, our own words). *Learn:* `psalm-137-1`. *Look:* the destruction of Jerusalem. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
5. **The case against: did the New Testament teach contempt?** *Finding:* Some New Testament words, torn from their family quarrel, became the root of centuries of Christian contempt and violence towards Jews; the Church has had to repent of it. *Read:* Matthew 27:24–26 and John 8:31–47. *Compare:* Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 16 (the harsh early reading). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Jules Isaac's 'teaching of contempt' (our own words), the Crusades massacres of 1096 (point ahead to Hall V as the hall), Luther's *On the Jews and Their Lies* (our own words), the Holocaust; *Nostra Aetate* 4 (1965) and the Protestant statements; Jewish responses (*Dabru Emet*, 2000, our own words). *Debate:* is the problem the text or its readers? *Learn:* `micah-what-does-the-lord-require`. *Look:* none that depicts Jews in caricature; choose a neutral image (Ecce Homo is used in *The last week*): a lament. *Music:* `gorecki-3`.
6. **Why keep the Old Testament?** *Finding:* Marcion cut the Old Testament out; the Church refused, insisting the God of Israel and the Father of Jesus are one. *Read:* Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* I.27 and III.12. *Compare:* Justin, *Dialogue with Trypho* 1–8 (his conversion and the conversation with a Jew). *Lecture:* Marcion; the canon (point back to *What is the Bible?*); supersessionism and its critics. *Learn:* `jeremiah-31-33`. *Look:* prophets of the Old Testament. *Music:* `mendelssohn-elijah`.
7. **One olive tree: the verdict.** *Finding:* Paul refused to say God had rejected Israel: 'the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable'; Jews and Christians today read the same Scriptures as two branches of one root. *Read:* Romans 9:1–5 and 11:11–36. *Compare:* the Jewish response *Dabru Emet* (2000; our own words) beside *Nostra Aetate*. *Lecture:* Jews and Christians today; Messianic Jews; what each tradition says of the other. *Learn:* new `romans-11-29`. *Look:* an olive tree or grove. *Music:* `o-come-emmanuel`.

**Objection:** session 5, in the terms of Jules Isaac and of the Jewish critics of Christian anti-Judaism.

**Traditions:** Catholic (*Nostra Aetate*), Orthodox, Lutheran (the 1994 repudiation of Luther's anti-Jewish writings), Reformed, evangelical and Jewish views side by side.

**Voices:** Amy-Jill Levine; Daniel Boyarin; Jacob Neusner; Jules Isaac; Geza Vermes; Paula Fredriksen.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'When did Judaism and Christianity part?'; 'Is the New Testament anti-Jewish?'; 'Why did the Church keep the Old Testament?'.
