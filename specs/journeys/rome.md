**Follows:** a first-year classics paper on Roman literature and law.

**Frame:** three gifts from Rome, each with its shadow: the law (justice as giving each their due, natural law), the republic (a mixed constitution and duty to the state), and the empire (peace, roads and a common language, bought by conquest). Virgil sings all three, and the hall asks what the faith from Jerusalem made of them.

**Real questions:** What did the Romans give us? Who was Cicero? What is the Aeneid about? What is natural law? What is Roman law and why does it still matter? What was the Roman Republic? Why did Rome become an empire? What is *pietas*? Did Virgil predict Christ? Was the Roman Empire good?

**Builds on:** the Athens hall (Stoics, Aristotle's politics, Polybius's model in *Democracy*). **Read elsewhere:** Cicero, *Against Verres* (*The last week*).

## Sessions

1. **Arms and the man.** *Finding:* Virgil wrote Rome's founding epic for Augustus: a hero who carries his father and his gods out of burning Troy to found a new city. *Read:* Aeneid I.254–296 (Jupiter: 'empire without end'). *Compare:* Daniel 2:31–45 (empires that fall). *Lecture:* Virgil and Augustus; *pietas*; the Aeneid's plot; Rome's founding myths. *Learn:* new `aeneid-1-33` or similar short line (Mackail). *Look:* Aeneas fleeing Troy (NGA: flag). *Music:* none; `tallis-fantasia`.
2. **The cost of duty.** *Finding:* Aeneas abandons Dido for his mission: Virgil makes duty heroic and its price heartbreaking. *Read:* Aeneid IV.279–392. *Compare:* Augustine, *Confessions* I.13 (weeping for Dido; point back if used in *Homer*: use instead Luke 9:57–62). *Lecture:* *pietas* versus love; Dido and Carthage; Purcell's *Dido and Aeneas*. *Learn:* `smooth-the-descent`. *Look:* Dido and Aeneas. *Music:* none in `music.json` (Purcell, flag).
3. **Remember, Roman, to rule.** *Finding:* Virgil gives Rome its mission: to spare the conquered and war down the proud; the empire saw itself as bringing peace and law. *Read:* Aeneid VI.756–853. *Compare:* Tacitus, *Agricola* 30 (Calgacus: 'they make a desert and call it peace') (Church and Brodribb). *Lecture:* the Pax Romana; roads, Latin and Greek; citizenship (Paul's, point back); the empire's victims. *Debate:* peace-bringer or robber? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Roman landscape (Claude, Panini). *Music:* none.
4. **The republic.** *Finding:* Rome's republic mixed monarchy, aristocracy and democracy (consuls, senate, people), and the Founders of America studied it closely. *Read:* Polybius VI.11–18. *Compare:* Cicero, *The Dream of Scipio* (the reward of those who serve their country). *Lecture:* the republic's offices; Cincinnatus; the fall of the republic (Caesar, Cicero's murder in 43 BC). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Roman senate or Cincinnatus. *Music:* none.
5. **True law is right reason.** *Finding:* Cicero taught that true law is right reason in agreement with nature, the same in Rome and Athens, binding every nation: the root of natural law and human rights. *Read:* Cicero, *Republic* III.33. *Compare:* Romans 2:14–15 (the law written on the heart). *Lecture:* natural law from the Stoics through Cicero to Aquinas (point ahead to Hall IX only as the hall) and the Declaration of Independence. *Learn:* new `cicero-true-law` (PD translation). *Look:* a lawgiver or Justice. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: an empire of blood?** *Finding:* Rome was built on conquest, slavery and the arena; Augustine said its glory was the love of domination; the question is what of Rome deserves to be kept. *Read:* Augustine, *City of God* V.12–13 (the Romans' love of praise) (Dods). *Compare:* Tacitus, *Agricola* 30 recalled, and Seneca, *Letter* 7 (the arena) (Gummere). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: slavery (perhaps 10–20% of Italy), gladiators, crucifixion (point back), conquest; replies (what the Church kept and what it changed). *Debate:* Virgil; Tacitus's Calgacus; Augustine. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a ruin of the Colosseum. *Music:* none.
7. **Justice to each: the verdict.** *Finding:* Rome gave the West its law (justice as giving each their due, codified by Justinian) and its idea of a common order, and Christians baptised both. *Read:* Justinian, *Institutes* I.1–2. *Compare:* Virgil, *Eclogue* IV (the child and the golden age, read by Christians as a prophecy of Christ). *Lecture:* Roman law to canon law and the civil-law tradition; *ius gentium*; Virgil as 'pagan prophet' (Dante's guide, point ahead as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Forum, or Justice. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, Rome as an empire of blood, in Tacitus's and Augustine's terms.
