**Follows:** a paper in Christian spirituality.

**Frame:** from noise to silence in seven steps: why we flee it (Pascal), where God speaks in it (Elijah), why Jesus sought it, why the desert fathers lived in it, how silence can be kept in a kitchen (Brother Lawrence), whether it is escape (the objection), and how to begin.

**Real questions:** Why is silence important? What did the desert fathers do? How do I find silence? Does God speak in silence? Is silence just meditation? Why do monks keep silence? What is contemplative prayer? Is silence escapism? How do I practise silence with a busy life? What is the practice of the presence of God?

**Builds on:** *How to pray*, *Mystics*, *Pascal* (diversion). **Read elsewhere:** Pascal 139 (*Pascal*); Cloud (*Mystics*); Benedict (*Monks*).

## Sessions

1. **The noise.** *Finding:* Pascal said all our unhappiness comes from not being able to sit quietly in a room; we flee silence because it shows us ourselves. *Read:* point back to Pascal 139; read Ecclesiastes 5:1–2. *Compare:* a modern study of people choosing electric shocks over sitting alone (Wilson et al., *Science* 2014, cited). *Lecture:* noise and attention today. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an empty room (Hammershøi is not NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
2. **A still small voice.** *Finding:* God was not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire, but in 'a still small voice' (or 'a sound of sheer silence'). *Read:* 1 Kings 19:9–13. *Compare:* Psalm 46:10 (point back). *Lecture:* the Hebrew phrase and its translations; Elijah's despair. *Learn:* `psalm-46`. *Look:* Elijah in the desert (Savoldo used: another). *Music:* none.
3. **A deserted place.** *Finding:* Jesus rose before dawn to pray alone and told his friends to come away and rest: silence is part of a busy life of love, not its opposite. *Read:* Mark 6:30–32 and Luke 5:15–16 (Mark 1:35 is in *Who was Jesus?*: point back). *Compare:* Matthew 6:6 (point back). *Lecture:* retreat; Sabbath (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Christ praying alone. *Music:* none.
4. **Flee, be silent, pray.** *Finding:* Arsenius, a tutor to emperors, heard a voice say 'flee, be silent, pray': the desert fathers sought silence to face their own hearts and God. *Read:* the *Sayings* (Budge), selected. *Compare:* James 1:19 (point back) or Proverbs 17:28. *Lecture:* the desert; the cell ('your cell will teach you everything'); hesychasm and the Jesus Prayer. *Learn:* new `jesus-prayer`. *Look:* a hermit in the desert. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
5. **Among the pots and pans.** *Finding:* Brother Lawrence, a lay brother in a Paris kitchen, found God as present among the pots as at the altar: silence can be carried inside. *Read:* *The Practice of the Presence of God*, Conversations 1–4 and Letter 1. *Compare:* Herbert's 'The Elixir' (memory). *Lecture:* recollection; arrow prayers. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a kitchen scene (Chardin used: another). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: escape from the world?** *Finding:* Critics say retreat into silence is selfish escape from duty and injustice; contemplatives answer that the deepest action comes from silence. *Read:* Luke 10:38–42 (Martha and Mary). *Compare:* Merton's 'the contemplative is not isolated' (our own words) and Bonhoeffer's *Life Together* on silence and community (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (the Reformers on monasticism, point back; activists); Dag Hammarskjöld (our own words). *Debate:* Martha; Mary; Merton. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Christ with Martha and Mary. *Music:* none.
7. **Ten minutes a day: the verdict.** *Finding:* The saints went into the desert to find God and themselves; anyone can begin with ten minutes of silence a day. *Read:* Psalm 62:1–2. *Compare:* Barclay on Quaker silence (point back if used). *Lecture:* a practical guide (time, place, a word, distraction). *Learn:* new `psalm-62-1`. *Look:* a quiet landscape at dusk. *Music:* `song-for-athene`.

**Objection:** session 6.
