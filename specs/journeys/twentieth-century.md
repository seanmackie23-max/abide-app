**Follows:** a paper on twentieth-century history and political thought.

**Frame:** the century that tested the death of God: two world wars, two totalitarian systems that tried to replace the Christian foundations with race or class, and the people who resisted (Barmen, Bonhoeffer, Solzhenitsyn, Havel, King) mostly from those very foundations. Daniel 3 is the pattern: the golden statue and those who would not bow.

**Real questions:** Why was the twentieth century so violent? What caused the Holocaust? Was Hitler a Christian? What did the churches do under the Nazis? Who was Bonhoeffer? What was the Gulag? Who was Solzhenitsyn? What is totalitarianism? What did Havel mean by 'living in truth'? How did King use Christianity? Could it happen again?

**Builds on:** *The death of God?*, *Dostoevsky*; Practice's greats (Bonhoeffer, Solzhenitsyn, King, Mandela). **Read elsewhere:** Practice entries for these people: point to them.

## Sessions

1. **The golden statue.** *Finding:* Daniel 3, written for Jews under a persecuting king, gives the pattern of the century: a state that demands worship, and those who will not bow 'even if he does not save us'. *Read:* Daniel 3. *Compare:* Revelation 13:1–10. *Lecture:* 1914–1918 and the collapse of Christian Europe's confidence; the new idols (nation, race, class). *Learn:* new `daniel-3-18`. *Look:* the three youths in the furnace (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
2. **Race: the Nazi religion.** *Finding:* Nazism was a racial religion that used Christian language while despising Christianity; most churches failed to resist it, and some resisted heroically. *Read:* *Mit brennender Sorge* §§1–8. *Compare:* the Barmen Declaration, theses 1–2. *Lecture:* the 'German Christians'; the Confessing Church; the Holocaust; was Hitler a Christian? (Richard Steigmann-Gall and his critics, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none that glorifies; a lament. *Music:* `gorecki-3`.
3. **Bonhoeffer.** *Finding:* Bonhoeffer called the church to stand with the Jews, joined the conspiracy against Hitler, and was hanged in April 1945. *Read:* Bonhoeffer (our own words; point to Practice). *Compare:* Matthew 16:24–25. *Lecture:* 'cheap grace' (point back); the ethics of tyrannicide; Bonhoeffer's poem of trust (in our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Practice portrait or a prison scene. *Music:* none.
4. **Class: the Gulag.** *Finding:* Soviet communism promised heaven on earth without God and built the Gulag; Solzhenitsyn concluded that 'the line dividing good and evil cuts through the heart of every human being'. *Read:* Solzhenitsyn (our own words; the short quotation). *Compare:* Jeremiah 17:9–10. *Lecture:* the famine of 1932–33; the purges; persecution of the Orthodox Church; *One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich*. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a winter camp or barracks (none in NGA: a winter landscape). *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: was it Christianity's fault?** *Finding:* Critics argue that Christian antisemitism prepared the Holocaust, that the churches collaborated, and that modern totalitarianism grew from Christian ideas of history; the case must be heard in full. *Read:* Thoreau, *Civil Disobedience* (on the conscience of the state's servants), and the objection set out from historians (our own words). *Compare:* Luther's *On the Jews* (point back to *One root, two branches*). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Daniel Goldhagen, John Cornwell's *Hitler's Pope* (and replies), secular humanist readings; Voegelin's 'political religions' and Holland's thesis on the other side. *Debate:* Goldhagen; a Catholic historian; Holland. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **Living in truth.** *Finding:* Havel's greengrocer, who stops putting the party slogan in his window, shows how tyranny falls: when ordinary people refuse to live by lies. *Read:* Havel (our own words) and Solzhenitsyn's 'Live Not by Lies' (our own words). *Compare:* Ephesians 4:25. *Lecture:* 1989; John Paul II in Poland (1979); the Velvet Revolution. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a crowd in a square. *Music:* `missa-solemnis`.
7. **A dream deeply rooted: the verdict.** *Finding:* Where the West abandoned its foundations it built camps; where people recovered them (King, Havel, Solzhenitsyn, the Confessing Church) they found the strength to resist. *Read:* King, 'Letter from Birmingham Jail' (our own words, one short quotation) and Amos 5:24 (point back). *Compare:* Thoreau recalled. *Lecture:* the civil rights movement; could it happen again? *Learn:* `amos-5-24`. *Look:* none. *Music:* `precious-lord`.

**Objection:** session 5, Christian complicity.
