**Follows:** a paper in modern theology.

**Frame:** five voices answering the century: the joyful paradox (Chesterton), the thunderclap (Barth), the costly disciple (Bonhoeffer, point back), the outsider's love (Weil), and the plain apologist (Lewis), with Eliot's poems of conversion. Each session: the voice's answer in our own words, read beside a public-domain text it echoes.

**Real questions:** How did Christianity survive the twentieth century? Who was G. K. Chesterton? What is Orthodoxy about? Who was Karl Barth? Who was Simone Weil? Why is C. S. Lewis so popular? Was T. S. Eliot a Christian? What is modern theology? Who are the great Christian writers of the twentieth century?

**Builds on:** *The twentieth century*, *Dostoevsky*. **Read elsewhere:** Chesterton's 'Ethics of Elfland' (*Is there a God?*); *Orthodoxy* ch. VI is assigned to *Is God coming back?*.

## Sessions

1. **The maniac.** *Finding:* Chesterton argued that the madman is not one who has lost his reason but one who has lost everything except his reason: modern thought had become a closed circle. *Read:* *Orthodoxy* ch. II. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 1:25. *Lecture:* Chesterton's life (Fleet Street, Father Brown, conversion 1922); paradox. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a lively crowd or a fool. *Music:* none.
2. **The God in the cave.** *Finding:* Chesterton said the strangest fact in history is that the God of the universe was born in a cave: Christmas as the hinge of the world. *Read:* *The Everlasting Man* II.1. *Compare:* Luke 2:1–20 (point back). *Lecture:* Chesterton's answer to H. G. Wells's *Outline of History*; its effect on C. S. Lewis. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Nativity (the Bassano is used: another). *Music:* `in-the-bleak-midwinter`.
3. **The thunderclap.** *Finding:* Barth, a Swiss pastor horrified by his teachers' support for the Kaiser's war, wrote that God is wholly other and speaks a 'No' before his 'Yes'; he drafted the Barmen Declaration. *Read:* Barth (our own words) beside Romans 1:16–17 (point back). *Compare:* Barmen (point back to *The twentieth century*). *Lecture:* liberal theology; Barth's *Romans* (1919); *Church Dogmatics*; Barth and Mozart. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none; a mountain storm. *Music:* `ave-verum` (Barth's love of Mozart).
4. **Affliction.** *Finding:* Simone Weil, a Jewish-born philosopher who worked in factories and died starving herself in solidarity with occupied France, wrote that affliction is the place where God's love can be known. *Read:* Weil (our own words), beside Herbert's 'Love (III)' (memory; point to Worth remembering). *Compare:* Psalm 22:1 (point back). *Lecture:* Weil's life; attention; 'waiting for God'; her refusal of baptism. *Learn:* `love3`. *Look:* a factory or labourer. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: comforting stories?** *Finding:* Critics say these writers offered beautiful consolations rather than truth, and that theology retreated into paradox when the arguments failed. *Read:* Freud, *The Future of an Illusion* (our own words) or Russell, 'Why I Am Not a Christian' (1927, original English: qualifies) selected. *Compare:* Chesterton's reply to sceptics in *Orthodoxy* ch. IX. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: religion as wish-fulfilment (Freud), as escapism (Marx's 'opium', mentioned only to describe), Russell's arguments; replies (Lewis's 'Argument from Desire', our own words). *Debate:* Russell; Chesterton; Lewis. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **Mere Christianity.** *Finding:* Lewis, an atheist Oxford don converted in 1931, became the century's most-read Christian writer by explaining the faith plainly on wartime radio. *Read:* Lewis (our own words) beside Psalm 19:1 (point back). *Compare:* Chesterton's *The Everlasting Man* recalled (Lewis's turning point). *Lecture:* Lewis's life; the Inklings and Tolkien; Narnia; *Mere Christianity* outline (Book 1 to Is there a God?, Book 2 to Who was Jesus?, Books 3 and 4 to Hall IX/VIII). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an Oxford scene or a wardrobe-like door. *Music:* none.
7. **All things new: the verdict.** *Finding:* Christians answered the twentieth century not with one system but with witnesses: joy, judgement, costly discipleship, attention and plain reason, and the churches grew fastest where it was hardest. *Read:* Eliot, 'Journey of the Magi' (if PD in our rule: published 1927, Eliot died 1965 — original English published before 1929 qualifies). *Compare:* Revelation 21:5 (point back). *Lecture:* the century's witnesses table (including Martin Luther King, Mother Teresa, the Ugandan martyrs); point ahead to *Is God coming back?* and *The world church*. *Learn:* new `journey-of-the-magi` excerpt (if qualifies). *Look:* the Adoration of the Magi. *Music:* `we-three-kings`.

**Objection:** session 5, Freud and Russell.
