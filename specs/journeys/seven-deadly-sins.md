**Follows:** a paper in moral theology and medieval literature.

**Frame:** a map of the heart drawn by the desert monks: eight 'thoughts' (Evagrius) became seven deadly sins (Gregory), arranged by Dante as one mountain where love is purified. The journey climbs it: pride, envy, wrath, sloth, avarice, gluttony, lust, each with its remedy, then asks whether 'sin' is an outdated word.

**Real questions:** What are the seven deadly sins? Where do they come from? Are they in the Bible? Why is pride the worst? What is sloth really? What is the difference between envy and jealousy? Is anger always wrong? What are the seven virtues that oppose them? Is greed good? Is sin an outdated idea?

**Builds on:** *Dante* (Inferno and Paradiso; the Purgatorio terraces kept for here), *The line through the heart*, *Faith, hope and love* (acedia). **Read elsewhere:** Inferno, Paradiso XXXIII, Purgatorio XXX (*Dante*).

## Sessions

1. **Thoughts in the desert.** *Finding:* Evagrius, a monk in Egypt (d. 399), named eight 'thoughts' that attack the soul; Gregory the Great reshaped them into seven, with pride as their root. *Read:* Gregory, *Moralia* XXXI.45. *Compare:* Proverbs 6:16–19. *Lecture:* Evagrius and Cassian; the list's history; 'deadly' (capital) sins. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Bosch's *Seven Deadly Sins* is in the Prado: NGA? flag. *Music:* none.
2. **Love gone wrong.** *Finding:* Dante explains every sin as love disordered: love of the wrong thing (pride, envy, wrath), too little (sloth), or too much of a good thing (avarice, gluttony, lust). *Read:* *Purgatorio* XVII.91–139. *Compare:* Augustine on ordered love, *City of God* XV.22 ('virtue is rightly ordered love') (Dods). *Lecture:* the mountain's seven terraces (a table). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a mountain. *Music:* none.
3. **Pride and envy.** *Finding:* Pride is the root: putting self in God's place; envy is sorrow at another's good; the remedies are humility and kindness. *Read:* *Purgatorio* X–XII (selected). *Compare:* Philippians 2:3–8 (point back). *Lecture:* Lewis's 'the great sin' (our own words); envy and social media. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a proud figure humbled. *Music:* none.
4. **Wrath and sloth.** *Finding:* Wrath is anger that wills harm; sloth (*acedia*) is not laziness but not caring, the 'noonday demon'. *Read:* *Purgatorio* XVIII. *Compare:* Ephesians 4:26–27 and James 1:19–20. *Lecture:* righteous anger; acedia (Kathleen Norris, our own words). *Learn:* new `james-1-19`. *Look:* a sleeping figure. *Music:* none.
5. **Avarice, gluttony, lust.** *Finding:* The three sins of excess turn good things (money, food, sex) into masters; the remedies are generosity, temperance and chastity. *Read:* *Purgatorio* XIX and Chaucer, *The Parson's Tale* (selected). *Compare:* Galatians 5:16–26. *Lecture:* each sin and virtue; Marlowe's pageant. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Miser (Bosch used: another). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: is 'sin' an outdated idea?** *Finding:* Critics say talk of sin breeds guilt and shame, polices pleasure, and that 'greed is good' drives prosperity; the tradition answers that the sins name real harms we still see. *Read:* Mandeville, *The Fable of the Bees* (1714), 'The Grumbling Hive' (private vices, public benefits). *Compare:* Marlowe, *Doctor Faustus* II.2. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Mandeville; Freud on guilt; modern therapy language); replies (Karl Menninger, *Whatever Became of Sin?* 1973, our own words). *Debate:* Mandeville; Menninger; Gregory. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Climbing the mountain: the verdict.** *Finding:* The seven sins are a mirror, not a whip: knowing what quietly shapes us is the first step to being reshaped by grace. *Read:* *Purgatorio* XXVII (Virgil's last words: 'free, upright and whole is thy will') (Longfellow). *Compare:* Psalm 139:23–24 (point back). *Lecture:* the examen (point ahead to *Finding God's will*); confession in the traditions. *Learn:* `psalm-139-you-have-searched-me`. *Look:* none. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
