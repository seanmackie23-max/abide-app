**Follows:** a paper on the English Bible and English literature.

**Frame:** one sentence, Luke 2:8–14, traced through five translations (Wycliffe, Tyndale, Coverdale, the Geneva, the King James) to see how English found its holy voice; then the men who paid for it, the committees who made it, the phrases it gave the language, and whether a 400-year-old translation still matters.

**Real questions:** What is the King James Bible? Why was it made? Who translated it? Who was William Tyndale? How much of the KJV is Tyndale? Is the KJV the most accurate? Why do some people only read the KJV? What phrases come from the KJV? Why does this app quote the World English Bible? Was it dangerous to translate the Bible?

**Builds on:** *How to read the Bible* (translations), *The Reformation*. **Read elsewhere:** none (but note this app's WEB rule: explain it).

## Sessions

1. **Into English.** *Finding:* Wycliffe's followers translated the whole Bible from Latin around 1382; reading it in English became a crime. *Read:* Wycliffe Bible, John 1:1–5 and Luke 2:8–14. *Compare:* the Latin Vulgate (Jerome). *Lecture:* Wycliffe and the Lollards; the Constitutions of Oxford (1409). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a medieval manuscript Bible. *Music:* none.
2. **The ploughboy.** *Finding:* Tyndale vowed to make a ploughboy know more Scripture than a priest, translated from Greek and Hebrew, and was strangled and burned for it in 1536. *Read:* Tyndale, *Obedience* (the ploughboy) and his *Prologue*. *Compare:* Tyndale's Matthew 6:9–13 and 'let there be light'. *Lecture:* Tyndale's life; Henry VIII; how much of the KJV is Tyndale (estimates, cite David Daniell, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a martyr, or Tyndale (NGA: unlikely). *Music:* none.
3. **The psalms we sing.** *Finding:* Coverdale's Psalter, made in 1535, is still sung in the Book of Common Prayer: the most-heard English version of the Psalms for four centuries. *Read:* Coverdale, Psalms 23 and 46. *Compare:* KJV Psalm 23. *Lecture:* Coverdale; the Great Bible (1539); the Geneva Bible (1560) and its notes; the Bishops' Bible. *Learn:* `ps23` (KJV/Coverdale in memory? check). *Look:* a choir. *Music:* `crimond`.
4. **Six companies.** *Finding:* In 1604 James I commissioned 47 scholars in six companies at Westminster, Oxford and Cambridge; they did not start from scratch but made 'a good one better'. *Read:* *The Translators to the Reader* (selected). *Compare:* Hampton Court and James's rules (our own words). *Lecture:* the committees; Lancelot Andrewes; the 1611 edition and its errors (the 'Wicked Bible'); revision to 1769. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar's study. *Music:* `zadok`.
5. **The phrases of English.** *Finding:* The KJV gave English hundreds of phrases still in use, and its rhythms shaped writers from Milton to Lincoln to King. *Read:* KJV Isaiah 40:1–11 and 1 Corinthians 13. *Compare:* the same in the WEB. *Lecture:* 'the salt of the earth', 'a law unto themselves', 'the powers that be' (a table); Lincoln and King (point back); Handel's *Messiah* text. *Learn:* `corinthians-love-is-patient`. *Look:* none; a reader. *Music:* `messiah`.
6. **The case against: beautiful but wrong?** *Finding:* Later manuscript discoveries and scholarship showed the KJV's Greek base was late and some readings wrong; critics also say its archaic English now hides the meaning. *Read:* KJV 1 John 5:7–8 (the Johannine Comma) and Mark 16:9–20, with the WEB's notes. *Compare:* the Revised Version preface (1881). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: textual criticism (point back to *What is the Bible?*); 'KJV-only' believers and their case; modern translations' aims. *Debate:* KJV-only; a textual critic; a literary defender (Robert Alter, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Why it still matters: the verdict.** *Finding:* The KJV is no longer the most accurate translation, but it remains the most influential book in English; this app quotes the WEB for accuracy and copyright reasons and keeps the KJV in its memory pieces and music. *Read:* KJV and WEB Luke 2:8–14 side by side. *Compare:* the 2011 quatercentenary (our own words). *Lecture:* choosing a Bible today; why the WEB here. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Annunciation to the shepherds. *Music:* `o-come-all-ye-faithful`.

**Objection:** session 6, the KJV's textual and linguistic weaknesses.

**Traditions:** Catholic English Bibles (Douay-Rheims 1582/1610), Orthodox, Protestant side by side.
