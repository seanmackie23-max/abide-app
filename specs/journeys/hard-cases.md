**Follows:** a paper in applied ethics (bioethics and the ethics of war).

**Frame:** four hard cases (war, the death penalty, abortion, euthanasia) reasoned with the tools the journey teaches first: the sanctity of life, double effect, the just war, conscience, and the harm principle. Each case: the question, the traditions' positions side by side with their reasons, the strongest dissent, and what is shared. Abortion, euthanasia, just war and the death penalty, with the traditions' positions set out fairly, including where Christians disagree. Needs a pastor's review.

**Real questions:** What does the Bible say about abortion? Is euthanasia wrong? What is a just war? Is the death penalty Christian? What is the doctrine of double effect? Are all Christians pro-life? Can Christians be pacifists? Should religious views shape the law? What is the harm principle? What do the churches teach?

**Builds on:** *Right and wrong*, *Number and nature* (Hippocratic Oath), *Sermon on the Mount* (the other cheek). **Read elsewhere:** Oath, Matthew 5:38–48.

## Sessions

1. **Thou shalt not kill.** *Finding:* The commandment forbids murder, the shedding of innocent blood, because humans bear God's image; it is the root of the sanctity of life. *Read:* Genesis 9:1–7, with Exodus 20:13 pointed back to *Exodus and the law*. *Compare:* Didache 1–2. *Lecture:* *ratsach* (murder) and killing; the image of God (point back); the early Church on infanticide and the arena (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Cain and Abel (point back) or a mother and child. *Music:* none.
2. **Double effect.** *Finding:* Aquinas reasoned that an act with a good and a bad effect may be allowed if the bad is not intended and is proportionate: the most used tool in Christian medical ethics. *Read:* Aquinas II–II q.64 a.7 (self-defence). *Compare:* q.64 a.2 and a.5. *Lecture:* the four conditions; cases (the terminal painkiller, ectopic pregnancy); critics. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
3. **War.** *Finding:* From Augustine to Aquinas the Church taught that war is sometimes just (right authority, just cause, right intention) and always tragic; pacifists have always dissented. *Read:* Augustine, *Letter* 189 and *City of God* XIX.7; Aquinas II–II q.40 a.1. *Compare:* Anscombe, 'Mr Truman's Degree' (our own words) on Hiroshima. *Lecture:* just war criteria; nuclear deterrence; pacifism (Mennonite, Quaker, Hauerwas, our own words). *Debate:* just war; pacifist; realist. *Learn:* `isaiah-2-4`. *Look:* none glorifying; a ruined city. *Music:* none.
4. **The death penalty.** *Finding:* The Bible allowed capital punishment; the Church accepted it for centuries; modern Catholic teaching (2018) and many Protestants now oppose it as unnecessary and contrary to dignity. *Read:* Genesis 9:6 recalled with John 8:2–11. *Compare:* Aquinas II–II q.64 a.2 (the common good). *Lecture:* positions side by side (Catholic catechism change; evangelical support; Orthodox); evidence on deterrence and wrongful convictions. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
5. **The beginning of life.** *Finding:* Christians from the Didache on opposed abortion; today churches differ on hard cases and on law, and the questions (when life begins, the mother's life, poverty) must be set out fairly. *Read:* Didache 2 recalled; Psalm 139:13–16 (point back). *Compare:* the case for choice at full strength (Judith Jarvis Thomson's violinist, our own words). *Lecture:* positions side by side (Catholic, Orthodox, evangelical, mainline Protestant; Jewish); what is shared (care for mothers and children). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Visitation. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: should faith shape the law?** *Finding:* Mill argued that the only reason to restrain someone is to prevent harm to others; many argue Christians should not impose their morality in a plural society. *Read:* Mill, *On Liberty* ch. I and IV. *Compare:* Devlin and Hart debate (1959–63; our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength; public reason (Rawls, our own words); replies (all laws rest on moral views; natural law as public reason). *Debate:* Mill; Devlin; a Christian liberal. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **The end of life: the verdict.** *Finding:* Christians reason about hard cases from the dignity of every life, the limits of what we may do, and love for the suffering; they often agree on principles and differ on application and law. *Read:* 2 Corinthians 4:16–18 or Psalm 31:15 ('my times are in your hand'). *Compare:* Cicely Saunders and the hospice movement (our own words). *Lecture:* euthanasia and assisted dying positions side by side; palliative care; how to disagree well. *Learn:* `psalm-31-5`. *Look:* a nurse at a bedside (Chardin used: another). *Music:* `abide-with-me`.

**Objection:** session 6.
