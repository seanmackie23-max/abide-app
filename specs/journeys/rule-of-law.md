**Follows:** a first-year paper in legal history and constitutional theory.

**Frame:** a chain of 'no's said to kings: Nathan to David (point back), Ambrose to Theodosius, the Pope to the Emperor at Canossa, the barons to John at Runnymede, Bracton and Coke to their kings, Rutherford's *Lex, Rex*, and Blackstone's law of nature. The idea: law is above the ruler because God is.

**Real questions:** What is the rule of law? What is Magna Carta? Why is Magna Carta important? Who was Ambrose and why did he stop an emperor? What happened at Canossa? Where do our legal rights come from? What is due process? What does 'Lex, Rex' mean? What is common law? Why does the rule of law matter today?

**Builds on:** *Kings and prophets* (the king under the law; Nathan and Naboth, read); *Rome* (Roman law). **Read elsewhere:** Deuteronomy 17, 1 Kings 21, 2 Samuel 12 (*Kings and prophets*).

## Sessions

1. **The king under the Torah.** *Finding:* The Bible put the king under the law and gave prophets the right to tell him so: a radical idea in the ancient world. *Read:* point back to the readings in *Kings and prophets*; read Psalm 72:1–4 and 12–14. *Compare:* Hammurabi's prologue (point back to *Exodus*). *Lecture:* law and king in the ancient Near East; the prophets as the first opposition. *Learn:* new `psalm-72-12`. *Look:* Nathan before David. *Music:* `zadok`.
2. **The bishop and the emperor.** *Finding:* In 390 Bishop Ambrose barred the emperor Theodosius from church until he did public penance for a massacre at Thessalonica. *Read:* Theodoret, *Church History* V.17. *Compare:* Ambrose, *Letter* 51 to Theodosius (NPNF II.10). *Lecture:* church and empire; two powers (Gelasius, 494). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Ambrose and Theodosius (Rubens/Van Dyck: NGA? flag). *Music:* none.
3. **Canossa.** *Finding:* In 1077 the emperor Henry IV stood barefoot in the snow at Canossa to beg Pope Gregory VII's pardon: the investiture contest made clear that no ruler owned the Church. *Read:* Gregory VII's letter on Canossa (Thatcher and McNeal). *Compare:* Dictatus Papae (1075), selected. *Lecture:* the investiture contest; the Concordat of Worms (1122); Harold Berman's 'Papal Revolution' (our own words). *Debate:* liberty of the Church or papal power grab? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a medieval king and pope. *Music:* none.
4. **Runnymede.** *Finding:* In 1215 the barons forced King John to seal Magna Carta: no free man to be imprisoned except by lawful judgement, and justice not to be sold. *Read:* Magna Carta clauses 1, 12, 39–40. *Compare:* Stephen Langton's role (our own words). *Lecture:* the charter's later life (1225, Coke, America); what it did and did not do for ordinary people. *Learn:* new `magna-carta-39`. *Look:* a medieval charter or king (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
5. **Under God and the law.** *Finding:* The English judges wrote that the king is under God and the law, because the law makes the king; Coke told James I so to his face. *Read:* Bracton I.8; Coke, *Prohibitions del Roy* (1607). *Compare:* the Petition of Right (1628). *Lecture:* common law; judges and juries; the divine right of kings (James I's *Trew Law*, our own words) and its opponents. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a judge or a court. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: whose law?** *Finding:* Critics say 'the rule of law' has often meant the rule of the strong through law; and religious law above the king can become clergy above the people. *Read:* Rutherford, *Lex, Rex* Q. XXVI (the law above the king, and resistance). *Compare:* Hobbes, *Leviathan* ch. XXVI and XXIX (the sovereign is not subject to the civil laws). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Hobbes; legal realists; theocracy fears; replies (Locke point ahead to Hall VII as the hall; Lord Bingham's eight principles, our own words). *Debate:* Hobbes; Rutherford; a modern legal scholar. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a crowd before a palace. *Music:* none.
7. **The law of nature: the verdict.** *Finding:* Blackstone taught that human law is valid only if it agrees with the law of nature and revelation: the long road from Sinai to the modern constitution. *Read:* Blackstone, *Commentaries* I, Introduction §2. *Compare:* Martin Luther King, 'Letter from Birmingham Jail' (1963) on unjust law and Aquinas (our own words; point ahead to Hall VII as the hall). *Lecture:* the rule of law today (independent courts, due process, equality before the law). *Learn:* `amos-5-24`. *Look:* Justice with her scales. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, Hobbes and the realists.
