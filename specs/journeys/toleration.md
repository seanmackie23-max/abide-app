**Follows:** a paper on the wars of religion and the origins of religious liberty.

**Frame:** a century of fire, and the slow discovery that faith cannot be forced: the massacre, the edict, the voice in the wilderness (Castellio), the colony built on freedom (Rhode Island), the philosopher who made the case (Locke). The objection: did Christians learn toleration, or did a secular state impose it on them?

**Real questions:** What were the wars of religion? What was St Bartholomew's Day? What was the Edict of Nantes? Who was Servetus? Where does religious freedom come from? Who was Roger Williams? What did Locke argue? Did secularism bring peace? Is tolerance a Christian virtue? Does tolerance mean everything is equally true?

**Builds on:** *The Reformation* (the cost). **Read elsewhere:** none.

## Sessions

1. **Compel them to come in?** *Finding:* From Augustine on, many Christians argued that the state should force heretics back: a reading of Luke 14:23 with a long and terrible history. *Read:* Augustine, *Letter* 93 (on the Donatists). *Compare:* Luke 9:51–56 (Jesus rebukes the disciples who want fire from heaven). *Lecture:* heresy laws; the Inquisition (with numbers and their uncertainty); Protestants too (Servetus, 1553). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a heresy trial. *Music:* none.
2. **The massacre.** *Finding:* On St Bartholomew's Day 1572, thousands of French Protestants were murdered in Paris and the provinces; Europe's religious wars killed millions over a century. *Read:* Sully's eyewitness account. *Compare:* Psalm 79:1–4. *Lecture:* the French wars; the Thirty Years' War (1618–48); the English Civil War. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a battle or massacre scene. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
3. **The man who said no.** *Finding:* After Servetus was burned at Geneva, Castellio wrote that 'to kill a man is not to defend a doctrine but to kill a man': the first great Christian case for liberty of conscience. *Read:* Castellio (our own words, short quotation). *Compare:* Matthew 13:24–30 (let the weeds grow until the harvest). *Lecture:* Castellio and Calvin; Anabaptists and the first demands for freedom; the wheat and tares through history. *Learn:* new `matthew-13-30`. *Look:* none; a scholar. *Music:* none.
4. **Peace by law.** *Finding:* Henri IV's Edict of Nantes (1598) gave French Protestants freedom of worship in set places: toleration as a peace treaty. *Read:* Edict of Nantes, selected articles. *Compare:* the Peace of Westphalia (1648; our own words). *Lecture:* *cuius regio, eius religio*; Henri IV ('Paris is worth a Mass'); the revocation (1685) and the Huguenot exodus. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Henri IV or a treaty scene. *Music:* none.
5. **Soul liberty.** *Finding:* Roger Williams, banished from Massachusetts, founded Rhode Island on 'soul liberty' and argued that forced worship 'stinks in God's nostrils'. *Read:* Williams, *The Bloudy Tenent* (selected). *Compare:* Isaiah 42:3 (a bruised reed). *Lecture:* Puritan New England; Williams and the Narragansett; Baptists and Quakers; William Penn. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an early American landscape. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: did toleration come from Christianity?** *Finding:* Many argue toleration was won against the churches by sceptics and states tired of war; others answer that its best arguments (conscience, the nature of faith) were Christian. *Read:* Locke, *A Letter Concerning Toleration* (opening and the magistrate). *Compare:* Voltaire, *Treatise on Toleration* (1763) ch. 22 (Smollett/Fleming: confirm) or Bayle (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: the secular account (William Cavanaugh's 'myth of religious violence' answers it from the other side; our own words); Locke's exclusions (Catholics and atheists). *Debate:* Voltaire; Locke; Cavanaugh. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Living with difference: the verdict.** *Finding:* Christians learned, slowly and at great cost, that faith cannot be forced because God himself does not force it; religious liberty is one of the West's hardest-won gifts, and it must be defended for all. *Read:* the Second Vatican Council, *Dignitatis Humanae* 2 (1965; our own words) beside the First Amendment (1791). *Compare:* 2 Corinthians 4:2. *Lecture:* tolerance and truth; religious freedom today worldwide. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a church and meeting-house side by side. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, toleration as a secular victory over religion.

**Traditions:** Catholic (*Dignitatis Humanae*), Reformed, Baptist, Anglican side by side on church and state.

**Voices:** Perez Zagorin, *How the Idea of Religious Toleration Came to the West*; William Cavanaugh; Benjamin Kaplan, *Divided by Faith*; Alexandra Walsham; John Coffey.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Is toleration a Christian idea?'; 'Assess Locke's Letter'; 'Tolerance and truth'.
