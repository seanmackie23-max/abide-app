**Follows:** an advanced paper on Western civilisation and its critics.

**Frame:** the capstone of the history halls: what is the West (Athens, Jerusalem and Rome, as Arnold's Hebraism and Hellenism), what did it give (the person, reason, law, liberty, science), what are its crimes (judged by its own principles), what are the strongest critiques (from outside and within), and is it worth defending, and how?

**Real questions:** What is Western civilisation? Is the West in decline? What did the West give the world? Is the West racist? What is Orientalism? What did Solzhenitsyn say at Harvard? Is Western civilisation Christian? Can the West survive without Christianity? Should we be proud or ashamed? How do you defend the West?

**Builds on:** every hall so far; the hall's case-against journeys (slavery, Crusades, conquest, wars of religion, the twentieth century).

## Sessions

1. **Athens, Jerusalem and Rome.** *Finding:* The West is the long conversation between three roots: Greek reason, Jewish and Christian faith, Roman law. *Read:* Arnold, *Culture and Anarchy*, Preface (Hebraism and Hellenism). *Compare:* Tertullian (point back). *Lecture:* definitions of the West (Christopher Dawson, Larry Siedentop, Rémi Brague, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a classical-Christian building (a cathedral with columns). *Music:* `tallis-fantasia`.
2. **What it gave.** *Finding:* The West's gifts to the world (the dignity of the individual, the university, science, the rule of law, representative government, abolition) came from those roots, and from people who believed them. *Read:* Tocqueville, *Democracy in America* I.ii.9. *Compare:* Luke 12:48. *Lecture:* a table of gifts and their roots (pointing back to the halls). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
3. **Its crimes, by its own measure.** *Finding:* Slavery, conquest, the Crusades' atrocities, the wars of religion and the camps were real; what condemned them was the West's own Christian and humane principles. *Read:* Las Casas recalled (point back) and Douglass (point back). *Compare:* Isaiah 2:2–4 (point back). *Lecture:* each hall's case-against recalled in one table. *Learn:* `micah-what-does-the-lord-require`. *Look:* none. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
4. **The case against, from outside.** *Finding:* Edward Said and postcolonial critics argue that the West's idea of itself was built by defining others as inferior, and its universalism hid domination. *Read:* Said (our own words). *Compare:* Kipling, 'The White Man's Burden' (1899, PD) as the critics' evidence. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength; its critics (Bernard Lewis, Ibn Warraq, our own words). *Debate:* Said; Lewis; a non-Western Christian voice (Sanneh, point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against, from within.** *Finding:* From Rousseau to Nietzsche to Marx and the Frankfurt School, the West's own thinkers have attacked its foundations, and some argue it has nothing left to defend. *Read:* Nietzsche (point back) and Spengler, *The Decline of the West* (1918; Atkinson 1926: Charles Francis Atkinson died?; our own words if unsure). *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 1:9 (point back). *Lecture:* decline narratives; Spengler, Toynbee; 'woke' and anti-'woke' debates set out fairly (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a ruin. *Music:* none.
6. **A world split apart.** *Finding:* Solzhenitsyn told Harvard in 1978 that the West had lost its courage and its spiritual centre, and that it could be saved only by recovering the sense that a human being is answerable to something higher. *Read:* Solzhenitsyn, Harvard address (our own words; one short quotation). *Compare:* Revelation 3:15–17 (Laodicea). *Lecture:* the speech and its reception; Roger Scruton's 'oikophobia' (our own words); Holland's *Dominion* recalled. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Practice portrait of Solzhenitsyn. *Music:* none.
7. **Worth defending? The verdict, and making the case.** *Finding:* The West is worth defending not because it is better than others' homes but because its best principles (every person in God's image, truth sought freely, power under law, mercy to the weak) are worth keeping for everyone; it is defended by living them. *Read:* Luke 12:48 recalled; Tocqueville recalled. *Compare:* the voice of a non-Western Christian on the West's gifts (Vishal Mangalwadi, *The Book That Made Your World*, our own words). *Lecture:* how to defend: by gratitude, by repentance, by renewal. **Make the case** (ends Hall VII). *Learn:* `lincoln-gettysburg`. *Look:* a cathedral at dawn. *Music:* `zadok`.
