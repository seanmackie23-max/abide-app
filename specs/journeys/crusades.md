**Follows:** a paper on the Crusades (medieval history).

**Frame:** a trial of the Crusades in seven hearings: the call (Clermont 1095), why men went, the eastern Christians' view, Jerusalem in 1099, a Muslim neighbour's view, the sack of Constantinople in 1204 (the worst charge), and the verdict. The hall's case against, faced honestly.

**Real questions:** What were the Crusades? Why did the Crusades start? Were the Crusades a response to Islamic aggression? Were crusaders greedy or pious? How many Crusades were there? What happened when Jerusalem was taken? What did Muslims think? What happened in 1204? Did the Pope apologise? Were the Crusades a betrayal of Christianity? Why do people still argue about them?

**Builds on:** *Chivalry*; *One root, two branches* (the 1096 massacres of Jews named there). **Read elsewhere:** none.

## Sessions

1. **God wills it.** *Finding:* In 1095 Pope Urban II called the knights of France to help the eastern Christians and free Jerusalem, promising forgiveness of sins: the crowd cried 'God wills it'. *Read:* Fulcher of Chartres's version of Urban's speech. *Compare:* Robert the Monk's more violent version. *Lecture:* the Seljuk advance; Manzikert (1071); Alexius's appeal; pilgrimage and penance. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a pope or a pilgrimage. *Music:* none.
2. **Why did they go?** *Finding:* Most crusaders paid heavily to go and many died; historians now see piety and penance as the main motive, mixed with adventure, family and greed. *Read:* the *Gesta Francorum* on the departure, or a crusader's charter selling land (Riley-Smith's evidence, our own words). *Compare:* Matthew 16:24. *Lecture:* the 'younger sons' theory and its rejection (Riley-Smith, our own words); the People's Crusade and the Rhineland massacres of Jews (1096). *Debate:* Riley-Smith; Runciman. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* knights departing. *Music:* none.
3. **The eastern Christians' view.** *Finding:* To the Byzantine princess Anna Comnena the crusaders were barbarians to be managed: allies and threats at once. *Read:* *Alexiad* X.5–6 (Dawes). *Compare:* Psalm 122:6–9 (pray for the peace of Jerusalem). *Lecture:* Byzantium; East and West after 1054 (point ahead to Hall VI? no: briefly here). *Learn:* new `psalm-122-6`. *Look:* a Byzantine icon or scene. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
4. **Jerusalem, 1099.** *Finding:* When the crusaders took Jerusalem they massacred many of its Muslim and Jewish inhabitants; the sources boast of it, and Muslim memory never forgot. *Read:* the *Gesta Francorum* on the fall of Jerusalem. *Compare:* Matthew 26:52 (point back to *The sound of faith*, where it is read within Matthew 26) and Psalm 122. *Lecture:* the evidence for the massacre (how many: Kedar, our own words); the Latin kingdom; Saladin's retaking in 1187 without massacre. *Learn:* new `matthew-26-52` (the verse alone, as a memory piece). *Look:* Jerusalem. *Music:* none.
5. **A Muslim neighbour.** *Finding:* Usama ibn Munqidh, a Syrian emir, found the Franks brave, crude and sometimes friendly: the Crusades seen from the other side. *Read:* Usama's *Memoirs* (our own words, with one short quotation). *Compare:* Ibn al-Athir on 1099 (our own words). *Lecture:* Muslim responses; jihad revived; how the Crusades were remembered in the Arab world (Carole Hillenbrand, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Middle Eastern scene. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: 1204 and betrayal.** *Finding:* In 1204 a crusade sacked Christian Constantinople, the worst crime of the movement; with the massacres and the Albigensian crusade it is the strongest case that the Crusades betrayed Christ. *Read:* Villehardouin on 1204 (Marzials). *Compare:* Nicetas Choniates's lament (our own words / Munro 1912). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: holy war as contradiction of the Sermon on the Mount (point back); Runciman's verdict ('a long act of intolerance in the name of God'); John Paul II's apology to the Orthodox (2001). *Debate:* Runciman; Thomas Madden; Bartholomew I. *Learn:* `micah-what-does-the-lord-require`. *Look:* the sack of a city. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
7. **Holy war? The verdict.** *Finding:* The Crusades were a response to real conquests, driven by real faith, and they included real atrocities; Christianity's own teaching is what condemns those atrocities, and the Church has said so. *Read:* Francis of Assisi meeting Sultan al-Kamil (1219) (Bonaventure's *Life* IX.7–9; tr. 1868/1904) as another way. *Compare:* Augustine on just war (point ahead to Hall IX as the hall). *Lecture:* the Crusades in modern politics; how to judge the past by its own best principles. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Francis before the Sultan (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, at full strength in Runciman's terms and the Orthodox memory of 1204.
