**Follows:** a paper on nineteenth-century thought and the loss of faith.

**Frame:** the tide going out (Arnold's 'Dover Beach'): the Victorians who lost their faith and felt its loss, the prophet who said God is dead and we have killed him (Nietzsche), what he thought would follow, the novelist who tried to keep morality without God (Eliot), and one man who came back (Tolstoy). What happens to a world that stops believing?

**Real questions:** What did Nietzsche mean by 'God is dead'? Was Nietzsche an atheist? What is nihilism? What is 'Dover Beach' about? Why did Victorians lose their faith? Can you have morality without God? What did Tolstoy find? Is the West post-Christian? What replaces God? Is God coming back?

**Builds on:** *Psalms and wisdom* (Tolstoy's despair, read), *Darwin and design*, *Hume and the miracles*. **Read elsewhere:** Tolstoy, *A Confession* IV–X (*Psalms and wisdom*); Nietzsche's *Genealogy* (*Sermon on the Mount*), *Antichrist* (*First Christians*), *Twilight* 'Problem of Socrates' (*Socrates*).

## Sessions

1. **The sea of faith.** *Finding:* Arnold heard the sea of faith's 'melancholy, long, withdrawing roar' on Dover beach in 1851: the Victorians lost faith slowly and with grief. *Read:* 'Dover Beach'. *Compare:* Psalm 77:1–10 ('has God forgotten to be gracious?'). *Lecture:* the Victorian crisis of faith (Darwin, biblical criticism, Strauss in Eliot's translation, moral objections to hell). *Learn:* new `dover-beach-closing`. *Look:* a seashore at night. *Music:* none.
2. **The darkling thrush.** *Finding:* Hardy, who could not believe, heard a thrush sing on the last evening of the century and wondered whether it knew of 'some blessed Hope' of which he was unaware. *Read:* 'The Darkling Thrush' and 'God's Funeral'. *Compare:* Romans 8:22–25 (point back). *Lecture:* Hardy; agnosticism (Huxley's coinage, 1869). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a winter landscape. *Music:* none.
3. **God is dead.** *Finding:* Nietzsche's madman says God is dead and we have killed him, and asks whether we are great enough to bear what we have done. *Read:* *The Gay Science* §125 and §343. *Compare:* Psalm 14:1 (point back). *Lecture:* what Nietzsche meant (a cultural event, not a metaphysical argument); his life (pastor's son, Basel, madness 1889); the will to power, the Übermensch. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a lantern at night. *Music:* none.
4. **Morality without God?** *Finding:* George Eliot told a friend that God was inconceivable, immortality unbelievable, and duty absolute; Nietzsche said that English morality without God would not survive. *Read:* Myers's account; Nietzsche, *Twilight* 'Skirmishes' §5. *Compare:* Micah 6:8 (point back). *Lecture:* Victorian humanism (Mill, Eliot, the Ethical Societies); Nietzsche's prediction; point ahead to *Right and wrong* (Hall IX) as the hall. *Debate:* Eliot or Nietzsche? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of George Eliot (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
5. **The case for the death of God.** *Finding:* The secular case at full strength: belief faded because it became less believable and less needed; secular societies are often peaceful, prosperous and humane. *Read:* Bertrand Russell, 'A Free Man's Worship' (1903) (PD). *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 1:2 (point back). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: secularisation theory (Steve Bruce, our own words); Scandinavian evidence; replies (Charles Taylor's *A Secular Age*, our own words). *Debate:* Russell; Bruce; Taylor. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a modern city scene. *Music:* none.
6. **What fills the gap.** *Finding:* Nietzsche warned that the death of God would bring nihilism and new gods; the twentieth century's ideologies are often read as his prophecy fulfilled. *Read:* Nietzsche, *The Will to Power* §2 (nihilism) (Ludovici, 1909). *Compare:* Romans 1:21–25 (exchanging God for idols). *Lecture:* nihilism; nationalism and communism as substitute religions (Eric Voegelin, Raymond Aron, our own words; point ahead to *The twentieth century* as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a broken statue or idol. *Music:* none.
7. **The road back: the verdict.** *Finding:* Tolstoy found his way back not through argument but by watching the faith of ordinary people who knew how to live and die; the death of God may be the death of a certain idea of God. *Read:* Tolstoy, *A Confession* XI–XVI (Maude). *Compare:* Luke 15:17–20 (point back). *Lecture:* Tom Holland's *Dominion* on how Christian the secular West still is (our own words); the return of the question (point ahead to *Is God coming back?*). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* peasants at work, or a village church. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.

**Objection:** session 5, the secular case.
