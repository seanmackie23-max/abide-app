**Follows:** a paper in liturgy and sacraments.

**Frame:** walk into a service and see what each part is and where it came from: the day (Sunday), the water (baptism), the word (readings and sermon), the table (the Eucharist), the prayers and the sending. Then the Reformation argument over what the bread is, and the verdict.

**Real questions:** What happens in a church service? Why do Christians go to church on Sunday? What is baptism? Why do some churches baptise babies? What is Communion? Is the bread really Jesus's body? Why are services so different (Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, evangelical)? What is the liturgy? Do I have to be baptised to take Communion? What should I do if I go?

**Builds on:** *The last week* (the supper), *Exodus* (Passover), *Time made holy* (the year and the day). **Read elsewhere:** Exodus 20 Sabbath (*Time made holy*), Acts 2 (*Time made holy*), Emmaus (*What is the Bible?*).

## Sessions

1. **The first day of the week.** *Finding:* Christians gathered on Sunday because it was the day of resurrection, and the shape of their gathering is described within a century. *Read:* Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 65–67. *Compare:* Pliny X.96 (point back: 'on a fixed day before dawn'). *Lecture:* Sabbath and Sunday; the oldest order of service. *Learn:* new `psalm-118-24`. *Look:* a congregation or church interior. *Music:* `holy-holy-holy`.
2. **Water: baptism.** *Finding:* Baptism is the door: dying and rising with Christ, once, in water and the name of the Trinity. *Read:* Romans 6:1–11 and Matthew 28:16–20. *Compare:* Didache 7 and Cyril of Jerusalem, *Mystagogical Lecture* 2. *Lecture:* John's baptism; early baptisteries; immersion and pouring; infant and believer's baptism side by side. *Debate:* infants or believers? *Learn:* new `romans-6-4`. *Look:* the Baptism of Christ. *Music:* `come-down-o-love-divine`.
3. **The word.** *Finding:* From the synagogue the Church kept readings, psalms and a sermon: the service teaches as well as prays. *Read:* Justin, *First Apology* 67 (again, on the readings) with Luke 4:16–21 (point back). *Compare:* the Book of Common Prayer (1662), Morning Prayer (opening sentences and Venite). *Lecture:* the lectionary; the creed (point ahead to Hall VIII as the hall); hymns. *Learn:* `psalm-95-1`. *Look:* a preacher, or Christ reading in the synagogue. *Music:* `crimond`.
4. **The table.** *Finding:* At the heart of most Christian worship is a meal: thanksgiving (*eucharistia*) over bread and wine, as Jesus commanded. *Read:* 1 Corinthians 11:17–34. *Compare:* Didache 9–10 (the earliest eucharistic prayers). *Lecture:* the shape of the liturgy (Dix's four actions, our own words); Orthodox, Catholic, Anglican, Reformed, Baptist side by side. *Learn:* new `1-corinthians-11-26`. *Look:* the Last Supper or a Mass scene. *Music:* `ave-verum`.
5. **The case against: empty ritual?** *Finding:* The prophets and the Reformers both attacked ritual without justice or faith; the defence of liturgy is that it trains love when feelings fail. *Read:* Amos 5:21–24 is used; use Isaiah 29:13 and Hebrews 10:23–25. *Compare:* the Quaker case for silence (Robert Barclay, *Apology*, Proposition 11, 1678). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: ritual as magic, hypocrisy or performance (Barclay, the Enlightenment, modern 'spiritual but not religious'); replies from the tradition. *Learn:* `corinthians-love-is-patient`. *Look:* a Quaker meeting or a simple chapel. *Music:* `dear-lord-and-father`.
6. **Is it really his body?** *Finding:* The Reformation's sharpest quarrel was over what the bread is: changed (Catholic), Christ present in, with and under (Lutheran), spiritually received (Reformed), or a memorial (Zwingli). *Read:* Ambrose, *On the Mysteries* 8–9. *Compare:* the Book of Common Prayer (1662), the words of administration and the 'Black Rubric'. *Lecture:* transubstantiation (Lateran IV, Aquinas), the Marburg Colloquy 1529, Cranmer; Orthodox mystery. *Debate:* four views side by side. *Learn:* new `john-6-35`. *Look:* the Mass of St Gregory or a Eucharist. *Music:* `o-magnum-mysterium`.
7. **Going for the first time: the verdict.** *Finding:* Every service, however different, has the same bones: gathering, word, table or prayer, sending; knowing them, you can walk into any church. *Read:* Hebrews 12:22–24 (the heavenly worship). *Compare:* the BCP dismissal and the Orthodox liturgy's 'let us depart in peace'. *Lecture:* a practical guide: what to expect, when to stand and sit, whether to receive. *Learn:* `blessing`. *Look:* a church interior full of light. *Music:* `zadok`. **Make the case** essay is in *The end of the story*, not here.

**Objection:** session 5, ritual as empty or magical, in the Quakers' and Enlightenment critics' terms.

**Traditions:** baptism and the Eucharist in six traditions side by side; who may receive (open and closed communion).

**Voices:** Gregory Dix, *The Shape of the Liturgy*; Alexander Schmemann, *For the Life of the World*; James K. A. Smith; Rowan Williams.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Infant or believer's baptism?'; 'What is the real presence?'; 'Why ritual?'.
