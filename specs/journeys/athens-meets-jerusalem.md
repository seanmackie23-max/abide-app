**Follows:** a paper on early Christian thought and its relation to classical philosophy.

**Frame:** a quarrel in one sentence. Tertullian asked, 'What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?'; Justin answered, 'Whatever has been well said belongs to us Christians'. The journey sets out the two answers, then the middle way of Clement, Origen and Basil (take the gold out of Egypt), then the pagans' own answer (Celsus), and the verdict.

**Real questions:** Did early Christians reject philosophy? What did Tertullian mean? Who was Justin Martyr? What is the Logos? Did Christianity borrow from Plato? Can you be a Christian and a philosopher? What did pagans think of Christian ideas? Is faith against reason?

**Builds on:** the Athens hall; *Is there a God?* (Acts 17, read); *Socrates* (Justin on Socrates, compare). **Read elsewhere:** Acts 17 (*Is there a God?*); Justin Second Apology 10 is a compare in *Socrates*: if so, read 13 here.

## Sessions

1. **Foolishness to the Greeks.** *Finding:* Paul said the cross was foolishness to the Greeks, yet argued with philosophers in their own terms: the tension is in the New Testament itself. *Read:* 1 Corinthians 1:18–31. *Compare:* Acts 17:22–31 recalled (point back). *Lecture:* Hellenistic Judaism and Philo; Greek in the New Testament; *logos* in John (point back). *Learn:* `acts-17-paul-athens`. *Look:* St Paul preaching at Athens (Raphael's cartoon is not NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
2. **The seeds of the Word.** *Finding:* Justin taught that the Logos sowed seeds of truth in every mind, so that Socrates and Heraclitus lived 'with reason' before Christ. *Read:* Justin, *First Apology* 46 and *Second Apology* 13. *Compare:* John 1:9. *Lecture:* Justin's life (philosopher's cloak, martyrdom in Rome c. 165); the *logos spermatikos*. *Learn:* `john-in-the-beginning-was-the-word`. *Look:* a philosopher-saint. *Music:* none.
3. **What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?** *Finding:* Tertullian warned that philosophy is the mother of heresy and that Christians need no curiosity after Christ. *Read:* Tertullian, *Prescription against Heretics* 7. *Compare:* Colossians 2:8. *Lecture:* Tertullian the lawyer of Carthage; Gnosticism; the 'credo quia absurdum' misquotation. *Debate:* Justin or Tertullian? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a severe church father. *Music:* none.
4. **Spoiling the Egyptians.** *Finding:* Clement, Origen and Basil found a middle way: philosophy was Greece's schoolmaster to Christ, and its gold can be taken out of Egypt and used for God. *Read:* Clement, *Stromata* I.5 and Origen, *Letter to Gregory*. *Compare:* Exodus 12:35–36. *Lecture:* Alexandria; the catechetical school; Origen's genius and condemnation. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Israelites with Egyptian gold, or a scholar. *Music:* none.
5. **How to read the pagans.** *Finding:* Basil told Christian students to read Homer and the poets like bees, taking the honey and leaving the poison: the charter of Christian classical education. *Read:* Basil, *Address to Young Men on Greek Literature* (Padelford). *Compare:* Jerome's dream ('you are a Ciceronian, not a Christian', *Letter* 22.30, NPNF). *Lecture:* the Cappadocians; Christian schools; why the classics survived. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Jerome (several NGA works). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: the pagans answer.** *Finding:* Celsus, the first great critic of Christianity, called it a faith for the ignorant that demands belief without reason; Origen answered him point by point. *Read:* Origen, *Against Celsus* I.9 and III.44 (Celsus's charge and the reply). *Compare:* Porphyry (fragments, in our own words); Julian the Apostate on Christians and the classics (*Rescript on Christian Teachers*, Wright 1923, Loeb: Wilmer Cave Wright died 1951, published 1923: qualifies). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: faith as credulity; Christianity as a religion of slaves and women (Celsus's own sneer); replies. *Debate:* Celsus; Origen; Julian. *Learn:* new `1-peter-3-15` (if not used). *Look:* a Roman emperor. *Music:* none.
7. **Faith seeking understanding: the verdict.** *Finding:* The Church chose Justin and Basil over Tertullian: faith and reason were to work together, and that choice made the medieval universities possible. *Read:* Philo, *On the Creation* 1–3 (a Jew who joined Moses and Plato first). *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* VIII.5–9 (the Platonists closest to us) (Dods). *Lecture:* the synthesis; what it cost and gained; Pope Benedict XVI's Regensburg lecture (2006; our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a School of Athens-like gathering. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, Celsus and Julian.
