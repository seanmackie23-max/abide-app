**Follows:** a paper in ethics (the cardinal virtues).

**Frame:** the four 'hinge' (*cardo*) virtues on which a life turns, one session each, then how they fit together: wisdom (seeing rightly), justice (giving each their due), courage (holding to the good under fear), self-control (ordering desire). Each session: the classical source, the Christian transformation, and a practice.

**Real questions:** What are the cardinal virtues? What is prudence? What is justice? What is courage? What is temperance? Where do the four virtues come from? Are they Christian or pagan? How do you become courageous? Is self-control old-fashioned? Which virtue is most important?

**Builds on:** *Aristotle* (habit and the mean), *Honour, duty and service* (Cicero), *Right and wrong*. **Read elsewhere:** EN II, VI (*Aristotle*); Cicero I.4–17 (*Honour*).

## Sessions

1. **The hinges.** *Finding:* Plato named four virtues of the good city and soul; the Bible's Wisdom of Solomon lists the same four; the Church called them cardinal. *Read:* *Republic* IV.427d–434c. *Compare:* Wisdom of Solomon 8:7. *Lecture:* the history of the four (Plato, Stoics, Cicero, Ambrose, Aquinas); why 'cardinal'. *Learn:* new `wisdom-8-7`. *Look:* the four virtues personified. *Music:* none.
2. **Wisdom.** *Finding:* Prudence is not caution but practical wisdom: seeing what is really there and choosing well; Aquinas made it the mother of the virtues. *Read:* Aquinas, *Summa* I–II q.61 a.2 (and II–II q.47 a.6, selected). *Compare:* Proverbs 4:7 (point back if used). *Lecture:* point back to phronesis; memory, docility, foresight. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Prudence (often with a mirror and serpent). *Music:* none.
3. **Justice.** *Finding:* Justice is the constant will to give each their due: to God, to neighbour, to the community; Christianity added mercy and the claim of the poor. *Read:* Justinian's definition (point back to *Rome*) with Amos 5:24 (point back to memory) — read Aquinas II–II q.58 a.1. *Compare:* Psalm 82. *Lecture:* commutative and distributive justice; justice and mercy. *Learn:* `amos-5-24`. *Look:* Justice with scales. *Music:* none.
4. **Courage.** *Finding:* Aristotle's courage is facing death in battle nobly; Aquinas said its chief act is not attack but endurance, and the martyr is its perfect form. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* III.6–9; Aquinas II–II q.123 a.6. *Compare:* Joshua 1:9. *Lecture:* courage in daily life; fear and rashness; the martyrs (point back). *Learn:* new `joshua-1-9`. *Look:* St George or a martyr. *Music:* none.
5. **Self-control.** *Finding:* Temperance orders desire for food, drink and sex so that they serve life; it is not hatred of pleasure but its right shape. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* III.10–12. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 9:24–27. *Lecture:* temperance and its parts (chastity, modesty, humility); fasting; addiction today. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Temperance pouring water into wine. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: pagan virtues, splendid vices?** *Finding:* Augustine is often said to have called pagan virtues 'splendid vices'; Luther distrusted virtue as self-improvement; Nietzsche called Christian virtues weakness: can a Christian pursue virtue at all? *Read:* Augustine, *City of God* XIX.25 (virtues without true religion) (Dods). *Compare:* Galatians 5:22–23 (fruit, not achievement). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength from both sides (Lutheran grace against virtue; Nietzschean strength against Christian humility); replies (Aquinas: grace perfects nature; Lewis's Book 3, our own words). *Debate:* Luther; Aquinas; Nietzsche. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **All four together: the verdict.** *Finding:* The virtues need one another (courage without justice is cruelty), and Christianity crowned them with faith, hope and love (the next journey). *Read:* Philippians 4:8. *Compare:* Pieper (our own words). *Lecture:* the four as one table; a practice plan (one virtue a week). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the virtues together. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6.
