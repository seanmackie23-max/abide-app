**Follows:** a first-year paper in world Christianity.

**Frame:** Christianity was never only European: a map in seven places across two thousand years (Ethiopia and India in the first centuries, Ireland, China in 781, the Americas and Asia with the Jesuits, Africa through African evangelists, and today's centre of gravity in the global South).

**Real questions:** Where is Christianity growing? Is Christianity a Western religion? How did Christianity reach Africa, India, China? Who was St Patrick? Who were the Nestorians? Was missionary work colonialism? Who was Samuel Ajayi Crowther? Where do most Christians live today? What is Pentecostalism? What is the future of Christianity?

**Builds on:** *Kings and prophets* (Acts 8, the Ethiopian), *Monks who saved the West* (Irish monks). **Read elsewhere:** Acts 8 (*Kings and prophets*), Acts 2 (several), Revelation 7 (*The end of the story*).

## Sessions

1. **To the ends of the earth.** *Finding:* From the start Christianity went east and south as much as west: Ethiopia, Armenia, Persia and India (the Thomas Christians). *Read:* Isaiah 49:6. *Compare:* Eusebius on Thomas and Thaddeus in Edessa (I.13) (NPNF). *Lecture:* Armenia (301); Ethiopia (Aksum, Ezana); the Church of the East. *Learn:* new `isaiah-49-6`. *Look:* an Ethiopian or Eastern scene (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
2. **Patrick.** *Finding:* A British boy enslaved in Ireland escaped, became a priest, and returned to evangelise his captors: the first great mission beyond the empire. *Read:* Patrick, *Confession* 1, 16–23, 61–62. *Compare:* the *Breastplate* (point back if read in *Monks*). *Lecture:* Patrick's Latin; Ireland's conversion; Irish monks to Europe (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an Irish landscape. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.
3. **The luminous religion.** *Finding:* In 781 Christians in China raised a stone telling the story of the 'luminous religion' brought by Persian monks in 635. *Read:* the Xi'an stele (Legge). *Compare:* Matteo Ricci in Beijing (our own words). *Lecture:* the Church of the East along the Silk Road; the Jesuits and Chinese rites; Japan's hidden Christians. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Chinese landscape (NGA has Chinese works? flag). *Music:* none.
4. **An African bishop.** *Finding:* Samuel Ajayi Crowther, a freed Yoruba slave, became the first African Anglican bishop in 1864 and translated the Bible into Yoruba: Africa was evangelised mainly by Africans. *Read:* Crowther's journals (selected). *Compare:* Acts 8:26–40 (point back). *Lecture:* Sierra Leone; Bible translation (Lamin Sanneh's thesis, our own words); Kimbangu, Harris. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of an African figure (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: the religion of empire?** *Finding:* Missions often came with conquest, cultural contempt and colonial power; critics call Christianity Europe's tool; yet most African and Asian Christians converted after the colonists left. *Read:* Chinua Achebe, *Things Fall Apart* (1958; our own words) as the critique. *Compare:* the Edinburgh 1910 report (the confidence of empire). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Achebe, Ngũgĩ, postcolonial critics; our own words); replies (Sanneh's 'translatability', Andrew Walls, Philip Jenkins, our own words); figures (Africa's Christians from about 9 million in 1900 to over 600 million, cite). *Debate:* Ngũgĩ; Sanneh; Jenkins. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **Fire from heaven.** *Finding:* Pentecostalism, born in Los Angeles in 1906 at Azusa Street, has become the fastest-growing form of Christianity, especially in Latin America, Africa and Asia. *Read:* Acts 2:1–11 (point back) with Joel 2:28–29. *Compare:* William Seymour's *Apostolic Faith* newspaper (1906, PD). *Lecture:* Azusa Street; Brazil, Nigeria, Korea; the house churches of China. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Pentecost (the Koerbecke or another). *Music:* `amazing-grace`.
7. **Every nation: the verdict.** *Finding:* The typical Christian today is not a European man but an African or Latin American woman; Christianity is the world's most diverse faith, and the West is now a mission field. *Read:* Revelation 7:9–10 (point back) with Edinburgh 1910's conclusion. *Compare:* Lamin Sanneh, *Whose Religion Is Christianity?* (our own words). *Lecture:* the numbers (Pew, World Christian Database, cited); persecution today; reverse mission. *Learn:* `revelation-21-4`. *Look:* many peoples gathered. *Music:* `for-all-the-saints`.

**Objection:** session 5, Christianity as empire.

**Traditions:** Orthodox (Oriental Orthodox churches), Catholic, Anglican, Pentecostal side by side.
