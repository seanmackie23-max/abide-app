**Follows:** a paper in philosophy of religion (faith and reason, the wager).

**Frame:** a mathematician who turned his genius on the human condition: the journey goes from Montaigne's doubt and Descartes's certainty, to Pascal's night of fire, to the thinking reed lost between two infinities, the hidden God, the wager, the heart's reasons, and the hall's 'make the case'.

**Real questions:** What is Pascal's wager? Is Pascal's wager a good argument? Who was Pascal? What does 'the heart has its reasons' mean? What did Pascal see on the night of fire? Why is God hidden? What is diversion? What did Descartes argue? Is it rational to believe? Can you choose to believe?

**Builds on:** *Faith and the new science*; *Is there a God?* (the heart's reasons, from William James). **Read elsewhere:** none of Pascal yet (but *Silence*, *Is God coming back?* and *Come and see* also list him: fragments 139, 233 and 277 belong here; the others take other fragments).

## Sessions

1. **What do I know?** *Finding:* Montaigne's motto 'What do I know?' and his Apology for Sebond made scepticism fashionable: the doubt Pascal set out to answer. *Read:* Montaigne, *Essays* II.12 (selected; Florio or Cotton). *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 1:2 (point back). *Lecture:* the Pyrrhonist revival; Montaigne's life and essay form. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar in a tower library. *Music:* none.
2. **I think, therefore I am.** *Finding:* Descartes answered doubt by doubting everything until he found one certainty, his own thinking, and built back up to God. *Read:* Descartes, *Meditations* I and II (Veitch). *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* XI.26 ('if I am deceived, I am') (Dods). *Lecture:* the method; the cogito; the God of the philosophers. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a philosopher at a desk. *Music:* none.
3. **Fire.** *Finding:* On the night of 23 November 1654 Pascal had an experience he sewed into his coat: 'God of Abraham, God of Isaac, God of Jacob, not of the philosophers and scholars'. *Read:* *The Memorial*. *Compare:* Exodus 3:6 (point back). *Lecture:* Pascal's life (calculating machine, vacuum, probability, Port-Royal, the *Provincial Letters*); the Jansenists. *Learn:* new `pascal-memorial-fire`. *Look:* a candle or night scene. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
4. **A thinking reed.** *Finding:* Pascal saw the human being as a reed, the weakest thing in nature, but a thinking reed: lost between the infinitely large and the infinitely small, and running from that thought into diversion. *Read:* *Pensées* 72, 139, 206, 347. *Compare:* Psalm 8:3–4 (point back). *Lecture:* greatness and wretchedness; diversion (screens and boredom today); 'the eternal silence'. *Learn:* new `pascal-thinking-reed`. *Look:* a lone figure under a vast sky. *Music:* none.
5. **The hidden God.** *Finding:* Pascal argued that God is hidden enough to leave room for refusal and visible enough to be found by those who seek: 'truly you are a God who hides yourself'. *Read:* *Pensées* 434 and selected fragments on hiddenness. *Compare:* Isaiah 45:15, with Hebrews 11:6 pointed back to *Faith, hope and love*. *Lecture:* divine hiddenness (J. L. Schellenberg's argument, our own words); Pascal's answer. *Learn:* new `isaiah-45-15`. *Look:* a chiaroscuro scene. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: a bad bet?** *Finding:* The wager says that if God may exist and the stakes are infinite, it is rational to live as if he does; critics say it has the wrong God, the wrong motive and cannot make anyone believe. *Read:* *Pensées* 233 (the wager). *Compare:* William James, 'The Will to Believe' (1896), opening sections (point back to James elsewhere; PD). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: the many-gods objection, Voltaire's mockery, W. K. Clifford's 'Ethics of Belief' (1877, PD); Pascal's reply ('take holy water'); decision theory. *Debate:* Clifford; Pascal; James. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* card players or dice. *Music:* none.
7. **The heart has its reasons: the verdict, and making the case.** *Finding:* Pascal's last word is not the wager but the heart, which knows first principles reason cannot prove, and the mystery of Jesus watching in agony 'until the end of the world'. *Read:* *Pensées* 277 and 553. *Compare:* Mark 14:32–42 (point back to *How to pray*). *Lecture:* reasons of the heart (not feeling but intuition); Pascal's influence (Kierkegaard, Eliot, modern apologetics). **Make the case** (ends Hall VI). *Learn:* new `pascal-heart-has-its-reasons`. *Look:* Christ in Gethsemane. *Music:* `st-matthew-passion`.

**Objection:** session 6, the case against the wager, in Clifford's and Voltaire's terms.

**Traditions:** Jansenism and its condemnation (Catholic), Protestant readings of Pascal side by side.

**Voices:** Peter Kreeft, *Christianity for Modern Pagans*; Graham Tomlin; T. S. Eliot's introduction to the *Pensées* (1931: our own words); J. L. Schellenberg; Alvin Plantinga.
