**Follows:** a paper in ethics and theology (guilt, repentance, forgiveness, reconciliation).

**Frame:** two directions of one gift: being forgiven (guilt, shame, confession, grace) and forgiving (the debt, the unforgivable, reconciliation). Two great novels carry the argument: Hawthorne's hidden guilt and Hugo's bishop who forgives a thief and changes his life.

**Real questions:** How do I deal with guilt? What is the difference between guilt and shame? Can God forgive anything? What is confession? How do I forgive someone who hurt me? Do I have to forgive? Is forgiveness the same as forgetting or excusing? What about the unforgivable? Can I forgive myself? What is reconciliation?

**Builds on:** *Kings and prophets* (David and Psalm 51), *Abraham* (Joseph's forgiveness, Luke 15 compare), *Why the cross?*. **Read elsewhere:** Psalm 51, Luke 15:11–32, Genesis 50.

## Sessions

1. **The weight of guilt.** *Finding:* Hawthorne's minister hides his sin for seven years and it eats him alive: unconfessed guilt destroys. *Read:* *The Scarlet Letter* ch. 17–18. *Compare:* Psalm 32:3–5. *Lecture:* guilt and shame (distinguished); Puritan New England. *Learn:* new `psalm-32-5`. *Look:* a figure in shame. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
2. **Lost and found.** *Finding:* Jesus's parables of the lost sheep and lost coin show God searching for sinners and heaven rejoicing when one returns. *Read:* Luke 15:1–10 (point back to 15:11–32). *Compare:* Luke 7:36–50 (the woman who loved much). *Lecture:* grace; repentance (*metanoia*); the Pharisees' objection. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Good Shepherd. *Music:* `amazing-grace`.
3. **Confession.** *Finding:* The Church gave people ways to confess, from public penance to the confessional to the general confession; they all say guilt can be named and lifted. *Read:* the BCP General Confession and Absolution (1662). *Compare:* James 5:16 and 1 John 1:8–9. *Lecture:* confession in the traditions side by side; the scarlet letter's public shame and the confessional's secrecy. *Learn:* new `1-john-1-9`. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
4. **Seventy times seven.** *Finding:* Jesus told Peter to forgive without limit, and told a parable of a servant forgiven a fortune who would not forgive a pittance. *Read:* Matthew 18:21–35. *Compare:* the Lord's Prayer's 'as we forgive' (point back to memory). *Lecture:* what forgiveness is and is not (not forgetting, excusing or trusting again); anger and justice. *Learn:* `lords`. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
5. **The bishop's candlesticks.** *Finding:* Hugo's bishop forgives Jean Valjean's theft and gives him the candlesticks too: one act of mercy remakes a life. *Read:* *Les Misérables* I.2.10–13 (Hapgood). *Compare:* Romans 12:20–21 (point back). *Lecture:* Hugo; mercy and law (Javert); point back to *Shakespeare*'s mercy. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* candlesticks, or a bishop. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: the unforgivable.** *Finding:* Some say there are wrongs no one should forgive, that forgiving perpetrators betrays victims, and that only victims can forgive; Wiesenthal's dying SS man asks the hardest question. *Read:* Wiesenthal, *The Sunflower* (our own words). *Compare:* Psalm 137:7–9 (point back) and Matthew 12:31–32. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Jean Améry's resentment; Jacques Derrida 'forgiving the unforgivable', our own words); replies (Tutu and the Truth and Reconciliation Commission; the Amish at Nickel Mines, 2006). *Debate:* Améry; Tutu; a Jewish view. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Reconciliation: the verdict.** *Finding:* We can be forgiven because God forgives first, and we can forgive because we have been forgiven; reconciliation is the harder, rarer gift that forgiveness makes possible. *Read:* 2 Corinthians 5:17–20 (point back if read in *Why the cross?*: then Colossians 3:12–15). *Compare:* Genesis 50:20 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* steps to forgive; Mandela (point to Practice). *Learn:* `genesis-50-20`. *Look:* the return of the prodigal (point back if used). *Music:* `it-is-well`.
