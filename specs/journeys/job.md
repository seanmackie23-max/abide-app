**Follows:** a first-year paper on the book of Job (wisdom literature and theodicy).

**Frame:** the trial of God, in Job's own legal language: the charge (an innocent man destroyed), the prosecution's experts (the friends), the defendant's silence and Job's demand for an arbiter, the hope of a redeemer, the summons answered from the whirlwind, and the verdict that surprised everyone: Job, not the friends, spoke rightly.

**Real questions:** What is the book of Job about? Why did God let Satan test Job? Who were Job's friends and why were they wrong? Did Job sin? What does 'I know that my Redeemer lives' mean? What does God say from the whirlwind? Does God answer Job's question? Why does Job get everything back? Is Job a true story? What does Job teach about suffering?

**Builds on:** *Bearing suffering* (Job 1–3 and 38, read; the whole problem of suffering), *Psalms and wisdom*. **Read elsewhere:** Job 1–3, 38 (*Bearing suffering*).

## Sessions

1. **The wager in heaven.** *Finding:* Job's prologue (point back) sets a question deeper than 'why suffering': is there such a thing as goodness for nothing? *Read:* point back to Job 1–3; read Job 1:9 ('does Job fear God for nothing?') with Chesterton's Introduction. *Compare:* Gregory the Great, *Moralia* Preface 1–8. *Lecture:* date, author, the Satan (the accuser, not the devil yet); the poem's structure. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Job on the dunghill. *Music:* none.
2. **The friends' theology.** *Finding:* Job's friends defend God by insisting suffering must be punishment: good theology, wrongly applied. *Read:* Job 4:1–21 and 8:1–7. *Compare:* Proverbs 3:33 (point back) and John 9:1–3 ('neither this man sinned'). *Lecture:* retribution theology; how comforters harm. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Job and his friends. *Music:* none.
3. **No one to arbitrate.** *Finding:* Job wants to take God to court but knows there is no arbiter between them who could lay a hand on both. *Read:* Job 9:1–35. *Compare:* 1 Timothy 2:5. *Lecture:* Job's legal language; protest as faith. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
4. **My Redeemer lives.** *Finding:* At his lowest Job says he knows his redeemer (*go'el*) lives and that he will see God: Christians and Jews have read it differently. *Read:* Job 19:1–29. *Compare:* Handel's aria text (point to music). *Lecture:* *go'el*; the Hebrew difficulties; the Christian reading. *Learn:* new `job-19-25`. *Look:* none. *Music:* `messiah`.
5. **Where is wisdom found?** *Finding:* In the middle of the argument a poem says wisdom cannot be dug out like silver: 'the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom'. *Read:* Job 28. *Compare:* Proverbs 8 (point back). *Lecture:* Job 28's place; mining in the ancient world. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a mountain or mine. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: God owes the innocent an answer.** *Finding:* Job's last speech demands that God answer in writing; readers from Jung to modern critics say God's whirlwind answer is bullying power that never addresses the question. *Read:* Job 31:35–40 and 40:1–14. *Compare:* Jung, *Answer to Job* (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: MacLeish's *J.B.*, Jung, Elie Wiesel's trial of God (our own words); replies (Gregory; Chesterton: God answers with riddles because Job is the riddle). *Debate:* Jung; Chesterton; a Jewish reading. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Blake's Job (if NGA). *Music:* none.
7. **You have spoken rightly: the verdict.** *Finding:* God rebukes the friends and says Job spoke rightly; the book's answer is not an explanation but a meeting, and permission to protest. *Read:* Job 42:1–17. *Compare:* Gregory, *Moralia* on 42 (selected). *Lecture:* 'repent in dust and ashes' (translation question); the restoration's problem; what Job teaches the grieving. *Learn:* `milton-on-his-blindness`. *Look:* Job restored. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6.

**Traditions:** Jewish (the Talmud's 'Job never existed' opinion), patristic (Gregory's moral reading), Reformed (Calvin's sermons), modern side by side.
