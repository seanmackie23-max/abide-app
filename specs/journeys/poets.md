**Follows:** a first-year English literature paper on seventeenth-century religious poetry.

**Frame:** the great century of English religious poetry as five voices speaking to God: Donne demands, Herbert argues, Vaughan remembers, Milton justifies, Bunyan walks. Each session pairs a poem with the psalm or passage it answers.

**Real questions:** Who were the metaphysical poets? What is a metaphysical poem? Who was John Donne? Who was George Herbert? What is Paradise Lost about? Is Satan the hero of Paradise Lost? What is The Pilgrim's Progress? Can poetry be prayer? Why was the seventeenth century so religious? What poems should I learn?

**Builds on:** *Shakespeare*; many memory pieces (`donne-*`, `herbert-the-elixir`, `love3`, `vaughan-i-saw-eternity`, `milton-on-his-blindness`). **Read elsewhere:** Paradise Lost I.1–26 (*In the beginning*), XII.315–371 (*The whole story*); Bunyan's river (*Death and hope*); 'Love (III)' (memory and Worth remembering).

## Sessions

1. **Batter my heart.** *Finding:* Donne, a rake turned Dean of St Paul's, wrote poems that argue with God in the language of love and violence. *Read:* Holy Sonnet XIV and 'A Hymn to God the Father'. *Compare:* Psalm 51 (point back to memory) or Jeremiah 20:7. *Lecture:* metaphysical wit (Johnson's 'heterogeneous ideas yoked by violence together', PD); Donne's life and sermons. *Learn:* `donne-death-be-not-proud`. *Look:* Donne (NGA: flag) or a deathbed. *Music:* none.
2. **The collar.** *Finding:* Herbert's poems are a record of a soul rebelling and being called back: 'Methoughts I heard one calling, Child!' *Read:* 'The Collar' and 'Prayer (I)'. *Compare:* Psalm 42. *Lecture:* Herbert at Bemerton; *The Temple* (1633); the shape poems; why Herbert is loved. *Learn:* `herbert-the-elixir` / `love3`. *Look:* a country church. *Music:* none.
3. **The pulley.** *Finding:* Herbert imagines God giving humanity every gift except rest, so that restlessness would toss us back to him: Augustine's restless heart as a fable. *Read:* 'The Pulley'. *Compare:* Augustine (memory, point back). *Lecture:* Herbert's theology of grace; Simone Weil and Herbert (point to Worth remembering). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
4. **Angel infancy.** *Finding:* Vaughan, a Welsh doctor, looked back to a childhood when he 'shined in my angel infancy' and found a country beyond the stars. *Read:* 'The Retreat' and 'Peace'. *Compare:* Matthew 18:1–4. *Lecture:* Vaughan and Herbert's influence; Traherne's *Centuries* (rediscovered 1896); Wordsworth's later Intimations ode. *Learn:* `vaughan-i-saw-eternity`. *Look:* a child in a landscape (Cole's Voyage of Life is used: another). *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: is Satan the hero?** *Finding:* Blake said Milton was 'of the Devil's party without knowing it'; Satan's speeches are the most thrilling in the poem, and many readers have taken his side against God. *Read:* *Paradise Lost* IV.32–113 (Satan on Niphates: 'Myself am Hell'). *Compare:* Blake, *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (PD). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: the Romantics (Blake, Shelley), William Empson's *Milton's God* (our own words: God as tyrant); C. S. Lewis's *Preface to Paradise Lost* reply (our own words). *Debate:* Blake; Empson; Lewis. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a fallen angel or Satan (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
6. **Justify the ways of God.** *Finding:* Milton ends his epic not in triumph but with Adam and Eve walking out of Eden hand in hand, with Providence their guide. *Read:* *Paradise Lost* XII.552–649. *Compare:* Genesis 3:23–24 (point back). *Lecture:* Milton's life (the republic, blindness, point to memory); free will in the poem. *Learn:* `milton-on-his-blindness`. *Look:* the Expulsion from Eden (several used: another). *Music:* none.
7. **The pilgrim's road: the verdict.** *Finding:* Bunyan, a tinker in prison, wrote the most-read Christian book after the Bible: the whole life of faith as a journey with burdens, friends and a cross. *Read:* *The Pilgrim's Progress*, the Slough of Despond and the Cross. *Compare:* Hebrews 12:1–2. *Lecture:* Bunyan's life; allegory; what poetry can say that prose cannot (a table of the five poets). **Make the case** is in *Pascal and doubt* (ends Hall VI). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a pilgrim on a road. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision` (Bunyan's 'He who would valiant be' if added).

**Objection:** session 5, Milton's God and Satan the hero.

**Traditions:** Anglican (Donne, Herbert), Puritan (Milton, Bunyan), Catholic (Southwell, Crashaw) side by side.

**Voices:** Helen Gardner; C. S. Lewis; William Empson; T. S. Eliot on the metaphysicals (1921: PD essay, may be quoted); John Drury, *Music at Midnight*.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Can poetry be prayer?'; 'Is Satan the hero?'; 'Herbert's art of simplicity'.
