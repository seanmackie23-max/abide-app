**Follows:** a first-year paper on early medieval monasticism and the transmission of learning.

**Frame:** a chain of hands across five centuries: the desert (Antony, the fathers), the rule (Benedict), the islands (Columba, Iona, Lindisfarne), the scriptorium (Bede at Jarrow), and the books that survived because monks copied them. At each link, what was saved and how.

**Real questions:** What did monks do all day? Why did people become monks? Who was St Benedict? What is the Rule of St Benedict? How did monks save the classics? Who were the Irish monks? Who was Bede? What is an illuminated manuscript? Were monasteries rich and corrupt? Do monks still exist?

**Builds on:** *The fall of Rome* (Gregory's life of Benedict, Boethius). **Read elsewhere:** Benedict on lectio (*How to read the Bible*); Gregory, *Dialogues* II (*Fall of Rome*).

## Sessions

1. **Into the desert.** *Finding:* Monasticism began in Egypt around 270 when Antony heard 'go, sell all you have' and walked into the desert; thousands followed. *Read:* Athanasius, *Life of Antony* 2–4. *Compare:* sayings of the desert fathers (Budge), a short selection. *Lecture:* hermits and communities (Pachomius); why the desert; Cassian brings it west. *Learn:* new `matthew-19-21`. *Look:* St Anthony Abbot (NGA has several; the Osservanza is used). *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
2. **A school of the Lord's service.** *Finding:* Benedict's Rule is short, moderate and practical: a family under an abbot, praying, working and reading at set hours. *Read:* *Rule*, Prologue 1–50 and chapter 4. *Compare:* Psalm 119:164. *Lecture:* the daily timetable (the hours: point back to *Time made holy*); stability, obedience, conversion of life. *Learn:* new `benedict-listen` (Prologue 1). *Look:* St Benedict. *Music:* `salve-regina`.
3. **Every guest as Christ.** *Finding:* Benedictine houses became inns, hospitals and schools because the Rule told monks to receive every guest as Christ. *Read:* *Rule* 53. *Compare:* Matthew 25:35 (point back) and Hebrews 13:2. *Lecture:* hospitality; the monastery as the medieval welfare system; Cluny. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a monk welcoming pilgrims, or the supper at Emmaus. *Music:* `ubi-caritas`.
4. **The island saints.** *Finding:* Irish and Scottish monks, from Columba's Iona (563) to Aidan's Lindisfarne, kept learning alive at the edge of the world and carried it back to Europe. *Read:* Adomnán, *Life of Columba* III.23 (his last days). *Compare:* *St Patrick's Breastplate* (point to Hall VII's world church if Patrick is read there; else read here, tr. Kuno Meyer). *Lecture:* Irish monasticism; the Book of Kells; Columbanus; Thomas Cahill's thesis (*How the Irish Saved Civilization*, our own words) and its critics. *Learn:* `vision` (Be thou my vision, if a memory exists: `vision`). *Look:* an Irish or northern landscape, or a manuscript. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.
5. **The scriptorium.** *Finding:* Nearly every classical Latin text we have survived because monks copied it; Bede, in a monastery in Northumbria, wrote the first history of the English. *Read:* Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* IV.24 (Caedmon, the cowherd who sang). *Compare:* Cuthbert's letter on Bede's death (735). *Lecture:* copying, parchment, Carolingian minuscule; Alcuin and Charlemagne; the Venerable Bede's AD dating. *Learn:* new Caedmon's hymn (Sellar). *Look:* a monk writing, or an illuminated page. *Music:* `hildegard`.
6. **The case against: rich, idle and corrupt?** *Finding:* By the later Middle Ages many monasteries were rich and slack, and critics from Bernard to Erasmus to Henry VIII's commissioners said so; reform movements kept arising from within. *Read:* Bernard of Clairvaux, *Apologia to William of St Thierry* 28–29 (against luxury in Cluniac art) (tr. in Coulton, *Life in the Middle Ages* 1928–30: confirm). *Compare:* Chaucer's Monk, *General Prologue* 165–207. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: wealth, idleness, the dissolution's charges (and how far they were true: Eamon Duffy, our own words); reform waves (Cluny, Cîteaux, the friars). *Debate:* Bernard; Chaucer; Duffy. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a monk at a feast, or Chaucer's pilgrims. *Music:* none.
7. **Still praying: the verdict.** *Finding:* Monks saved the West not by a plan but by a rule of life repeated daily; communities still keep it today. *Read:* Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* II.13 (the sparrow) if not used; else Benedict, *Rule* 72 (the good zeal). *Compare:* Psalm 133. *Lecture:* monasticism today (Taizé, Iona, Benedictines, Orthodox Athos); the Benedict Option recalled; how a lay person can keep a rule (point to Practice). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a monastery or cloister. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.

**Objection:** session 6, monastic wealth and corruption.

**Traditions:** monasticism in Orthodoxy (Athos), Catholicism, Anglicanism (revived), and its rejection by most Protestants.

**Voices:** Eamon Duffy; Diarmaid MacCulloch; Thomas Cahill; Christopher Dawson; Rod Dreher.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Did monks save classical learning?'; 'Why did monasticism appeal?'; 'Benedict's moderation'.
