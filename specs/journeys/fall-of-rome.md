**Follows:** a first-year paper on late antiquity.

**Frame:** a city that 'took the whole world was itself taken' (Jerome): what people said when Rome fell (Jerome, Augustine), who blamed whom (pagans and Christians), what one man wrote in prison while waiting to die (Boethius), what a few monks quietly started (Benedict), and Gibbon's verdict. What survives a fall?

**Real questions:** Why did the Roman Empire fall? When did Rome fall? Did Christianity cause the fall of Rome? What were the Dark Ages? Who sacked Rome? What is the Consolation of Philosophy? Who was Boethius? Who was Benedict? Is the West falling now? What is the 'Benedict Option'?

**Builds on:** *Augustine*; *Bearing suffering* (Boethius II, read). **Read elsewhere:** Boethius II (*Bearing suffering*); the memory `boethius-fortunes-wheel`. Benedict's *Rule* is read in *Monks who saved the West* (Hall V): here only Gregory's life of Benedict.

## Sessions

1. **The city that took the world.** *Finding:* When Alaric's Goths sacked Rome in 410, the shock was felt across the empire: Jerome in Bethlehem could hardly speak. *Read:* Jerome, *Letter* 127.12. *Compare:* Lamentations 1:1–6. *Lecture:* the western empire's crisis; Goths, Huns, Vandals; dates (410, 455, 476). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a ruined city, or Rome in ruins (Panini, Robert). *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
2. **Who is to blame?** *Finding:* Pagans blamed the Christians for abandoning the gods; Augustine answered that Rome had suffered worse under the gods and that Christians had sheltered pagans in their churches. *Read:* *City of God* I.1–7. *Compare:* Symmachus's plea for the Altar of Victory (384) and Ambrose's reply (Letters 17–18, NPNF II.10). *Lecture:* the Altar of Victory dispute; Orosius's history; Salvian's charge that the Romans deserved it (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a pagan altar or Roman ruin. *Music:* none.
3. **Philosophy comes to a prisoner.** *Finding:* Boethius, a Roman senator condemned by a Gothic king, wrote in prison that Lady Philosophy came to him to cure his self-pity. *Read:* *Consolation* I, prose 1–4. *Compare:* Psalm 73:1–17. *Lecture:* Boethius (c. 480–524), Theodoric, Ravenna; the book's influence (Alfred, Chaucer, Elizabeth I's translation: point back to Practice). *Learn:* `boethius-fortunes-wheel`. *Look:* a scholar in prison, or Philosophy personified. *Music:* none.
4. **Providence and freedom.** *Finding:* Boethius argued that God sees all time in one present, so foreknowledge does not force our choices: the argument still used today. *Read:* *Consolation* V, prose 6. *Compare:* Psalm 90:4. *Lecture:* eternity and time; foreknowledge; how it shaped Aquinas. *Learn:* `psalm-90`. *Look:* an allegory of time. *Music:* none.
5. **A little rule for beginners.** *Finding:* While the empire fell, a young Roman named Benedict left the city for a cave and founded communities whose rule would carry learning and order through the next centuries. *Read:* Gregory the Great, *Dialogues* II.1–3. *Compare:* Psalm 34:11–14 (quoted in Benedict's Prologue). *Lecture:* Monte Cassino (529); Cassiodorus's Vivarium; Irish monks (point ahead to Hall V as the hall). *Learn:* new `psalm-34-14`. *Look:* St Benedict. *Music:* `hildegard` or `salve-regina`.
6. **The case against: did Christianity cause the fall?** *Finding:* Gibbon argued that Christianity weakened Rome by turning minds from public service to the next world; most historians now give other causes, and the eastern, more Christian empire survived another thousand years. *Read:* Gibbon, ch. XXXVIII, 'General Observations'. *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* V.17–18. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength in Gibbon's own words; the 210 causes; Peter Heather and Bryan Ward-Perkins (our own words); Byzantium. *Debate:* Gibbon; Ward-Perkins; Peter Brown's 'transformation'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a ruin. *Music:* none.
7. **What survives: the verdict.** *Finding:* What survived Rome was what people carried: the faith, the books the monks copied, Roman law and the Latin language, and the habit of building communities that last. *Read:* *Consolation* V recalled; read Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* II.13 (the sparrow in the hall) (Giles/Sellar). *Compare:* Alasdair MacIntyre's 'another, doubtless very different, St Benedict' (*After Virtue*, 1981; our own words, one short quotation allowed). *Lecture:* the 'Dark Ages' debate; the Benedict Option (Rod Dreher, our own words); is the West falling? **Make the case** (ends Hall IV is *The art of persuasion*: not here). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a monastery. *Music:* `locus-iste`.

**Objection:** session 6, Gibbon's charge.

**Traditions:** East and West after 476 side by side (Byzantium, the papacy).
