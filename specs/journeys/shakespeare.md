**Follows:** a first-year English literature paper on Shakespeare.

**Frame:** the plays as a long argument about mercy: mercy demanded and refused (Merchant), justice and hypocrisy (Measure), prayer that cannot rise (Hamlet), forgiveness given by the wronged (Lear), a dead love restored (Winter's Tale), and power laid down (Tempest). Was Shakespeare a Christian, and does it matter?

**Real questions:** Was Shakespeare religious? Was Shakespeare Catholic? What is 'the quality of mercy'? Is The Merchant of Venice antisemitic? What is Measure for Measure about? Why is King Lear so dark? What does the ending of The Tempest mean? How much of the Bible is in Shakespeare? Why is Shakespeare so great?

**Builds on:** *The King James Bible*, *The Reformation*; Practice memory pieces (`shakespeare-tomorrow`, `shakespeare-what-a-piece-of-work`, `julius-caesar-cowards`, `shakespeare-sonnet-116`). **Read elsewhere:** Hamlet II.2 'What a piece of work' (memory), Macbeth V.5 (memory).

## Sessions

1. **The world of the plays.** *Finding:* Shakespeare wrote in a country that had changed its religion three times in his parents' lifetime; the plays are full of the Bible and careful about doctrine. *Read:* Hamlet III.3.36–98 (Claudius cannot pray). *Compare:* Psalm 51:16–17 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* Stratford, the Reformation in England, recusancy; the Bible in Shakespeare (Naseeb Shaheen's count, our own words); 'Was he Catholic?' debate. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of Shakespeare or a theatre. *Music:* none.
2. **The quality of mercy.** *Finding:* Portia's plea says mercy is God's own attribute and the source of earthly power's dignity: the Sermon on the Mount on a Venetian stage. *Read:* *Merchant* IV.1.180–203. *Compare:* Matthew 7:1–2 and the Lord's Prayer's 'forgive us'. *Lecture:* the trial scene; law and mercy; Shakespeare's sources. *Learn:* new `merchant-quality-of-mercy`. *Look:* Portia (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
3. **Hath not a Jew eyes?** *Finding:* The play that contains the most Christian speech also contains Shylock, whose humanity Shakespeare gives a voice; the question of its antisemitism is real. *Read:* *Merchant* III.1.50–69. *Compare:* Leviticus 19:33–34 (point back). *Lecture:* Jews in Elizabethan England (expelled 1290; Rodrigo Lopez); the forced conversion; productions after 1945. *Debate:* antisemitic play or play about antisemitism? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none suitable; avoid caricature. *Music:* none.
4. **Measure for measure.** *Finding:* A puritan deputy condemns a man for fornication and then demands a nun's virginity as the price of mercy: Shakespeare's darkest study of hypocrisy and grace. *Read:* *Measure* II.2.73–79 and II.4. *Compare:* Matthew 7:2 (the title's source) and John 8:1–11. *Lecture:* the play's theology (Isabella, the Duke as Providence); the 'problem play'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a judge or a nun. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: As flies to wanton boys.** *Finding:* *King Lear* seems to show a world without justice or gods ('as flies to wanton boys are we to the gods'); many critics read it as Shakespeare's verdict that the heavens are empty. *Read:* *Lear* IV.1.36–37 and V.3.304–311 (Lear over Cordelia's body). *Compare:* Job 3 (point back). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Jan Kott and the absurdist reading; Dr Johnson unable to bear the ending (PD, may quote); the Christian readings (A. C. Bradley, 1904, PD). *Debate:* Kott; Bradley; a modern critic. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a storm on a heath. *Music:* none.
6. **Forgiveness.** *Finding:* At the heart of the same play is Cordelia forgiving her father ('No cause, no cause'), and in the late plays the wronged forgive and the dead seem to return. *Read:* *Lear* IV.7.26–84 and *Winter's Tale* V.3. *Compare:* Luke 15:20–24 (point back). *Lecture:* the romances; grace and resurrection images; 'It is required you do awake your faith'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the return of the prodigal son. *Music:* none.
7. **I'll drown my book: the verdict.** *Finding:* Prospero chooses 'the rarer action' of virtue over vengeance, gives up his magic and asks the audience's prayers: Shakespeare's last word is mercy asked and given. *Read:* *Tempest* V.1.20–32 and Epilogue. *Compare:* Romans 12:19–21 (point back to *The first Christians*). *Lecture:* was Shakespeare a Christian? the evidence and its limits; why the plays still matter. *Learn:* `shakespeare-sonnet-116`. *Look:* a seascape or island. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 5, Lear's empty heavens.
