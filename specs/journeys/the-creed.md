**Follows:** a first-year paper in Christian doctrine.

**Frame:** the Creed line by line as answers to questions people asked and fought over: one God (the Shema), Father, Son and Spirit (the baptismal formula), 'of one being' (Nicaea against Arius), three persons not three gods (the Cappadocians), one person two natures (Chalcedon), and the Spirit and the Church. The objection: is it an invention of the fourth century?

**Real questions:** What is the Creed? What is the Trinity? Is the Trinity in the Bible? How can God be three and one? What happened at the Council of Nicaea? Who was Arius? Is Jesus God? What is the Holy Spirit? Why do Christians say the Creed? Do Muslims and Jews believe in the same God? What does 'of one being with the Father' mean?

**Builds on:** *Who was Jesus?*, *Why the cross?*, *When Athens met Jerusalem*, *Is there a God?*. **Read elsewhere:** Athanasius 6–10 (*Why the cross?*); John 1 (*Psalms and wisdom*); the creeds are memory pieces.

## Sessions

1. **Hear, O Israel.** *Finding:* Christianity began as Jewish monotheism: one God, maker of all; the Creed never stops saying 'one'. *Read:* Deuteronomy 6:4 (point back) with Isaiah 45:5–7 and 1 Corinthians 8:4–6 (Paul splits the Shema around Jesus). *Compare:* the Qur'an, Sura 112 (Rodwell) as the Muslim challenge. *Lecture:* monotheism; Paul's Christological Shema (Bauckham, our own words). *Learn:* `deuteronomy-6-4`. *Look:* the Holy Trinity (Masaccio is Florence: NGA others). *Music:* `holy-holy-holy`.
2. **In the name.** *Finding:* From the start Christians baptised 'in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit', and worshipped Jesus within a generation. *Read:* 2 Corinthians 13:14, with Matthew 28:19 pointed back to *What happens at church*. *Compare:* Pliny's 'hymn to Christ as to a god' (point back). *Lecture:* early worship of Jesus (Hurtado); the Apostles' Creed's origin in baptism. *Learn:* `apostles-creed`. *Look:* the Baptism of Christ. *Music:* `come-down-o-love-divine`.
3. **Of one being: Nicaea.** *Finding:* When Arius taught that the Son was the first and greatest creature, the Council of Nicaea (325) answered that he is 'of one being with the Father': true God from true God. *Read:* Arius's *Thalia* (in Athanasius, *Orations* I.5) and the Creed of Nicaea. *Compare:* Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 54. *Lecture:* Alexandria; Constantine; *homoousios*; the long aftermath to 381. *Debate:* Arius or Athanasius? *Learn:* `nicene-creed`. *Look:* a council of bishops. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
4. **Three, not three gods.** *Finding:* The Cappadocians explained one being (*ousia*) in three persons (*hypostases*): not three gods, as Peter, James and John are three men, because the three act always as one. *Read:* Gregory of Nyssa, *On 'Not Three Gods'*. *Compare:* the Athanasian Creed's opening (PD). *Lecture:* Basil, Gregory, Gregory; *perichoresis*; analogies and their failures (the shamrock heresy, gently). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Hospitality of Abraham (Rublev's subject; NGA has an Abraham and the angels?). *Music:* none.
5. **Lover, beloved and love.** *Finding:* Augustine found the Trinity's image in the mind (memory, understanding, will) and in love itself: God is love, and love needs a lover, a beloved and the love between them. *Read:* Augustine, *On the Trinity* IX.2 and XV.17. *Compare:* 1 John 4:8, 16 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* the filioque and East–West difference side by side; C. S. Lewis's 'the dance' (Book 4, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Trinity image. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: a fourth-century invention?** *Finding:* Critics say the Trinity is not in the Bible, was imposed by an emperor, makes no sense, and divides Christians from Jews and Muslims; each charge has a reply, and some force. *Read:* Joseph Priestley, *An History of the Corruptions of Christianity* (1782), Part I (PD). *Compare:* Isaac Newton's anti-Trinitarian writings (our own words) and the Qur'an 5:73 (Rodwell). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Unitarians, Jehovah's Witnesses, Muslims, logical incoherence (Dale Tuggy, our own words); replies. *Debate:* Priestley; Athanasius; a modern analytic defender. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **One person, two natures: the verdict.** *Finding:* Chalcedon (451) said Christ is one person, truly God and truly human, 'without confusion, change, division or separation'; the Creed is less an explanation than a fence around a mystery Christians worship. *Read:* the Chalcedonian Definition. *Compare:* Philippians 2:5–11 (point back). *Lecture:* the churches that did not accept Chalcedon (Oriental Orthodox, side by side); the Creed today. *Learn:* `nicene-creed`. *Look:* Christ Pantocrator or the Transfiguration. *Music:* `mass-in-b-minor` (the Credo).
