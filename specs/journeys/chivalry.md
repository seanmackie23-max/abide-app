**Follows:** a paper on medieval culture and the ethics of war.

**Frame:** how the Church tried to tame the warrior: the raw warrior (Roland), the Church's limits on violence (the Peace and Truce of God), the knight's vows (Llull), the ideal (Chaucer's 'verray parfit gentil knight'), the dream (Arthur and Lancelot), and the question for today: what is strength for?

**Real questions:** What is chivalry? What did knights vow? Were knights really chivalrous? What is the Song of Roland? What was the code of chivalry? What is the Peace of God? Who was King Arthur? Is chivalry sexist? Is chivalry dead? What does it mean to be a gentleman (point back)?

**Builds on:** *Homer* (the warrior code), *Honour, duty and service*. **Read elsewhere:** Micah 6:8 (memory).

## Sessions

1. **Roland's horn.** *Finding:* The earliest French epic shows the warrior at his most magnificent and most reckless: Roland would rather die than call for help. *Read:* *Song of Roland* laisses 79–92 and 168–176. *Compare:* Iliad IX recalled (point back). *Lecture:* Roncevaux (778); the poem (c. 1100); *prouesse* and *sagesse* ('Roland is brave, Oliver is wise'). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a knight in armour. *Music:* none.
2. **Taming the warrior.** *Finding:* The Church tried to limit violence by declaring the Peace of God (no attacks on clergy, peasants, the poor) and the Truce of God (no fighting on holy days). *Read:* Charroux (989) and the Truce of God (1083). *Compare:* Isaiah 2:4 (point back). *Lecture:* violence after the Carolingians; relics and oaths; how far it worked. *Learn:* `isaiah-2-4`. *Look:* St Martin dividing his cloak (El Greco used: another), or St George. *Music:* none.
3. **The knight's vows.** *Finding:* Llull's handbook said the knight exists to defend the Church, the weak and justice: strength bound to service. *Read:* Llull, *Order of Chivalry* I–II (Caxton). *Compare:* Ephesians 6:10–18. *Lecture:* dubbing; the vigil; the knight's arms explained as virtues; orders of knighthood. *Learn:* new `ephesians-6-10`. *Look:* St George or a knight kneeling. *Music:* none.
4. **A very perfect gentle knight.** *Finding:* Chaucer's Knight, a veteran of real crusades, is meek as a maid, never speaks rudely and goes on pilgrimage in his stained tunic: the ideal at its gentlest. *Read:* Chaucer, *General Prologue* 43–78. *Compare:* the Squire (79–100). *Lecture:* courtly love; ladies, troubadours; Terry Jones's 'mercenary' reading (our own words) and its critics. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Chaucer's pilgrims. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: brutality in a velvet glove.** *Finding:* Knights also raped, looted and massacred; historians argue chivalry was class privilege and a licence for violence dressed up in religion. *Read:* Froissart's *Chronicles* on the sack of Limoges (1370) (Berners/Macaulay 1895). *Compare:* Bernard's *In Praise of the New Knighthood* on killing for Christ (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Richard Kaeuper, *Chivalry and Violence* (our own words); chivalry and women; the Black Prince. *Debate:* Kaeuper; Maurice Keen; Llull. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a battle scene. *Music:* none.
6. **The fellowship broken.** *Finding:* Malory's Arthur ends in the fellowship's ruin, and the finest tribute is Ector's lament over Lancelot: the ideal kept alive by its failure. *Read:* Malory, *Morte d'Arthur* XXI.13. *Compare:* Tennyson, 'Morte d'Arthur' closing lines ('the old order changeth') (point to Practice's Tennyson if used). *Lecture:* the Arthurian legend; the Grail; Victorian chivalry (Mark Girouard, our own words). *Learn:* new `malory-ector-lament`. *Look:* an Arthurian subject (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
7. **What is strength for? The verdict.** *Finding:* Chivalry's lasting gift is the idea that strength must serve the weak; it survives in the soldier, the doctor and the ordinary person who protects. *Read:* John 15:13. *Compare:* C. S. Lewis, 'The Necessity of Chivalry' (1940; our own words). *Lecture:* chivalry today: military ethics, the Geneva Conventions (Henri Dunant), everyday courtesy. *Learn:* new `john-15-13`. *Look:* St George and the dragon. *Music:* `tallis-fantasia`.

**Objection:** session 5, chivalry as violence and privilege.
