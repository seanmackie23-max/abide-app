**Follows:** a paper in the history and philosophy of science (the Scientific Revolution).

**Frame:** the two books, Scripture and nature, that the founders of modern science believed God wrote: the journey follows the new astronomy (Copernicus, Kepler, Galileo), the trial that became the great myth, the new method (Bacon, Boyle), Newton's laws and his God, and asks whether science and faith went to war.

**Real questions:** Did the Church oppose science? Why was Galileo put on trial? Was Galileo tortured? Did Copernicus believe in God? Was Newton a Christian? What is the 'conflict thesis'? Did the Church believe the earth was flat? What did Bacon mean by the two books? Were the first scientists Christians? Do science and religion conflict today?

**Builds on:** *Number and nature* (Greek science, the conflict thesis outlined), *Reason and revelation*. **Read elsewhere:** Psalm 19 (*Is there a God?*), Genesis 1 (several).

## Sessions

1. **The sun at the centre.** *Finding:* Copernicus, a Polish canon, put the sun at the centre in 1543 and dedicated the book to the Pope; the first opposition came from Protestants as well as Catholics. *Read:* Copernicus's preface (our own words, short quotation). *Compare:* Joshua 10:12–14 (the sun stands still). *Lecture:* Ptolemy; Copernicus; Luther's table-talk remark (doubtful); Osiander's preface. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an astronomer. *Music:* none.
2. **Thinking God's thoughts.** *Finding:* Kepler, a devout Lutheran, believed the universe was built on geometry because God is a geometer, and found the elliptical orbits by refusing to ignore an eight-minute error. *Read:* Kepler (our own words; the 1598 letter's phrase). *Compare:* Wisdom 11:20 (point back). *Lecture:* Tycho Brahe; Kepler's three laws; his mother's witch trial. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the night sky. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
3. **Two books.** *Finding:* Galileo argued that the Bible teaches 'how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go', and that the two books cannot contradict. *Read:* Galileo, *Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina* (Salusbury). *Compare:* Bacon, *Advancement of Learning* I.1.3. *Lecture:* the telescope (1609–10); Baronius's saying; Augustine's rule on Genesis (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scientist with a telescope. *Music:* none.
4. **The trial.** *Finding:* In 1633 Galileo was tried, forced to abjure and kept under house arrest: a real injustice, whose causes were politics, personality and theology together. *Read:* Galileo's abjuration. *Compare:* the 1616 decree on Copernicus (our own words). *Lecture:* Urban VIII and the *Dialogue*; what happened and what did not (no torture, no 'eppur si muove' in the record); John Paul II's statement (1992). *Debate:* why was Galileo condemned? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a tribunal. *Music:* none.
5. **The new method.** *Finding:* Bacon and the founders of the Royal Society saw experiment as a religious duty: studying God's works to relieve human suffering. *Read:* Boyle, *The Christian Virtuoso* (selected). *Compare:* Genesis 1:28 (point back) and Psalm 111:2 (point back to *Cathedrals and universities*). *Lecture:* the Royal Society (1660); Boyle's lectures; Puritanism and science (Merton thesis, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an experiment (Joseph Wright of Derby is not NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: war after all?** *Finding:* The 'warfare' story (Draper, White) says religion always fought science; historians now reject the simple version, but real conflicts (Galileo, Bruno, later Darwin) and real tensions remain. *Read:* Andrew Dickson White, *A History of the Warfare of Science with Theology* (1896), ch. 1 (PD). *Compare:* Bruno's execution (1600), in our own words. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength in White's terms; replies (Ronald Numbers, *Galileo Goes to Jail*; Peter Harrison; our own words); where tension is real (miracles, the soul). *Debate:* White; Numbers; a modern scientist (Dawkins, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **This most beautiful system: the verdict.** *Finding:* Newton ended his *Principia* by saying that 'this most beautiful system' could only come from an intelligent and powerful Being; science and faith did not go to war, but they did learn to talk differently. *Read:* Newton, General Scholium (Motte). *Compare:* Psalm 19:1–6 (point back). *Lecture:* Newton's faith (unorthodox, anti-Trinitarian); Leibniz's 'clockmaker' complaint; the god of the gaps; science and faith today (Lemaître, Collins, Polkinghorne in our own words; point ahead to *Darwin and design* as the hall). **Make the case** is in *Pascal and doubt*. *Learn:* `psalm-19-1`. *Look:* a starry sky. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.

**Objection:** session 6, the warfare thesis.
