**Follows:** a first-year paper on medieval learning and institutions.

**Frame:** from the cathedral school to the university: a teacher (Abelard) who drew crowds to Paris, the guild of masters and students that grew around such teachers, what they learned (the seven liberal arts and the three higher faculties), how they lived, and what we inherited (degrees, gowns, lectures, the freedom to argue).

**Real questions:** When were universities invented? What was the first university? Why did the Church found universities? What did medieval students study? What are the liberal arts? Who was Abelard and what happened with Heloise? Was the Church against learning? Where do degrees and gowns come from? What does 'standing on the shoulders of giants' mean?

**Builds on:** *Reason and revelation* (Abelard's method); *Light, stone and glass* (the cathedrals as buildings). **Read elsewhere:** cathedral architecture (*Light, stone and glass*).

## Sessions

1. **The schools of Paris.** *Finding:* In the twelfth century students flocked to teachers in cathedral towns; Abelard's brilliance made Paris the centre of learning. *Read:* Abelard, *Story of My Misfortunes* 1–2. *Compare:* Proverbs 9:1–10. *Lecture:* cathedral schools (Chartres, Laon, Paris); the twelfth-century renaissance. *Learn:* new `proverbs-9-10`. *Look:* a medieval teacher with students. *Music:* `palestrina-marcelli`.
2. **Abelard and Heloise.** *Finding:* Abelard's love affair with his brilliant student Heloise ended in his castration and their entering religion: their letters are among the great documents of the age. *Read:* *Story of My Misfortunes* 6 (Bellows). *Compare:* Heloise's first letter to Abelard (tr. Scott Moncrieff, 1925: Scott Moncrieff died 1930: qualifies). *Lecture:* Heloise as scholar; the letters' authenticity debate. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* lovers or a convent. *Music:* none.
3. **Shoulders of giants.** *Finding:* The medieval scholar thought himself a dwarf on the shoulders of the ancients: seeing further because carried by them. *Read:* John of Salisbury on Bernard of Chartres (short quotation and our own words). *Compare:* Newton's letter to Hooke (1676). *Lecture:* the liberal arts (trivium, quadrivium); the canon of authorities; Chartres. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the liberal arts personified. *Music:* none.
4. **The guild of masters and students.** *Finding:* The university (*universitas*) was a guild that won the right to govern itself from pope and king: Bologna (1088), Paris (c. 1150–1200), Oxford (c. 1167). *Read:* the Paris statutes of 1215 (Robert de Courçon) (PD translation: Munro, *Translations and Reprints* 1898). *Compare:* Chaucer's Clerk of Oxford. *Lecture:* faculties (arts, theology, law, medicine); degrees, gowns, the lecture and the disputation; colleges. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar in a gown. *Music:* none.
5. **What they learned.** *Finding:* Medieval students studied logic, Aristotle, mathematics and astronomy before theology; the curriculum joined Athens and Jerusalem. *Read:* Hugh of St Victor on learning everything (our own words, one short quotation). *Compare:* Grosseteste or Roger Bacon on experiment (*Opus Majus* VI, Burke 1928: Robert Belle Burke died?; confirm). *Lecture:* the arts course; Oxford science (Grosseteste, Bacon, the Merton calculators); Buridan's impetus. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an astronomer or a globe. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: a Church that controlled thought?** *Finding:* Universities had censorship, condemnations and heresy trials; they also had more freedom to argue than almost any institution in the ancient world, protected by the Church. *Read:* the Condemnation of 1277, selected articles (Munro/Thorndike; confirm). *Compare:* the trial of Abelard at Sens (1141), in our own words. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: the Inquisition and censorship (point ahead to Galileo in Hall VI as the hall); academic freedom's medieval roots (Edward Grant, our own words). *Debate:* a modern secular critic; Grant; Hastings Rashdall. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a council or tribunal. *Music:* none.
7. **What we inherited: the verdict.** *Finding:* The university is the Middle Ages' greatest gift that is still working: a free community of teachers and students seeking truth together. *Read:* Newman, *The Idea of a University*, Preface or Discourse V.1 ('knowledge its own end'). *Compare:* Psalm 111:2. *Lecture:* from Paris to Harvard (1636, 'Veritas Christo et Ecclesiae'); the university today. *Learn:* new `psalm-111-2` (carved over the Cavendish Laboratory door, Cambridge). *Look:* a library or college. *Music:* `locus-iste`.

**Objection:** session 6, censorship and control.
