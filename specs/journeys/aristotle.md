**Follows:** a first-year philosophy paper on Aristotle's ethics and metaphysics.

**Frame:** one question, asked of everything: what is it for? Aristotle asks it of an acorn (nature), of a person (ethics), of the city (politics), of the soul (psychology) and of the universe (the first mover); the journey follows the question outward, faces his worst answer, and ends where Aquinas took him.

**Real questions:** Who was Aristotle? What are the four causes? What is the golden mean? What is virtue? What is practical wisdom? What is happiness for Aristotle? What did he mean by 'man is a political animal'? Did Aristotle believe in God? Did he defend slavery? How did Aristotle come back to the West? How is Aristotle different from Plato?

**Builds on:** *Plato*; *A good life* (EN I.7, the function argument, already read); *Human dignity* (Politics I.4–6 on natural slaves, already read). **Read elsewhere:** those two passages; Politics III.7, V.10 (*Kings*), III.11, IV.11 (*Democracy*); Poetics (*Tragedy*); Metaphysics I.3–5 (*Number and nature*), I.9 (*Plato*).

## Sessions

1. **What is it for?** *Finding:* Aristotle explained things by four causes, above all the final cause, what a thing is for: nature does nothing in vain. *Read:* *Physics* II.3 and II.8 (translation: see Flags). *Compare:* Romans 1:20. *Lecture:* Aristotle's life (Stagira, the Academy, Alexander, the Lyceum); the four causes; biology. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a naturalist, or an acorn and oak. *Music:* none.
2. **You become what you repeat.** *Finding:* We become good as we become builders, by practice: virtue is a settled habit of choosing the mean between too much and too little. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* II.1–6 (Peters). *Compare:* William James on habit (point back to *A good life*). *Lecture:* the mean table (courage, temperance, generosity...); habituation and education. *Learn:* `by-doing-just-actions`. *Look:* a craftsman at work. *Music:* none.
3. **Practical wisdom.** *Finding:* The virtues need *phronesis*, practical wisdom: seeing what this situation asks, which no rule book can give. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.5 and VI.13. *Compare:* Proverbs 4:5–9. *Lecture:* phronesis and rules; the unity of the virtues; MacIntyre's revival (our own words). *Learn:* `one-swallow`. *Look:* a judge or counsellor. *Music:* none.
4. **The political animal.** *Finding:* For Aristotle a human being can flourish only in a community: whoever lives without a city is a beast or a god. *Read:* *Politics* I.2. *Compare:* Genesis 2:18 (point back). *Lecture:* friendship and the city (point ahead to Hall IX's love journey as the hall); the household; citizenship. *Learn:* `donne-no-man-is-an-island`. *Look:* a market or civic scene. *Music:* none.
5. **The soul and the unmoved mover.** *Finding:* The soul is the form of a living body, not a prisoner in it; and the universe's motion points to an unmoved mover, thought thinking itself. *Read:* *De Anima* II.1 and *Metaphysics* XII.7 and 9. *Compare:* Aquinas, *Summa contra Gentiles* I.13 (the argument from motion, built on Aristotle). *Lecture:* hylomorphism; the prime mover is not the God of Abraham (it does not know or love the world); the Five Ways (point ahead to Hall V as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the heavens, or an allegory of the spheres. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
6. **The case against: natural slaves and lesser women.** *Finding:* The philosopher of the good life argued that some people are slaves by nature and women are deficient men: his greatest errors, built on his own method, which later Christians had to break. *Read:* *Politics* I.12–13 (women and slaves in the household) with point back to I.4–6 in *Human dignity*. *Compare:* Las Casas against Sepúlveda at Valladolid (point ahead to Hall VI as the hall; our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: if Aristotle's teleology yields slavery, is it rotten at the root? Replies: Aquinas's revisions; the modern reconstruction. *Debate:* Aristotle; his Christian critics; a modern defender of teleology. *Learn:* `within-our-power` (Epictetus, born a slave). *Look:* a servant or labourer painted with dignity. *Music:* none.
7. **Happiness is contemplation: the verdict.** *Finding:* Aristotle's highest happiness is contemplation, the most divine activity; Aquinas said he was right and incomplete, because the end is not to think about God but to see him. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* X.7–8. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 13:12 and Aquinas, *Summa* I–II q.3 a.8 (perfect happiness is the vision of God). *Lecture:* Aristotle's return through Avicenna, Averroes and Maimonides to Paris (point ahead to Hall V as the hall); 'the Philosopher'; Aristotle and Plato compared (a table). *Learn:* new `1-corinthians-13-12`. *Look:* a scholar contemplating, or Aristotle with a bust of Homer (Rembrandt's is at the Met, not NGA: check). *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.

**Objection:** session 6, natural slavery and women, at full strength as a charge against his whole method.

**Traditions:** Aristotle in Catholic (Thomist), Orthodox (more cautious), Protestant (Luther's hostility, Melanchthon's use) thought, side by side.

**Voices:** Alasdair MacIntyre, *After Virtue*; Martha Nussbaum; Jonathan Barnes; Edith Hall, *Aristotle's Way*.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'What is the function argument and does it work?'; 'Can teleology survive Darwin?' (point ahead to Hall VII as the hall); 'Plato or Aristotle?'.
