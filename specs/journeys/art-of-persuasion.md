**Follows:** a paper in rhetoric and argument.

**Frame:** the oldest quarrel in education: is persuasion a craft of manipulation (Plato's charge) or the art of making truth heard (Aristotle, Cicero, Augustine)? The journey learns the tools (ethos, pathos, logos; to teach, delight and move), watches them used by Paul and Lincoln, and ends with the hall's 'make the case'.

**Real questions:** What is rhetoric? What are ethos, pathos and logos? Is persuasion manipulation? How do I win an argument? How do I talk about my faith without being preachy? What made Lincoln a great speaker? What is apologetics? How should I disagree well? What is a fallacy?

**Builds on:** *Socrates*, *Democracy* (the assembly), *Rome* (Cicero); Practice's Worth remembering (Gettysburg). **Read elsewhere:** the Gettysburg Address (memory and Worth remembering).

## Sessions

1. **Flattery or art?** *Finding:* Plato charged that rhetoric is flattery, making the worse case seem better; every defender of persuasion must answer him first. *Read:* Plato, *Gorgias* 452d–466a. *Compare:* Proverbs 26:24–28. *Lecture:* the sophists; Gorgias; persuasion in a democracy (point back). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an orator or a crowd. *Music:* none.
2. **Three ways to persuade.** *Finding:* Aristotle said we persuade by character (*ethos*), emotion (*pathos*) and argument (*logos*), and that truth has a natural advantage if well argued. *Read:* *Rhetoric* I.2. *Compare:* Acts 26:1–29 (Paul before Agrippa: all three at work). *Lecture:* the three proofs; the enthymeme; kinds of speech. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Paul preaching. *Music:* none.
3. **The orator.** *Finding:* Cicero said the true orator needs knowledge of everything; Quintilian, that he must be 'a good man skilled in speaking'. *Read:* Cicero, *On the Orator* I.30–34; Quintilian XII.1. *Compare:* James 3:1–12 (the tongue). *Lecture:* the five canons (invention, arrangement, style, memory, delivery); Roman education; Cicero's speeches. *Learn:* new `james-3-5`. *Look:* Cicero denouncing Catiline (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
4. **To teach, to delight, to move.** *Finding:* Augustine, once a professor of rhetoric, taught Christian preachers to use the art: teach so they understand, delight so they listen, move so they act. *Read:* *On Christian Doctrine* IV.12–17. *Compare:* 1 Peter 3:15–16 ('with gentleness and respect'). *Lecture:* preaching; Chrysostom 'golden mouth'; the plain style of the Puritans. *Learn:* `1-peter-3-15` (if new elsewhere, reuse). *Look:* a preacher. *Music:* none.
5. **A speech that heals.** *Finding:* Lincoln's Second Inaugural, 703 words, persuades by humility: both sides prayed to the same God, and judgement belongs to God. *Read:* Lincoln, *Second Inaugural* (whole). *Compare:* Psalm 19:9 (quoted in it; point back). *Lecture:* reading the speech line by line; Frederick Douglass's 'a sacred effort'; the King James Bible in Lincoln's prose. *Learn:* `lincoln-with-malice`. *Look:* Lincoln (NGA has portraits). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: the dark arts.** *Finding:* Persuasion has served demagogues and propagandists; the defence is that the only cure for bad speech is better speech by good people. *Read:* Plato, *Gorgias* 456a–457c (Gorgias boasts) recalled; read Schopenhauer, *The Art of Controversy* stratagems 1–5 (Bailey Saunders, 1896). *Compare:* Proverbs 18:17. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: propaganda (Goebbels, our own words), advertising, social media; fallacies; Orwell's 'Politics and the English Language' (our own words). *Debate:* Plato; Quintilian; a modern critic. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none suitable; a mask or theatre. *Music:* none.
7. **Defending what is true: the verdict, and making the case.** *Finding:* Persuasion is a moral art: speak the truth, with good character, for the listener's good, gently and with respect; that is how to defend what is true and good. *Read:* 1 Peter 3:15–16 recalled; Acts 17 (point back). *Compare:* a short passage from Pascal on persuasion (*On the Art of Persuasion*, 1658; tr. in *Thoughts, Letters and Minor Works*, Harvard Classics 1910). *Lecture:* how to disagree well; steelmanning; apologetics. **Make the case** (ends Hall IV). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a disputation or council. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, persuasion as manipulation.
