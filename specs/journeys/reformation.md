**Follows:** a first-year paper on the European Reformations.

**Frame:** one question that split Europe: how is a sinner made right with God? The journey follows it from a monk's terror (Luther) through the theses and the freedom of a Christian, the humanist critic who would not break (Erasmus), the Reformed system (Calvin), the Catholic answer (Trent and Ignatius), the cost (More, the martyrs on both sides, the wars), and the verdict for today, when Catholics and Lutherans have signed a joint declaration.

**Real questions:** What was the Reformation? What did Martin Luther believe? What were indulgences? What are the Ninety-Five Theses? What does 'justification by faith' mean? What is the difference between Catholics and Protestants? Who was Calvin? What was the Council of Trent? Who were the Jesuits? Was the Reformation good or bad? Can Catholics and Protestants agree now?

**Builds on:** *Why the cross?* (the Reformers' courtroom picture); *Abraham* (Genesis 15:6, Romans 4, Galatians 3, James 2 already read there: point back). **Read elsewhere:** Romans 4 and James 2:21–24 (*Abraham*), Romans 3:21–26 (*Why the cross?*).

## Sessions

1. **A monk in terror.** *Finding:* Luther, an Augustinian friar and professor, could find no peace until he read Romans 1:17 as a gift: righteousness given, not earned. *Read:* Romans 1:16–17. *Compare:* Luther's 1545 preface (our own words; the famous sentences quoted briefly). *Lecture:* late medieval piety: purgatory, penance, indulgences; Luther's life to 1517. *Learn:* new `romans-1-17`. *Look:* a monk at study, or Luther (NGA: portrait by Cranach? confirm). *Music:* to add: a verified recording of 'Ein feste Burg' (the memory piece a-mighty-fortress exists).
2. **Ninety-five theses.** *Finding:* On 31 October 1517 Luther challenged the sale of indulgences to debate; printing turned an academic protest into a European revolt. *Read:* selected theses. *Compare:* Tetzel's preaching as reported (our own words). *Lecture:* printing; Worms 1521 ('Here I stand', and its doubtful wording); the Peasants' War. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a printing press or a church door. *Music:* the same recording, once added.
3. **The freedom of a Christian.** *Finding:* Luther taught that a Christian is a free lord of all, subject to none, and a dutiful servant of all, subject to all: faith frees and love binds. *Read:* *The Freedom of a Christian* (opening and the happy exchange). *Compare:* Galatians 5:1, 13–14. *Lecture:* the priesthood of all believers; vocation (point ahead to *Work and money* as the hall); the two kingdoms. *Learn:* new `galatians-5-1`. *Look:* a family at home or at table. *Music:* `bach-magnificat`.
4. **The humanist and the reformer.** *Finding:* Erasmus mocked the Church's abuses before Luther and edited the Greek New Testament, but would not leave Rome; the two quarrelled over free will. *Read:* *The Praise of Folly* 53–54. *Compare:* James 2:14–20 (2:21–24 is read in *Abraham*: point back). *Lecture:* humanism (*ad fontes*); Erasmus's Greek New Testament (1516); *On Free Will* and *The Bondage of the Will* (1524–25) in our own words. *Debate:* Erasmus or Luther? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Erasmus (Holbein portraits). *Music:* none.
5. **Geneva and Trent.** *Finding:* Calvin built the Reformed system on God's sovereignty and knowledge of God and self; the Council of Trent answered with its own clear teaching on justification, grace and works. *Read:* Calvin, *Institutes* I.1.1–2 and III.11.1; Trent, Session VI, ch. 7–9 and canons. *Compare:* the side-by-side table. *Lecture:* Calvin's Geneva; predestination; Trent (1545–63); the Jesuits and Ignatius (*Principle and Foundation*). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a council, or St Ignatius. *Music:* `palestrina-marcelli` (the music Trent nearly banned).
6. **The case against: the cost.** *Finding:* The Reformation broke the Western Church, unleashed persecution on all sides (More and Tyndale, Cranmer and Campion) and fed wars that killed millions; Catholics, Protestants and secular historians each tell the cost differently. *Read:* Thomas More's last letter to his daughter Margaret. *Compare:* Hugh Latimer at the stake ('play the man, Master Ridley'), from Foxe's *Book of Martyrs* (1563; PD). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength in two forms: the Catholic (Brad Gregory, *The Unintended Reformation*, our own words: fragmentation and secularism) and the secular (religion as cause of war; point ahead to *Toleration* as the hall); replies (Diarmaid MacCulloch, our own words). *Debate:* Gregory; MacCulloch; a Protestant historian. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a martyrdom, or More (Holbein). *Music:* none.
7. **Five hundred years on: the verdict.** *Finding:* The Reformation was a quarrel about grace that both sides took with deadly seriousness; in 1999 Lutherans and Catholics declared they share 'a basic truth' on justification, while real differences remain. *Read:* Romans 3:21–28 recalled (point back). *Compare:* the *Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification* (1999; our own words). *Lecture:* what remains divided (authority, the sacraments, Mary); what is shared; the Reformation's legacy (literacy, conscience, vernacular Bibles, point ahead to *The King James Bible*). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a church interior. *Music:* `jesu-joy`.

**Objection:** session 6, the cost, from Catholic and secular sides.
