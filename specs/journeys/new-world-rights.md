**Follows:** a paper on early modern Spain and the origins of international law and human rights.

**Frame:** a trial held in 1550 at Valladolid, the only time an empire stopped its conquests to ask whether they were just. The journey leads up to it (the encounter, the sermon of 1511, the eyewitness, the papal bull, the lawyer), stages the debate, and asks what it began.

**Real questions:** What happened when Europeans arrived in the Americas? Was it genocide? Who was Bartolomé de las Casas? What was the Valladolid debate? Did the Church support the conquest? Where do human rights come from? What is international law? Who was Francisco de Vitoria? Did Christians condemn slavery in the New World? What about African slavery?

**Builds on:** *Human dignity* (Aristotle's natural slaves, read), *Aristotle* (session 6), *The rule of law*. **Read elsewhere:** Aristotle *Politics* I.4–6 (*Human dignity*).

## Sessions

1. **The encounter.** *Finding:* Columbus's letters show wonder at the islanders and, in the same pages, plans to enslave them. *Read:* Columbus's letter of 1493. *Compare:* Genesis 1:27 (point back). *Lecture:* 1492; the Taíno; the encomienda; disease (the larger part of the deaths, our own words with figures and their uncertainty). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a New World landscape or encounter (NGA: Catlin is used; choose other). *Music:* none.
2. **Are these not men?** *Finding:* In Advent 1511 a Dominican friar, Antonio de Montesinos, told the Spanish settlers of Hispaniola that they were in mortal sin for their cruelty: 'Are these not men?' *Read:* Las Casas's report of the sermon (short quotation, our own words). *Compare:* Isaiah 58:6–7 (point back if read in *The poor*). *Lecture:* the Dominicans; the Laws of Burgos (1512); the *Requerimiento*. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Dominican friar preaching. *Music:* none.
3. **The eyewitness.** *Finding:* Las Casas, a former slave-owner turned friar, spent fifty years documenting atrocities and pleading for the Indians at court. *Read:* *A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies*, opening and Hispaniola. *Compare:* Matthew 25:40 (point back). *Lecture:* Las Casas's conversion (1514); his early proposal to use African slaves, later repented; the 'Black Legend'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of a friar. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
4. **Sublimis Deus.** *Finding:* In 1537 Pope Paul III declared that the Indians are true human beings, capable of faith, who must not be enslaved or deprived of their property. *Read:* *Sublimis Deus*. *Compare:* Acts 17:26 (point back). *Lecture:* the bull's force and its revocation; the Church's mixed record. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a pope (Titian's Paul III at Naples; NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
5. **The law of nations.** *Finding:* At Salamanca, Francisco de Vitoria argued that the Indians had true dominion over their lands and that no pope or emperor could take them by right of discovery: the birth of international law. *Read:* Vitoria, *De Indis* I.1–4 and III (Bate). *Compare:* Cicero's natural law (point back to *Rome*). *Lecture:* the School of Salamanca; *ius gentium*; Grotius later. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar or university scene. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: the Valladolid debate.** *Finding:* Sepúlveda argued, from Aristotle, that the Indians were natural slaves and war on them just; this is the conquest's case at full strength, and a sign that Christendom's own scholars could argue for it. *Read:* Sepúlveda's case (our own words) and Las Casas's reply (*In Defense of the Indians*, our own words; Poole 1974 is copyright). *Compare:* Aristotle, *Politics* I.5 (point back). *Lecture:* the debate (1550–51); no formal verdict; what changed (the New Laws of 1542 earlier, the 1573 ordinances). *Debate:* Sepúlveda; Las Casas; a modern historian (Anthony Pagden, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none suitable; a council. *Music:* none.
7. **Equal before God: the verdict.** *Finding:* Conquest and enslavement were real crimes of Christian nations; the first and loudest protests against them came from Christians, on Christian grounds, and began the idea of universal human rights. *Read:* Las Casas's final testament (our own words) or *Sublimis Deus* recalled. *Compare:* the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) article 1 and its debt to natural law (our own words). *Lecture:* from Valladolid to the UN; African slavery (point ahead to Hall VII's *The end of slavery* as the hall); Brian Tierney on natural rights (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of an Indigenous person with dignity (NGA: Catlin used; another). *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6, Sepúlveda's case, and the record of the conquest itself.

**Traditions:** Catholic (Dominicans, Jesuits in Paraguay), Protestant colonial records (English and Dutch) side by side.

**Voices:** Anthony Pagden; Lewis Hanke, *All Mankind Is One*; Brian Tierney; Gustavo Gutiérrez on Las Casas; Charles Mann, *1491*.
