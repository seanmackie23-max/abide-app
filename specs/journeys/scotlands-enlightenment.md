**Follows:** a paper on the Scottish Enlightenment.

**Frame:** a small, poor country that for about seventy years produced the ideas of the modern world: universal schooling from the Reformation, the moral sense (Hutcheson), the sceptic (Hume) and his answer (Reid's common sense), the moral and economic system (Smith), the history of society (Ferguson), and the minister who tried to rebuild the parish in a city (Chalmers).

**Real questions:** What was the Scottish Enlightenment? Why did Scotland produce so many thinkers? Who was David Hume? What is the is–ought problem? What did Adam Smith actually believe? What is the 'invisible hand'? What is common sense philosophy? Was the Scottish Enlightenment religious? Who was Thomas Chalmers? What did Scotland give the world?

**Builds on:** *The Reformation* (Knox), *Rights and revolutions*. **Read elsewhere:** Hume's *Dialogues* V (*Is there a God?*); Smith's *Wealth of Nations* I (*The poor*). Hume on miracles is its own journey next.

## Sessions

1. **A school in every parish.** *Finding:* The Scottish Reformers planned a school in every parish so every child could read the Bible; by 1700 Scotland was one of Europe's most literate nations. *Read:* *The First Book of Discipline*, on schools. *Compare:* Deuteronomy 6:6–9. *Lecture:* Knox; the Kirk; the five universities; the Union of 1707. *Learn:* `deuteronomy-6-4`. *Look:* a Scottish landscape or schoolroom. *Music:* `crimond`.
2. **The moral sense.** *Finding:* Hutcheson, a Presbyterian minister's son teaching at Glasgow, argued we have a moral sense that delights in benevolence, and first wrote 'the greatest happiness for the greatest numbers'. *Read:* Hutcheson, *Inquiry* II.3. *Compare:* Romans 2:14–15 (point back). *Lecture:* moral sense theory; Hutcheson against slavery; his influence on Smith and Jefferson. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of a philosopher. *Music:* none.
3. **The great sceptic.** *Finding:* Hume argued that reason is the slave of the passions and that you cannot derive an 'ought' from an 'is': the most unsettling philosopher in English. *Read:* Hume, *Treatise*, Introduction and III.i.1 (is and ought). *Compare:* Hume on his own good-humoured death (Smith's letter to Strahan, 1776). *Lecture:* Hume's life and 'Le Bon David'; empiricism; causation. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Hume (NGA: Ramsay? flag). *Music:* none.
4. **Common sense.** *Finding:* Thomas Reid, a minister and Hume's successor in argument, replied that philosophy which denies what everyone must believe has gone wrong somewhere. *Read:* Reid, *Inquiry* Dedication and ch. I. *Compare:* Proverbs 1:7 (point back). *Lecture:* common sense realism; its influence in America (Princeton) and on modern philosophy (Plantinga, our own words). *Debate:* Hume or Reid? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a plain portrait. *Music:* none.
5. **The impartial spectator.** *Finding:* Adam Smith, before he wrote on wealth, wrote on sympathy and conscience: we judge ourselves by imagining an impartial spectator. *Read:* *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* I.i.1 and III.3. *Compare:* the golden rule (point back) and the 'invisible hand' in TMS IV.1 (not only in *Wealth of Nations*). *Lecture:* the 'Adam Smith problem'; markets and morals (point back to *The poor*). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of Smith (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: did the Enlightenment free us from religion?** *Finding:* The usual story says Hume and the philosophers liberated reason from faith; in Scotland most Enlightenment men were ministers or believers, and the sceptic was the exception who was answered. *Read:* Ferguson, *Essay on the History of Civil Society* I.1 and III.2 (and his warning about commerce). *Compare:* Hume, 'Of Superstition and Enthusiasm' (1741). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Peter Gay's 'rise of modern paganism', our own words); replies (Jonathan Israel's radical and moderate Enlightenments; Alexander Broadie; the Moderates in the Kirk). *Debate:* Gay; Broadie; Himmelfarb. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Edinburgh. *Music:* none.
7. **A godly commonwealth: the verdict.** *Finding:* Scotland taught the modern world literacy, moral sentiment, economics and common sense, and Chalmers showed the churches how to serve the industrial city; it was an enlightenment within a Christian culture, not against it. *Read:* Chalmers, *Christian and Civic Economy* (selected). *Compare:* James Clerk Maxwell's prayer (found among his papers, PD, short) (point ahead to *Darwin and design* as the hall). *Lecture:* Scottish legacy (Hume, Smith, Watt, Maxwell, Livingstone, the Free Church of 1843). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an Edinburgh or Glasgow scene. *Music:* `crimond`.

**Objection:** session 6, the secularising Enlightenment.

**Traditions:** Presbyterian Moderates and Evangelicals; Episcopalians and Catholics in Scotland.
