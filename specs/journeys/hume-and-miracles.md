**Follows:** an advanced paper in philosophy of religion (miracles and testimony).

**Frame:** a courtroom with one rule: Hume's maxim that no testimony can establish a miracle unless its falsehood would be more miraculous. The journey hears the case (Hume, Part I and II), the cross-examination (Campbell, Paley, Whately's satire), modern probability (Bayes, our own words), and the resurrection as the test case.

**Real questions:** What is a miracle? What did Hume say about miracles? Is it irrational to believe in miracles? Do miracles break the laws of nature? Can testimony prove a miracle? What is Bayes' theorem and miracles? Do miracles happen today? Did Jesus perform miracles? What would count as evidence?

**Builds on:** *Who was Jesus?* (miracles session), *The last week* (the resurrection, the objection), *Scotland's Enlightenment*. **Read elsewhere:** 1 Corinthians 15 (*The last week*).

## Sessions

1. **What is a miracle?** *Finding:* A miracle in the Bible is a sign; in modern debate it is a violation of a law of nature by a god. Which definition we use decides much of the argument. *Read:* John 2:1–11 or Mark 5:21–43. *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* XXI.8 (miracles are not against nature, only against what we know of it). *Lecture:* definitions (Aquinas, Hume, Swinburne, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the Wedding at Cana. *Music:* none.
2. **A wise man proportions his belief.** *Finding:* Hume argued that our experience of nature's laws is uniform, so the evidence against any miracle is always as strong as can be. *Read:* Hume, *Enquiry* X, Part I. *Compare:* Hume's own maxim, shown as a balance. *Lecture:* Hume's argument step by step; 'uniform experience'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scale or balance (Vermeer's *Woman Holding a Balance* is used: another). *Music:* none.
3. **Four reasons to doubt witnesses.** *Finding:* Hume added four practical reasons: miracle witnesses are never many and learned enough, people love wonders, miracles abound among 'ignorant and barbarous nations', and rival religions' miracles cancel out. *Read:* *Enquiry* X, Part II. *Compare:* the Abbé Pâris miracles Hume cites (our own words). *Lecture:* each of the four, fairly. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
4. **The cross-examination.** *Finding:* George Campbell, Aberdeen minister, answered that testimony is a primary source of knowledge and that Hume's 'uniform experience' assumes what it should prove. *Read:* Campbell, *Dissertation* I.1–2. *Compare:* Paley, *Evidences*, Preparatory Considerations. *Lecture:* testimony as evidence; the Indian prince and the ice (Hume's own example and Locke's); Price on probability (our own words). *Debate:* Hume; Campbell; Paley. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a courtroom. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: Hume at full strength.** *Finding:* Modern defenders of Hume, armed with probability theory, argue that the prior improbability of a miracle is so great that no ancient testimony can overcome it. *Read:* Hume, *Enquiry* X recalled, with the probabilistic reconstruction (our own words). *Compare:* Whately, *Historic Doubts* (the satire: by Hume's rules Napoleon did not exist). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: J. L. Mackie, *The Miracle of Theism* (our own words); John Earman's *Hume's Abject Failure* on the other side (our own words); Bayes. *Debate:* Mackie; Earman; Swinburne. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **The test case: the resurrection.** *Finding:* Whether the resurrection is credible depends less on the testimony than on what you think is possible: if there is a God who might act, the evidence must be weighed; if not, no evidence will do. *Read:* 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 (point back). *Compare:* C. S. Lewis, *Miracles* (our own words: the argument that naturalism is self-refuting). *Lecture:* background beliefs; the resurrection debate (point back to *The last week*); Craig Keener's modern miracle accounts (our own words). *Learn:* `cor-15-3`. *Look:* the Resurrection. *Music:* `christ-the-lord-is-risen`.
7. **Can a reasonable person believe? The verdict.** *Finding:* A reasonable person can believe in miracles if they have good reason to think God exists and good testimony; a reasonable person can also doubt; what is unreasonable is deciding the question before looking. *Read:* Mark 5:21–43 recalled or Hume's closing paragraph of X (the 'continued miracle' of faith). *Compare:* Pascal (point back) on signs. *Lecture:* summary table of the arguments. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a healing scene. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 5.
