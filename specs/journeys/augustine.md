**Follows:** a paper on Augustine's thought (history of Christian doctrine).

**Frame:** the restless heart: the first line of the *Confessions* as a map of one life (restless as a child, a thief, a lover, a seeker, a divided will, a convert, at rest with his mother at Ostia), and then of all history in the *City of God*: two loves making two cities.

**Real questions:** Who was Augustine? What are the *Confessions*? What does 'our heart is restless' mean? Why did he steal pears? What happened in the garden? What is original sin? What is the City of God? What did Augustine think about sex? Why is Augustine so influential? Was Augustine right about grace?

**Builds on:** *Plato* (Confessions VII.9–10, read); several journeys have read Confessions IV, IX.6–7, X and XI (see below). **Read elsewhere:** Confessions III.5 (*What is the Bible?*), IV (*Why beauty matters*), VII.9–10 (*Plato*), IX.6–7 (*The sound of faith*), X (*A good life*, *Dust that wonders*, *Light, stone and glass*, *The sound of faith*), XI (*Time made holy*), XII (*What is the Bible?*), I.13 (*Homer*/*Rome*); City of God XIV.28, XV.1, XXII.24, XXII.30, IV.4, V.12–13, XIV.9, VIII.5–9.

## Sessions

1. **You have made us for yourself.** *Finding:* Augustine wrote his life as a prayer to God, beginning with the restlessness he found even in a baby's jealousy. *Read:* *Confessions* I.1 and I.6–7. *Compare:* Psalm 145:3 (or 'Great art thou, O Lord', Psalm 147:5). *Lecture:* his life (Thagaste 354, Carthage, Rome, Milan, Hippo, d. 430); the genre of confession. *Learn:* `augustine-our-heart-is-restless`. *Look:* St Augustine (NGA: confirm). *Music:* none.
2. **The pears.** *Finding:* As a teenager he stole pears he did not want, for the thrill of doing wrong with friends: Augustine's study of why we love what is bad. *Read:* *Confessions* II.4–10. *Compare:* Genesis 3:6 (point back). *Lecture:* sin as disordered love; the role of friends; privation theory of evil (point ahead to Hall VIII as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a pear tree or orchard; a still life with pears. *Music:* none.
3. **Two wills.** *Finding:* Augustine discovered that he could command his hand but not his will: 'give me chastity, but not yet'. *Read:* *Confessions* VIII.5 (and VI.11 on his career). *Compare:* Romans 7:15–25. *Lecture:* the Manichees; Ambrose in Milan; his partner and son Adeodatus; career ambition. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a figure torn between two paths. *Music:* none.
4. **Take up and read.** *Finding:* In a garden in Milan in 386, hearing a child's voice, Augustine opened Paul and was changed in a moment that had been years in coming. *Read:* *Confessions* VIII.8–12. *Compare:* Romans 13:13–14 (the verse he read). *Lecture:* Antony's story (point back to *How to read the Bible* if used); conversion as event and process (William James, point back). *Learn:* `romans-13-13`. *Look:* the conversion of Augustine. *Music:* none.
5. **At Ostia.** *Finding:* With his mother Monica at a window in Ostia, days before she died, Augustine climbed in thought beyond all things to touch eternal Wisdom, for a moment. *Read:* *Confessions* IX.10. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 2:9. *Lecture:* Monica; mysticism (point ahead to Hall V's mystics as the hall); grief. *Learn:* `augustine-too-late-loved-i-thee`. *Look:* Augustine and Monica (Ary Scheffer is not NGA: flag). *Music:* `faure-requiem`.
6. **The case against: grace and freedom.** *Finding:* Pelagius said we can choose the good if we try; Augustine said we need grace even to want it; critics ancient and modern have called Augustine's view a cruel fatalism. *Read:* Augustine, *On Nature and Grace* 1–7 (NPNF I.5) beside Pelagius's *Letter to Demetrias* in our own words. *Compare:* Philippians 2:12–13. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: original sin, infant damnation, predestination, his view of sex; the Council of Orange (529); Orthodox, Catholic, Lutheran and Reformed views side by side. *Debate:* Pelagius; Augustine; the Orthodox middle. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a child, or Adam and Eve. *Music:* none.
7. **Two loves, two cities: the verdict.** *Finding:* After Rome was sacked, Augustine wrote that history is the story of two cities made by two loves; the earthly peace is good but never final. *Read:* *City of God* XIX.13–17. *Compare:* *City of God* XIV.28 (point back). *Lecture:* church and state; just war (point ahead to Hall IX as the hall); Augustine's influence (Aquinas, Luther, Calvin, Pascal, Arendt). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the City of God or a heavenly city. *Music:* `locus-iste`.

**Objection:** session 6, Pelagius and modern critics on grace, sin and sex.

**Traditions:** original sin and grace (Orthodox ancestral sin, Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed) side by side.

**Voices:** Peter Brown, *Augustine of Hippo*; James O'Donnell; Hannah Arendt (*Love and Saint Augustine*); Rowan Williams, *On Augustine*.
