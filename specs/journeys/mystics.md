**Follows:** a paper on Christian spirituality and mysticism.

**Frame:** two ways of knowing God that the mystics held together: the way of light and love (Bernard, Francis, Julian: God known in all things) and the way of darkness (the Cloud: God beyond all things). The journey walks both, faces the sceptic's charge that it is all feeling or brain chemistry, and ends with how ordinary people can begin.

**Real questions:** What is a mystic? What is mysticism? Who was Julian of Norwich? What is the Cloud of Unknowing? Who was Hildegard? What did St Francis experience? Can anyone have a mystical experience? Is mysticism just brain chemistry? What is the difference between knowing about God and knowing God? How do I start?

**Builds on:** *How to pray*, *Augustine* (Ostia), *Bearing suffering* (Julian 27–29, read). **Read elsewhere:** Julian 27–29 (*Bearing suffering*); Kempis III.1–2 (*How to pray*); William James on mysticism (*Is there a God?*, *How to pray*).

## Sessions

1. **Show me your glory.** *Finding:* The Bible itself speaks of seeing God's back, not his face: knowing God is a gift that both reveals and hides. *Read:* Exodus 33:18–23. *Compare:* Psalm 27:4–8 ('your face, Yahweh, I will seek'). *Lecture:* what 'mysticism' means (and William James's four marks, point back); Gregory of Nyssa's *Life of Moses* (our own words). *Learn:* new `1-john-4-16`. *Look:* Moses on the mountain. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
2. **The four degrees of love.** *Finding:* Bernard taught that love climbs from loving self for self's sake to loving self only for God's sake: the mystic way is a school of love. *Read:* Bernard, *On Loving God* VIII–X. *Compare:* Song of Songs (point back). *Lecture:* Bernard and the Cistercians; affective mysticism. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Bernard's vision. *Music:* `salve-regina`.
3. **Brother Sun.** *Finding:* Francis, near the end of his life, blind and in pain, sang the whole creation as his brothers and sisters: mysticism of joy in things. *Read:* *Canticle of Brother Sun* (Arnold) and *Little Flowers* 8 (perfect joy). *Compare:* Psalm 148. *Lecture:* Francis's life; the stigmata (1224); the friars. *Learn:* new `canticle-brother-sun`. *Look:* St Francis (Strozzi used; another). *Music:* `all-creatures`.
4. **The cloud.** *Finding:* An anonymous fourteenth-century English priest taught that God cannot be reached by thought, only by 'a naked intent' of love, through a cloud of unknowing. *Read:* *The Cloud of Unknowing* 3–7 (Underhill). *Compare:* Pseudo-Dionysius, *Mystical Theology* 1 (Parker, 1897). *Lecture:* apophatic theology; Dionysius; Eckhart (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a mist or night scene. *Music:* `spem-in-alium`.
5. **The hazelnut.** *Finding:* Julian of Norwich, an anchoress, saw all that is made as small as a hazelnut in God's hand, kept by love. *Read:* Julian, *Revelations* 5 and 86 (Warrack). *Compare:* Wisdom 11:24–26. *Lecture:* anchoresses; Julian's sixteen showings (1373); 'God as mother'. *Learn:* `julian-all-shall-be-well`. *Look:* a still life with a nut, or a contemplative woman. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: just feelings?** *Finding:* Sceptics argue mystical experiences are brain events or projected wishes, and that mystics contradict each other; the defenders answer that a cause in the brain does not show there is no object. *Read:* Freud, *The Future of an Illusion* (1927; Robson-Scott 1928: died?; our own words if not qualifying) or Bertrand Russell, *Mysticism and Logic* (1918; original English: qualifies) chapter I. *Compare:* Hildegard's account of her visions (our own words) beside Oliver Sacks's migraine reading (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: neurotheology (Newberg), William James's reply (point back), Richard Swinburne's principle of credulity (our own words). *Debate:* Russell; James; Sacks. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a visionary. *Music:* `hildegard`.
7. **Imitation: the verdict.** *Finding:* The mystics agree that God is known by love more than by mind, and that the way is ordinary humility; Thomas à Kempis made it a book for everyone. *Read:* *The Imitation of Christ* I.1–3. *Compare:* Micah 6:8 (point back). *Lecture:* the Devotio Moderna; mysticism in the traditions side by side (Orthodox hesychasm, Catholic Carmelites ahead, Protestant pietism); how to begin. *Learn:* `kempis-love-to-be-unknown`. *Look:* a reader at prayer. *Music:* `jesu-joy`.

**Objection:** session 6, mystical experience as illusion.

**Traditions:** Orthodox hesychasm (the Jesus Prayer), Catholic mysticism, Protestant pietism and Quakers side by side.
