**Change requested (owner, 25 October 2026):** make the start of the Old Testament read Genesis the way *How to read the Bible* teaches: symbolically, as the old interpreters did, following Matthieu Pageau's *The Language of Creation*. The owner's frame is below. It replaces the earlier small change (Peterson becomes one voice in session 1's debate).

**Frame: two ways of seeing the world.** The modern, materialist way sees only matter. The ancient, religious way sees two things, meaning and matter, and orders them.
- **Heaven and earth (Genesis 1:1).** "Heaven" is meaning: purpose, identity, what a thing is for. "Earth" is matter without meaning yet: the unknown, the possible, "without form and void". God orders them by speaking: meaning above, matter below.
- **The garden (Genesis 2).** Eden is the centre, where heaven and earth meet. The tree stands at that meeting point, as trees still do in fantasy stories. The human being, dust and breath, is the link between the two.
- **The serpent (Genesis 3).** It comes from the edge and offers knowledge. The eyes of Adam and Eve are opened and they see their nakedness: they become self-conscious, aware of themselves as objects among objects. They are sent from the centre to the edge.
- **The fruit again.** Modern science re-enacts the story. Studying the world as matter, from the outside, we found the earth was not the centre: it goes round the sun, which is one star among trillions. Knowledge of matter alone opens our eyes and leaves us exposed, small and exiled.
- **The way back (John 1).** The way back to the centre is not to reject science but to join it with meaning, and the Word does that. A word is a sound or a mark, matter, that gathers many things into one meaning. Language is where heaven and earth meet. John's Gospel opens as Genesis does: "In the beginning was the Word." Then "the Word became flesh": in Christ, meaning does not just point to matter, it becomes matter, and heaven and earth meet in a person. The centre we lost was never a place in space. It is the meeting of heaven and earth, in you, and in Christ it is restored.

**Note on wording:** "In the beginning was the Word" is John 1:1, not Genesis. John deliberately begins with Genesis's first words, and Genesis 1 already has God create by speaking ("God said…"). The journey makes that link explicit and attributes each line to the right book.

## Sessions

1. **Two ways of seeing: heaven and earth.** *Answers:* What does "the heavens and the earth" mean? Is Genesis 1 a science lesson? *Finding:* Read as the ancient interpreters read it, Genesis 1 is not a description of planet and outer space. It speaks of two orders, meaning ("heaven") and matter ("earth", formless and void), which God orders by speaking. The modern eye sees only the second.
   *Read:* Genesis 1:1–2:3. *Compare:* Augustine, *Confessions* XII.2–8 (Pusey): the "heaven of heaven" as the spiritual creation, and the formless earth as matter.
   *Lecture:* *shamayim* and *erets*; *tohu wa-bohu*; creation by word, separation and naming; the Hebrew picture of the world; Genesis against the myths of its neighbours (kept). *Debate:* four readings: the merism, "heaven and earth" meaning everything, the visible sky and land (most modern commentators, in our own words); the symbolic reading (Augustine, Origen; Pageau, in our own words); the psychological reading (Peterson, 2017 lectures, cited by lecture and date, in our own words); the literal-scientific reading (young-earth creationism, fairly).
   *Learn:* existing Genesis 1:1 piece or new `genesis-1-1`. *Look:* creation or a night sky. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
2. **The centre: where heaven and earth meet.** *Answers:* What is Eden? Why a tree? What am I? *Finding:* Eden is the centre of the world, the place where heaven and earth meet, and the human being, dust of the ground with God's breath in him, stands there as the link between them.
   *Read:* Genesis 2:4–25. *Compare:* Ezekiel 28:12–14 ("Eden, the garden of God… the holy mountain of God"): Eden as the first sanctuary, on the mountain where heaven and earth meet. Ezekiel 47's river is read in *Holy places*: point ahead to the hall only.
   *Lecture:* the garden as sanctuary (Wenham, 1986; Beale; our own words); the four rivers going out to the four corners; the tree at the centre across cultures (the world tree, in our own words) and in modern fantasy (in our own words, no quotation); "image of God" (kept). *Debate:* a place, a temple-picture, or both?
   *Learn:* new `genesis-2-7`. *Look:* the garden of Eden or Paradise. *Music:* choose from `music.json`.
3. **The serpent from the edge: eyes opened.** *Answers:* What went wrong? What does "their eyes were opened" mean? *Finding:* Genesis tells the fall as a change of seeing. Knowledge grasped apart from God makes the man and woman self-conscious: they see themselves as exposed objects, hide, and are sent from the centre east, to the edge.
   *Read:* Genesis 3:1–24. *Compare:* choose a text not read elsewhere (specs_check).
   *Lecture:* the serpent as a creature of the edge; nakedness and shame; the cherubim and the flaming sword guarding the way back; original sin across the traditions (kept, fairly: Augustine, Orthodox ancestral sin, modern views). *Debate:* kept from the current session 3.
   *Learn:* existing. *Look:* the Fall, or the Expulsion. *Music:* existing.
4. **Further from the centre: Cain.** *Answers:* Why do people hurt each other? *Finding:* Away from the centre, violence spreads. Cain goes further east and builds the first city, yet God still marks and protects him.
   *Read:* Genesis 4:1–16 (and 4:23–24, 6:1–8 as now). Keep the current session's content, re-framed as the movement outward.
5. **The waters return.** *Answers:* Will God give up on us? *Finding:* The flood is creation undone: the waters above and below that God separated on the second day pour back, and the world returns to formless matter. Then God orders it again and makes a promise. Keep the current content on the ancient flood stories and geology.
6. **The fruit again: doesn't science say otherwise?** *Answers:* Doesn't science disprove Genesis? Why does the universe make us feel small? *Finding:* Science rules out reading Genesis as a scientific timetable, but it does not refute what Genesis says. Taken alone, though, knowledge of matter re-enacts the fruit: it opens our eyes and leaves us exposed, small and off-centre. The way back is not to reject science but to join it to meaning.
   *Read:* Huxley (1885), kept as the objection at full strength. *Compare:* Psalm 8 (point back; quote one line at most).
   *Lecture:* Copernicus, Galileo and the "demotion" story told as the owner frames it, then corrected fairly. In medieval cosmology the centre was the lowest place, not the most honoured, so the first Copernicans did not feel demoted (Dennis Danielson, 'The Great Copernican Cliché', *American Journal of Physics* 69, 2001; our own words). Freud's "blows" to human self-love (1917; our own words); the scale of the cosmos; Pascal's "these infinite spaces" points ahead to Hall VI. *Debate:* does modern knowledge diminish us? (Sagan's "pale blue dot", our own words; the ancient answer.) **Objection session.**
   *Learn:* existing. *Look:* a starry or vast landscape. *Music:* existing.
7. **Babel and the Word: the centre restored.** *Answers:* Why so many peoples and languages? What does John mean by "the Word"? How do we get back to the centre? *Finding:* At Babel people try to reach heaven from below, by matter alone, and their one language is scattered. John answers Genesis: the Word through whom all things were made, meaning itself, became flesh, so heaven and earth meet in a person. The centre was never a place in space. It is that meeting, offered to each person.
   *Read:* Genesis 11:1–9; Genesis 12:1–3; John 1:1–14. *Compare:* Acts 2 (point back: Babel reversed at Pentecost).
   *Lecture:* what a word is (a sound or mark that carries meaning: matter and sense together); *logos* in Greek thought (point ahead to the Athens hall, not to a session); John's deliberate echo of Genesis 1:1; the Incarnation as the meeting of heaven and earth; Abraham's call as the start of the way back (leading into *Abraham*). *Debate:* none; the verdict: what does Genesis 1–11 say about the world and about us?
   *Learn:* `john-in-the-beginning-was-the-word` (existing). *Look:* St John writing, or the Nativity. *Music:* existing or `o-magnum-mysterium`.
