**Follows:** a paper in medieval philosophy and philosophy of religion.

**Frame:** a ladder with two rails: reason climbs from below (Anselm's argument, Aquinas's Five Ways), revelation comes from above (what reason cannot reach: the Trinity, the Incarnation), and the medieval schoolmen insisted the two rails belong to one ladder. The objection: Bernard's fear and the modern sceptic's charge that the ladder was rigged.

**Real questions:** What did medieval people think about faith and reason? What is the ontological argument? What are Aquinas's Five Ways? Who was Thomas Aquinas? Can you prove God exists? What is scholasticism? Did Muslims and Jews influence Christian philosophy? What did Aquinas think reason cannot prove? Is faith blind?

**Builds on:** *Is there a God?* (the arguments in outline), *Aristotle*, *When Athens met Jerusalem*. **Read elsewhere:** Aquinas I q.2 a.3 first and second ways (*Aristotle*): here the Five Ways whole, with point-back.

## Sessions

1. **Faith seeking understanding.** *Finding:* Anselm prayed his way into an argument: God is that than which nothing greater can be thought, and such a being must exist. *Read:* *Proslogion* 1–4. *Compare:* Psalm 14:1 (the fool). *Lecture:* Anselm (Aosta, Bec, Canterbury); the ontological argument step by step. *Learn:* new `anselm-credo-ut-intelligam`. *Look:* a monk at prayer in a study. *Music:* `palestrina-marcelli`.
2. **On behalf of the fool.** *Finding:* A monk named Gaunilo answered at once with a perfect island; the argument has been refuted and revived ever since (Aquinas, Descartes, Kant, Plantinga). *Read:* Gaunilo, *On Behalf of the Fool*, and Anselm's reply (excerpt). *Compare:* Kant's 'existence is not a predicate' (our own words). *Lecture:* the argument's later history; modal version (Plantinga, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an island seascape. *Music:* none.
3. **Yes and no.** *Finding:* Abelard set authorities against each other to teach students to think: doubt leads to inquiry, inquiry to truth. *Read:* Abelard, *Sic et Non*, Prologue (in our own words if no PD English; the famous sentence may be quoted). *Compare:* Bernard's charge against Abelard (Letter 190, our own words). *Lecture:* the scholastic method (question, objections, sed contra, answer, replies); Paris schools (point ahead to *Cathedrals and universities* only as the hall). *Debate:* Abelard or Bernard? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a disputation. *Music:* none.
4. **The Philosopher returns.** *Finding:* Aristotle came back to the West through Muslim and Jewish scholars (Avicenna, Averroes, Maimonides), forcing Christians to ask how far reason could go. *Read:* Maimonides, *Guide for the Perplexed*, Introduction (Friedländer). *Compare:* Averroes, *The Decisive Treatise* (in our own words). *Lecture:* Toledo translators; the condemnations of 1277; 'double truth'. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* scholars of three faiths, or a Spanish scene. *Music:* none.
5. **The Five Ways.** *Finding:* Aquinas offered five short arguments from the world (motion, cause, contingency, degrees, order) to a first cause 'all men call God'. *Read:* *Summa* I q.2 a.3 (whole; *Aristotle* read the argument from motion in the *Summa contra Gentiles*). *Compare:* Romans 1:19–20. *Lecture:* each Way in plain words; what they prove and do not (not the Christian God fully); Hume's and Kant's objections (point ahead to Hall VII as the hall). *Learn:* `psalm-19-1`. *Look:* St Thomas Aquinas (NGA: confirm). *Music:* `ave-verum` (Aquinas wrote *Pange lingua*; Mozart's *Ave verum* is not his: choose `o-magnum-mysterium` or note).
6. **The case against: a rigged ladder?** *Finding:* Critics say the schoolmen began with their conclusions and found reasons afterwards; others, from Luther to Pascal, that reason cannot reach the living God at all. *Read:* Aquinas, *Summa contra Gentiles* I.3–7 (what reason cannot reach, and why faith is not irrational). *Compare:* Luther on reason in the *Heidelberg Disputation* theses 19–24 (1518; Philadelphia ed. or our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength from two sides: the rationalist (Russell's 'not philosophy but special pleading', our own words) and the fideist (Luther, Kierkegaard, our own words). *Debate:* Russell; Luther; Aquinas. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar with a skull or vanitas. *Music:* none.
7. **Straw, and the verdict.** *Finding:* Aquinas held that faith and reason cannot truly conflict because both come from God; near the end he stopped writing and called his work straw beside what he had seen. *Read:* *Summa* I q.1 a.1 and a.8. *Compare:* Isaiah 55:8–9; the account of Aquinas's last months (Bartholomew of Capua's testimony, in our own words). *Lecture:* *Fides et Ratio* (1998, our own words); the Catholic, Orthodox (apophatic) and Protestant positions side by side. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Aquinas, or a vision scene. *Music:* `ave-verum`.

**Objection:** session 6, rationalist and fideist critics.

**Traditions:** natural theology (Catholic: Vatican I), apophatic theology (Orthodox), Reformed epistemology and Barth's 'no' (Protestant) side by side.

**Voices:** Edward Feser, *Five Proofs*; Brian Davies; Alvin Plantinga; Bertrand Russell; Etienne Gilson.
