**Follows:** a paper on contemporary religion and culture.

**Frame:** each session pairs one modern voice (our own words, cited) with the Bible passage or public-domain text they discuss, as the hall plan sets out: the psychologist (Peterson), the historian (Holland), the ex-Muslim activist (Hirsi Ali), the novelist-environmentalist (Kingsnorth), the neuroscientist (McGilchrist), the broadcaster (Brierley); then the hardest question: is finding the Bible meaningful the same as believing it true?

**Real questions:** Is there a return to Christianity? Why are young men going to church? Is Jordan Peterson a Christian? What is Tom Holland's Dominion? Why did Ayaan Hirsi Ali become a Christian? Who is Paul Kingsnorth? What does Iain McGilchrist say about religion? Is cultural Christianity enough? Do you have to believe it to benefit? What do the statistics say?

**Builds on:** *The death of God?*, *After the death of God*, *In the beginning* (Peterson in the debate, if added). **Read elsewhere:** Genesis 4 and 32 (*In the beginning*, *Abraham*); Galatians 3:28 (*How we got here*); Pascal fragments 72, 139, 206, 233, 277, 347, 434, 553 (*Pascal*).

## Sessions

1. **The stories we live by.** *Finding:* Jordan Peterson's lectures on Genesis drew millions to the Bible as a map of meaning: Cain and Abel as resentment, Jacob as wrestling with God. *Read:* Genesis 4 and 32 point back; Peterson's readings (our own words, cited to the 2017 Biblical Series and *We Who Wrestle with God*, 2024). *Compare:* Matthew 13:44–46. *Lecture:* Peterson's psychological reading; Jung behind it (our own words); what it adds and leaves out. *Learn:* `genesis-32-26`. *Look:* Jacob wrestling the angel (Veronese's Rebecca used; another). *Music:* none.
2. **We are all Christians now.** *Finding:* Tom Holland, a historian of the ancient world, concluded that the West's values (care for the weak, equality, human rights) are Christian through and through, even in its critics. *Read:* Galatians 3:28 (point back); Holland, *Dominion* (2019; our own words). *Compare:* Nietzsche (point back to *Sermon on the Mount*). *Lecture:* the thesis; its critics. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
3. **Why I am now a Christian.** *Finding:* Ayaan Hirsi Ali, once an atheist campaigner, announced in 2023 that she had become a Christian because the West needed a story to defend it and she needed one too. *Read:* John 18:37–38 (point back to *The last week*) beside Acts 17:22–28 (point back). *Compare:* Hirsi Ali's essay (*UnHerd*, November 2023; our own words). *Lecture:* civilisational Christianity; her critics (who ask whether she believes it is true); her later statements. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Christ before Pilate (point back if used). *Music:* none.
4. **The machine and the holy.** *Finding:* Paul Kingsnorth, an environmentalist who became Orthodox in 2021, says the modern 'Machine' empties the world of the sacred and that only worship can resist it. *Read:* Kingsnorth (our own words; *Against the Machine*, 2025). *Compare:* Psalm 104:24–30 (point back) or Genesis 2:15. *Lecture:* technology and attention; Orthodoxy's appeal. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a wild landscape. *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
5. **The matter with things.** *Finding:* Iain McGilchrist argues that our culture's over-reliance on one mode of attention has shut us off from meaning, beauty and the divine. *Read:* McGilchrist (our own words; *The Master and His Emissary*, 2009; *The Matter with Things*, 2021). *Compare:* Pascal, *Pensées* 430. *Lecture:* hemispheres (and the science's caveats); the religious turn of his recent work. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: meaningful is not true.** *Finding:* Kaczor and Petrusek's challenge: finding the Bible meaningful is not believing it true, and Peterson's 'I act as if God exists' may be Christianity without Christ. *Read:* Chesterton, *Orthodoxy* ch. VI (Christianity's paradoxes are true or nothing). *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 15:14–19 (point back to *The last week*: if Christ has not been raised, our faith is in vain). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Kaczor and Petrusek (2021, our own words); the secular critics who say the revival is nostalgia or politics; statistics (Bible Society's *The Quiet Revival*, 2025: cite carefully, with its critics). *Debate:* Peterson; Kaczor and Petrusek; a secular critic. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Treasure in a field: the verdict.** *Finding:* Something is happening: thinking people are taking Christianity seriously again; whether it becomes faith depends on the question Pilate asked and Paul answered: is it true? *Read:* Matthew 13:44–46 recalled; Pascal, *Pensées* 425. *Compare:* Justin Brierley, *The Surprising Rebirth of Belief in God* (2023; our own words). *Lecture:* what the voices agree on and where they part (a table). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a treasure or pearl, or a field. *Music:* none.
