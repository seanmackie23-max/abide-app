**Follows:** a first-year paper in ancient political thought.

**Frame:** the world's first democracy as a laboratory: how it was built (Solon, Cleisthenes), how it worked at its best (Pericles), how it went wrong (Mytilene, the death of Socrates), and how Plato and Aristotle judged it. Then the question for us.

**Real questions:** Did the Greeks invent democracy? How did Athenian democracy work? Who could vote? Was it really a democracy if women and slaves were excluded? Why did Plato hate democracy? What did Aristotle think was the best government? What is a demagogue? What did the Bible add?

**Builds on:** *Kings and prophets* (1 Samuel 8, Deuteronomy 17: kings under law; Aristotle Politics III.7 and V.10 already read there). **Read elsewhere:** Pericles' funeral oration (*How we got here*): point back.

## Sessions

1. **Solon: law instead of strife.** *Finding:* Athens' democracy began with a lawgiver who cancelled debt-slavery and gave the poor a voice. *Read:* Plutarch, *Life of Solon* 18–25. *Compare:* Leviticus 25:8–17 (the jubilee). *Lecture:* archaic Athens, debt, Solon's poems; seisachtheia. *Learn:* new Solon fragment only if PD. *Look:* a classical lawgiver. *Music:* none fitting.
2. **How it worked.** *Finding:* Athenian democracy was direct: an assembly of citizens, officials chosen by lot, juries of hundreds, and ostracism. *Read:* Aristotle, *The Athenian Constitution* 20–22 (Cleisthenes) and 42–43. *Compare:* the ostraka evidence (archaeology, our own words). *Lecture:* who counted (about 30,000 adult male citizens out of perhaps 250,000); women, metics and slaves excluded. *Learn:* `whole-earth-their-tomb` recalled. *Look:* the Acropolis or Athens. *Music:* `tallis-fantasia`.
3. **At its best.** *Finding:* Thucydides judged that under Pericles Athens was 'in name a democracy, in fact the rule of the first citizen', and fell apart without him. *Read:* Thucydides II.65 (point back to the funeral oration). *Compare:* Proverbs 29:2 and 29:18. *Lecture:* leadership in a democracy; the plague (point ahead to *The first historians* only as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a statesman's portrait. *Music:* none.
4. **The crowd decides.** *Finding:* In the Mytilene debate the assembly voted to kill a whole city's men, then repented overnight: democracy's danger is passion without restraint. *Read:* Thucydides III.36–50. *Compare:* Exodus 23:2 ('you shall not follow a crowd to do evil'). *Lecture:* Cleon and Diodotus; demagogues; the trial of the generals after Arginusae (406). *Debate:* Cleon or Diodotus? *Learn:* new `exodus-23-2`. *Look:* a crowd. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: Plato.** *Finding:* Plato argued that democracy's love of freedom breeds disorder and ends in tyranny: rule should belong to those who know. *Read:* Plato, *Republic* VIII.557a–569c. *Compare:* the ship of state, *Republic* VI.488a–489a. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength in Plato's own terms; the death of Socrates as democracy's crime; modern echoes (Jason Brennan, *Against Democracy*, our own words). *Debate:* Plato; Pericles; Aristotle. *Learn:* `turning-of-the-soul`. *Look:* Socrates (person portrait exists as art; or a painting). *Music:* none.
6. **Aristotle: the mixed constitution.** *Finding:* Aristotle defended the wisdom of the many under law and argued for a mixed government resting on a strong middle class. *Read:* Aristotle, *Politics* III.11 and IV.11. *Compare:* Deuteronomy 17 (point back). *Lecture:* six constitutions; the rule of law (point ahead to Hall V as the hall); Polybius and the Roman republic; Madison. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Renaissance School of Athens type subject. *Music:* none.
7. **What Jerusalem added: the verdict.** *Finding:* Athens gave the West self-government; Jerusalem gave it the equal worth of every person and the king under God's law; modern democracy needs both. *Read:* Galatians 3:28 point back; read Lord Acton, 'The History of Freedom in Antiquity' (1877), the passage on Athens. *Compare:* the Mayflower Compact (1620). *Lecture:* from Athens to Westminster and Philadelphia; Tocqueville on religion and democracy (Reeve, 1835; our own words or short PD extract). *Learn:* `lincoln-gettysburg` recalled. *Look:* an American or British civic scene. *Music:* `zadok`.

**Objection:** session 5, Plato in his own words.

**Traditions:** Christian political thought briefly (Augustine, Aquinas, Calvin) as a table.
