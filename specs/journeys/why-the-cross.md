**Follows:** a paper in Christian doctrine on the atonement.

**Frame:** one event, five pictures, as the New Testament itself uses them: the sacrifice (Temple), the ransom (slave market), the victory (battlefield), the courtroom (law) and the example (the road). The traditions chose different pictures as the key; the journey shows why, and the objection asks whether any of them make sense.

**Real questions:** What does 'Jesus died for our sins' mean? Why did Jesus have to die? Why couldn't God just forgive? Who was the ransom paid to? Is it fair for an innocent person to be punished? Is the cross 'divine child abuse'? What does 'atonement' mean? What is penal substitution? How do Orthodox Christians see the cross?

**Builds on:** *The last week and the cross* (what happened), *Exodus* (Passover lamb), *Kings and prophets* (Isaiah 53).

## Sessions

1. **At-one-ment.** *Answers:* What does 'atonement' mean? What did sacrifice mean to Israel? *Finding:* The first Christians explained the cross with Israel's own pictures of sacrifice, above all the Day of Atonement and the Passover.
   *Read:* Leviticus 16:1–22 (the Day of Atonement and the scapegoat). *Compare:* Hebrews 9:11–28.
   *Lecture:* sacrifice in Israel and the ancient world; Tyndale's coinage 'at-one-ment'; Yom Kippur today. *Debate:* is sacrifice a primitive idea?
   *Learn:* `isaiah-53-5` recalled; new `hebrews-9-28`. *Look:* the sacrifice of Isaac (as a type) or the Lamb of God. *Music:* `ave-verum`.
2. **A ransom for many.** *Answers:* Who was the ransom paid to? What does 'redemption' mean? *Finding:* Jesus spoke of giving his life as a ransom; the early Church asked to whom, and some answered the devil.
   *Read:* Mark 10:35–45. *Compare:* Gregory of Nyssa, *Great Catechism* 22–24 (the fish-hook) (NPNF II.5).
   *Lecture:* redemption in the slave market and the Exodus; the ransom theory and Gregory of Nazianzus's rejection of it (Oration 45.22). *Debate:* a metaphor or a transaction?
   *Learn:* new `mark-10-45`. *Look:* Christ washing feet, or the release of captives. *Music:* `sacred-head`.
3. **Victory: Christ the conqueror.** *Answers:* How do Orthodox Christians see the cross? What is 'Christus Victor'? *Finding:* For the early Church and the East the cross is first a victory over sin, death and the devil, and the incarnation and the cross are one rescue.
   *Read:* Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 6–10. *Compare:* Colossians 2:13–15 and Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.1 (recapitulation).
   *Lecture:* Irenaeus and Athanasius; the harrowing of hell; Easter icons; Gustaf Aulén's *Christus Victor* (our own words). *Debate:* is the cross a victory or a payment?
   *Learn:* `donne-death-be-not-proud`. *Look:* the Harrowing of Hell (Anastasis). *Music:* `rachmaninoff-vigil`.
4. **Satisfaction: Anselm.** *Answers:* Why couldn't God just forgive? What did Anselm argue? *Finding:* Anselm argued that sin dishonours God infinitely and only one who is both God and man could make it right: the most influential argument in the West.
   *Read:* Anselm, *Why God Became Man* I.11–15, II.6. *Compare:* Abelard on love as the cross's effect (Exposition of Romans; in our own words if no PD English).
   *Lecture:* medieval honour and debt; Anselm's dialogue form; Abelard's 'moral influence' view; Aquinas's synthesis. *Debate:* Anselm against Abelard.
   *Learn:* new `romans-5-8`. *Look:* a crucifixion with a donor kneeling, or the Man of Sorrows. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
5. **In our place: the Reformers.** *Answers:* What is penal substitution? Is it fair for an innocent person to be punished? *Finding:* The Reformers read the cross through the courtroom: Christ bore the penalty in our place, so that God is both just and the justifier.
   *Read:* Romans 3:21–26 and 2 Corinthians 5:14–21. *Compare:* Calvin, *Institutes* II.16.5–7 (Beveridge).
   *Lecture:* justification (point ahead to *The Reformation* only as the hall); Luther's 'happy exchange'; the debate within Protestantism. *Debate:* Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox side by side.
   *Learn:* new `2-corinthians-5-21`. *Look:* a crucifixion with the Law and Gospel theme. *Music:* `in-christ-alone`.
6. **The case against: can a death save anyone?** *Answers:* Is it 'divine child abuse'? Is it moral to punish the innocent? Why would God need blood? *Finding:* The objection is strongest against a crude picture of an angry Father and an innocent victim; each tradition answers that Father and Son act as one, freely, out of love.
   *Read:* Thomas Paine, *The Age of Reason* Part I (on redemption and the 'pecuniary idea'). *Compare:* 1 Peter 2:21–25.
   *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Paine; Kant on non-transferable guilt (*Religion within the Bounds*, Greene and Hudson 1934 is copyright: our own words); modern feminist critiques (our own words); René Girard's scapegoat (our own words). *Debate:* Paine; Girard; Fleming Rutledge (our own words).
   *Learn:* `romans-nothing-can-separate-us`. *Look:* Abraham and Isaac (pointing back) or the Lamb. *Music:* `faure-requiem`.
7. **Many pictures, one love: the verdict.** *Answers:* So why the cross? Which picture is right? *Finding:* The New Testament keeps all the pictures because no one picture holds it all; together they say that God took the worst of the world into himself to heal it.
   *Read:* John 3:14–17. *Compare:* George Herbert, 'The Sacrifice' (1633), selected stanzas.
   *Lecture:* the pictures side by side as a table; how they show in hymns and art; what it means to live under the cross. *Debate:* none; the verdict.
   *Learn:* `john-3-16`. *Look:* the Crucifixion with the Virgin and St John. *Music:* `sacred-head`.

**Objection:** session 6, in Paine's and modern critics' terms.

**Traditions:** the five pictures and which each tradition makes central (Orthodox victory and theosis, Catholic satisfaction and sacrifice of the Mass, Reformed penal substitution, liberal moral influence); Jewish views of atonement without sacrifice since AD 70.

**Voices:** Gustaf Aulén; John Stott, *The Cross of Christ*; Fleming Rutledge, *The Crucifixion*; René Girard; Kant (our own words).

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Assess Anselm's argument'; 'Is penal substitution morally defensible?'; 'Why does the New Testament use so many images?'.
