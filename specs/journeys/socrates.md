**Follows:** a first-year philosophy paper on Socrates and the early dialogues.

**Frame:** a trial in 399 BC: the charges, the defence, the verdict, the prison and the last day; at each step, what Socrates did (asking questions) and why it got him killed. The examined life, tested by death.

**Real questions:** Who was Socrates? Why was he put to death? What is the Socratic method? What did he mean by 'I know that I know nothing'? What is the examined life? Why didn't he escape from prison? Did Socrates believe in God? Is Socrates like Jesus? Why didn't he write anything down?

**Builds on:** *Democracy* (the city that killed him), *Death and hope* (the end of the *Phaedo*, already read). **Read elsewhere:** *Phaedo* 114d–118a (*Death and hope*); *Apology* 40c–42a is the memory `hour-of-departure`; *Euthyphro* is kept for *Right and wrong*.

## Sessions

1. **The gadfly of Athens.** *Finding:* Socrates was a stonemason's son who wrote nothing and spent his life questioning people in the market place; we know him through three very different witnesses. *Read:* Xenophon, *Memorabilia* I.1. *Compare:* Aristophanes, *Clouds* 218–256 (the comic Socrates in his basket). *Lecture:* the Socratic problem (Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes); Athens after the war; the Thirty Tyrants. *Learn:* `unexamined-life`. *Look:* Socrates (the David portrait exists as `person-socrates`; or a painting). *Music:* none.
2. **I know that I do not know.** *Finding:* The oracle called Socrates the wisest man; he concluded his wisdom was knowing he knew nothing, and set out to test everyone who claimed to know. *Read:* *Apology* 17a–24b. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 1:18–25 (the foolishness of God). *Lecture:* the oracle at Delphi; 'know thyself'; Socratic ignorance. *Learn:* new `apology-21d` (Jowett). *Look:* Delphi or a seer. *Music:* none.
3. **The method.** *Finding:* Socratic questioning (the *elenchus*) shows people they do not know what they think they know, and the *Meno* suggests learning is drawing out what is already within. *Read:* *Meno* 80a–86c (the slave boy). *Compare:* Augustine, *The Teacher* (point to Augustine's inner teacher in our own words) or Proverbs 20:5. *Lecture:* the elenchus step by step; recollection; Socratic teaching today. *Learn:* new `proverbs-20-5`. *Look:* a teacher with a pupil. *Music:* none.
4. **The defence.** *Finding:* On trial for impiety and corrupting the young, Socrates refused to stop philosophising or beg for mercy: he would obey God rather than Athens. *Read:* *Apology* 28b–31c (the gadfly, 'I shall obey God rather than you'). *Compare:* Acts 5:29 (point back to *Tragedy*) or Daniel 3:16–18. *Lecture:* the charges and the vote (280 to 220, then the penalty); Socrates' *daimonion*. *Learn:* `hour-of-departure`. *Look:* Socrates before his judges. *Music:* none.
5. **Why he did not run.** *Finding:* In prison, offered escape, Socrates let the Laws of Athens argue that a citizen who has accepted their protection must not break them. *Read:* *Crito* 47a–54e. *Compare:* Romans 13:1–7 (and Antigone, point back). *Lecture:* civil obedience and civil disobedience (point ahead to Hall VII's King only as the hall); the social contract's first form. *Debate:* was Socrates right to stay? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a prison scene. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: was Socrates a danger?** *Finding:* Athens had reasons: his pupils included the traitor Alcibiades and the tyrant Critias, and his questioning dissolved the city's beliefs without replacing them; the charge deserves a hearing. *Read:* Aristophanes, *Clouds* 1476–1510 (the school burned). *Compare:* Nietzsche, *Twilight of the Idols*, 'The Problem of Socrates' (Ludovici, 1911). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: I. F. Stone, *The Trial of Socrates* (1988, our own words); Nietzsche; Hegel on Socrates and the city. *Debate:* the prosecution; Plato; Nietzsche. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Alcibiades or a symposium. *Music:* none.
7. **Practising death: the verdict.** *Finding:* Socrates said philosophy is practice for dying, and the soul's care the only thing that matters; the Church Fathers called him a Christian before Christ, while noting what he did not have. *Read:* *Phaedo* 57a–69e. *Compare:* Justin Martyr, *Second Apology* 10 (Socrates and the Logos). *Lecture:* Socrates and Jesus compared (a table: both taught orally, both were tried, both died; the differences); Erasmus's 'Saint Socrates, pray for us'. *Learn:* `death-of-socrates`. *Look:* the Death of Socrates (point back if used). *Music:* `faure-requiem`.
