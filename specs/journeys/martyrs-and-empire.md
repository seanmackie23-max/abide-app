**Follows:** a first-year paper in early church history.

**Frame:** two hundred and fifty years from Nero to Constantine, told through the people who died and the officials who killed them: why Rome persecuted (religion and loyalty were one), how Christians died (Polycarp, Perpetua), what they claimed to be (Diognetus), and how it ended in a vision and an edict, which raised a new problem.

**Real questions:** Why did the Romans persecute Christians? How many were killed? Were Christians fed to lions? Who were Polycarp and Perpetua? What is a martyr? Why did Constantine become a Christian? What was the Edict of Milan? Was Constantine's conversion good for the Church? Are Christians persecuted today?

**Builds on:** *First Christians* (Pliny, read); *What is the Bible?* (Tacitus, read). **Read elsewhere:** Pliny, Tacitus; Tertullian, *Apology* 39 (*First Christians*).

## Sessions

1. **A fiery trial.** *Finding:* From Nero in AD 64 Christians could be killed for the name alone; the New Testament already speaks to a persecuted church. *Read:* 1 Peter 4:12–19 and Revelation 2:8–11 (to Smyrna). *Compare:* Tacitus (point back). *Lecture:* why Rome persecuted: the gods and the safety of the city; 'atheism'; local and empire-wide persecution. *Learn:* new `revelation-2-10`. *Look:* a Roman arena or Christian martyrs. *Music:* `for-all-the-saints`.
2. **Eighty-six years.** *Finding:* Old Bishop Polycarp refused to curse Christ: 'eighty-six years have I served him and he has done me no wrong'. *Read:* *Martyrdom of Polycarp* 9–16. *Compare:* Ignatius, *To the Romans* 4 ('I am the wheat of God') (ANF). *Lecture:* Smyrna; the first account of a martyrdom; relics and saints' days begin. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a martyr bishop. *Music:* none.
3. **Perpetua's diary.** *Finding:* A young mother of twenty-two wrote her own account in prison: the earliest surviving writing by a Christian woman. *Read:* *Passion of Perpetua* 3–6 and 18–21. *Compare:* Romans 8:35–39 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* Carthage 203; Perpetua and Felicity, free and slave; her father; visions. *Learn:* `romans-nothing-can-separate-us`. *Look:* female martyrs (St Catherine, St Agnes). *Music:* none.
4. **Resident aliens.** *Finding:* Christians said they lived in their own countries as foreigners: loyal, law-abiding, but with a citizenship in heaven. *Read:* *Letter to Diognetus* 5–6. *Compare:* Philippians 3:20 and Jeremiah 29:7 (point back). *Lecture:* Christian loyalty and refusal; prayers for the emperor; the army question. *Learn:* `jeremiah-29-7`. *Look:* an early Christian community. *Music:* none.
5. **The blood of the martyrs.** *Finding:* Tertullian told the persecutors that the more they mowed Christians down, the more they grew: martyrdom became witness. *Read:* Tertullian, *Apology* 50. *Compare:* Lactantius, *Deaths of the Persecutors* 12–13 (the Great Persecution begins, 303). *Lecture:* Decius (250), Valerian, Diocletian; numbers (thousands, not millions: Candida Moss, our own words, and her critics); the lapsed and Cyprian. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Sebastian or St Lawrence. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: myths of martyrdom?** *Finding:* Some modern historians argue the persecution story was exaggerated to build Christian identity; the evidence shows real, sporadic, sometimes savage persecution, and later legends added to it. *Read:* Eusebius, *Church History* VIII.6–9 (the Great Persecution). *Compare:* Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* ch. XVI (the persecutions 'exaggerated'). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Gibbon; Candida Moss, *The Myth of Persecution* (our own words); replies (Ste. Croix, Barnes, our own words). *Debate:* Gibbon; Moss; Eusebius. *Learn:* none. *Look:* a martyrdom scene. *Music:* none.
7. **In this sign: the verdict.** *Finding:* Constantine's vision before the Milvian Bridge (312) and the Edict of Milan (313) ended persecution; the Church won freedom and gained a new danger: power. *Read:* Lactantius, *Deaths of the Persecutors* 44 and 48; Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* I.28–31. *Compare:* the Donatist schism or Ambrose barring Theodosius (point ahead to Hall V as the hall). *Lecture:* was Constantine sincere?; Christendom begins; persecution of Christians today (Open Doors figures, cited, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Constantine or the Chi-Rho. *Music:* `zadok`.

**Objection:** session 6, in Gibbon's and Moss's terms.

**Traditions:** veneration of martyrs and saints (Catholic, Orthodox) and Protestant martyrologies (Foxe) side by side.

**Voices:** Candida Moss; G. E. M. de Ste. Croix; T. D. Barnes; Robert Louis Wilken.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Why did Rome persecute?'; 'Was Constantine a Christian?'; 'Did persecution help the Church grow?'.
