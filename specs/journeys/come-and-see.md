**Follows:** a capstone: a verdict across all the halls.

**Frame:** Philip's invitation, 'come and see', as the method of the whole app: the journey gathers the evidence from every hall into one case, hears the case for staying agnostic at full strength, and ends with an honest invitation and a first step, never pressure. The last journey in Rose Window. Its practice is a first step: a visit to church and a week of the daily rhythm.

**Real questions:** Is Christianity true? How do I know? What if I have doubts? Can I be a Christian with doubts? How do people become Christians? What do I have to believe? What is the first step? Do I have to go to church? What if I'm not ready? Why not just stay agnostic?

**Builds on:** everything. **Read elsewhere:** Luke 24 (*What is the Bible?*, *The whole story*), Acts 17 (*Is there a God?*), Augustine *Confessions* VIII (*Augustine*), Pascal's wager (*Pascal*).

## Sessions

1. **Come and see.** *Finding:* The first disciples were not argued into faith but invited to come and see, and they found more than they expected. *Read:* John 1:35–51. *Compare:* Luke 24:13–35 (point back). *Lecture:* the method of the app; what 'faith' means here (trust, point back). *Learn:* new `john-1-39`. *Look:* the calling of the apostles (the Duccio is used: another). *Music:* none.
2. **The case so far.** *Finding:* Across the halls, the case for Christianity rests on several strands at once: a world that makes sense, the moral law, the person of Jesus, the resurrection evidence, the lives it has changed, and its account of beauty and dignity. *Read:* none new; the 'case so far' gathered from each hall (our own words, pointing back). *Compare:* Pascal, *Pensées* 194 (on those who seek and those who do not). *Lecture:* cumulative case (Basil Mitchell, Richard Swinburne, our own words); a table of the strands with the journeys that teach them. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
3. **The case against, again.** *Finding:* The strongest reasons against have also been heard in every hall: suffering, hiddenness, science, history, the Church's crimes; they too form a cumulative case. *Read:* none new; the objections gathered (our own words, pointing back). *Compare:* Mark 9:24. *Lecture:* a table of the objections and the replies given. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
4. **The case for staying agnostic.** *Finding:* Huxley coined 'agnostic' for the honest refusal to claim knowledge beyond the evidence; many thoughtful people stay there. *Read:* Huxley, 'Agnosticism' (1889). *Compare:* Pascal, *Pensées* 229 (on indifference) and 233 (point back). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: suspension of judgement as intellectual virtue (Huxley; Clifford point back; modern agnostics); replies (James's forced option; Pascal; Newman's *Grammar of Assent*, our own words). *Debate:* Huxley; Newman; James. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a philosopher in thought. *Music:* none.
5. **How people come.** *Finding:* People come to faith by many roads: argument (Lewis), longing (Augustine, point back), a person (Newman), crisis, beauty, a church; most say it was gradual. *Read:* Newman, *Apologia*, the last pages. *Compare:* Lewis, *Surprised by Joy* (our own words: 'the most dejected and reluctant convert in all England', short quotation). *Lecture:* conversion stories (point back to Practice's greats). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the conversion of St Paul. *Music:* none.
6. **To whom would we go?** *Finding:* When many left Jesus, Peter said 'Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life': faith often begins as the best answer available, not a certainty. *Read:* John 6:66–69. *Compare:* Mark 9:24 recalled. *Lecture:* doubt and faith; what Christians must believe (the creeds, point back). *Learn:* new `john-6-68`. *Look:* St Peter. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.
7. **A first step: the verdict.** *Finding:* Rose Window cannot tell you whether it is true; it can only show you the evidence, the objections and the life, and invite you to take one step: a week of the daily rhythm and one visit to a church. *Read:* Luke 24:28–35 (point back) or Revelation 3:20. *Compare:* Augustine, *Confessions* I.1 (point back). *Lecture:* first steps (pray, read a Gospel, go to a service, talk to a Christian); honest, never pressure. **Make the case** (ends Hall IX and the whole curriculum). *Learn:* `augustine-our-heart-is-restless`. *Look:* an open door or a church at evening. *Music:* `abide-with-me`.

**Objection:** session 4, the case for staying agnostic.
