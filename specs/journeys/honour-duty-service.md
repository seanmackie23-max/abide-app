**Follows:** a paper in ethics (virtue and public life).

**Frame:** three portraits of the noble person: Cicero's Roman (duty to country, keeping your word), Ambrose's Christian (the same virtues for servants of the poor), and Newman's gentleman (who never inflicts pain, and why that is not enough). Then the hard case: does honour still mean anything, or has the word become embarrassing?

**Real questions:** What is honour? Is honour still a thing? What is duty? What makes someone a gentleman or a lady? What is noblesse oblige? What is servant leadership? Can you be ambitious and humble? How did Christianity change the idea of a great man? What does 'decorum' mean?

**Builds on:** *Rome* (Cicero, *pietas*), *Homer* (glory), *Sermon on the Mount*. **Read elsewhere:** Mark 10:42–45 (*Why the cross?*); John 13 (*The last week*).

## Sessions

1. **The Roman's duty.** *Finding:* Cicero wrote his son a handbook on duty: what is honourable is always truly useful, and justice comes before profit. *Read:* Cicero, *On Duties* I.4–17. *Compare:* Proverbs 22:1. *Lecture:* Cicero's life and death; the four cardinal virtues (point ahead to Hall IX as the hall); *honestum* and *utile*. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a Roman portrait bust or statesman. *Music:* none.
2. **Regulus keeps his word.** *Finding:* The Roman ideal was the man who goes back to torture rather than break an oath: honour as faithfulness. *Read:* *On Duties* III.99–115. *Compare:* Psalm 15:4 ('who keeps an oath even when it hurts'). *Lecture:* oath and promise; Roman exempla; Thomas More (point ahead to Hall VI as the hall). *Learn:* new `psalm-15-4`. *Look:* Regulus departing (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
3. **Decorum.** *Finding:* Cicero taught that a good person acts fittingly, with self-control in word, dress and manner: manners as the visible form of virtue. *Read:* *On Duties* I.93–99. *Compare:* Washington, *Rules of Civility* 1–30. *Lecture:* decorum; Washington copying the rules as a boy; courtesy and respect. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of a statesman (Gilbert Stuart's Washington at NGA). *Music:* none.
4. **The greatest is the servant.** *Finding:* Jesus turned the ancient idea of greatness upside down: the greatest is the one who serves. *Read:* Philippians 2:3–8. *Compare:* Mark 10:42–45 recalled (point back). *Lecture:* *megalopsychia* (Aristotle's great-souled man) beside Christian humility; servant leadership (Robert Greenleaf, our own words). *Learn:* `philippians-2-5`. *Look:* Christ washing the feet. *Music:* `ubi-caritas`.
5. **Cicero baptised.** *Finding:* Ambrose of Milan rewrote Cicero's *On Duties* for his clergy, keeping the virtues and giving them Christ's reasons and the poor's claims. *Read:* Ambrose, *On the Duties of the Clergy* I.24–27. *Compare:* Ambrose melting church plate to ransom captives (II.28). *Lecture:* Ambrose (governor turned bishop); duties to the poor; the Christian gentleman's roots. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* St Ambrose. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: honour's dark side.** *Finding:* Honour has killed: duels, honour killings, wars of pride; modern critics say it is vanity dressed up, and Christianity itself said pride is the first sin. *Read:* Falstaff on honour (*Henry IV Part 1*, V.1.127–141). *Compare:* Burke, *Reflections*, the lament for 'the age of chivalry' (1790). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Hobbes on honour as power; Falstaff's catechism; Kwame Anthony Appiah, *The Honor Code* (our own words, how honour ended duelling and footbinding). *Debate:* Falstaff; Burke; Appiah. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a duel or a knight. *Music:* none.
7. **The gentleman, and the verdict.** *Finding:* Newman praised the gentleman who never inflicts pain, then warned that good manners without grace can be 'the beauty of the marble'; a noble life today is duty and courtesy rooted in service. *Read:* Newman, *The Idea of a University*, Discourse VIII.10. *Compare:* Micah 6:8 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* nobility today: public service, military, medicine, teaching; what the greats in Practice did (point to Practice). *Learn:* `micah-what-does-the-lord-require`. *Look:* a portrait of quiet dignity. *Music:* `tallis-fantasia`.

**Objection:** session 6, honour as vanity and violence, in Falstaff's and Hobbes's terms.
