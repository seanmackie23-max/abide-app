**Follows:** a first-year philosophy paper on Plato's metaphysics and psychology.

**Frame:** the cave: prisoners who take shadows for reality, one who is freed and climbs to the sun, and returns. Each session is a step of the climb: shadows (what seems), the forms (what is), the soul (who climbs), the Good (the sun), the return (the just city), the objection (is there a sun?), and what Christians did with it.

**Real questions:** What is Plato's cave? What are the forms? Did Plato believe in God? What is the soul? What are the three parts of the soul? What is justice in the Republic? Was Plato a totalitarian? What did Christians take from Plato? What is Platonic love? How is Plato different from Aristotle?

**Builds on:** *Socrates*; *Dust that wonders* (the charioteer, read), *Why beauty matters* (the ladder of love, read). **Read elsewhere:** *Phaedrus* 246a–248b (*Dust that wonders*), *Symposium* 210a–212b (*Why beauty matters*), *Republic* III.398c–402a (*The sound of faith*), *Republic* II ring of Gyges (*Kings and prophets*), *Timaeus* (*Number and nature*).

## Sessions

1. **The cave.** *Finding:* Plato pictured us as prisoners taking shadows for reality: education is turning the whole soul towards the light. *Read:* *Republic* VII.514a–521b. *Compare:* John 3:19–21 or 2 Corinthians 4:6. *Lecture:* Plato's life and the Academy; the dialogue form; the cave step by step. *Learn:* `turning-of-the-soul`. *Look:* a figure emerging into light, or a lantern scene. *Music:* `o-radiant-dawn`.
2. **The forms.** *Finding:* Plato argued that beyond changing things are unchanging forms, Justice itself, Beauty itself, which the mind knows and the senses only glimpse. *Read:* *Phaedo* 72e–77a (recollection and equality itself). *Compare:* Hebrews 8:5 and 11:1–3 (shadow and substance). *Lecture:* the theory of forms; the divided line (*Republic* VI.509d–511e); problems. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a geometric or idealised subject. *Music:* none.
3. **The soul in three parts.** *Finding:* Justice in the soul is reason ruling spirit and appetite, as justice in the city is each class doing its work. *Read:* *Republic* IV.434d–441c. *Compare:* Romans 7:15–25 (point ahead to *The line through the heart* as the hall only). *Lecture:* reason, spirit, appetite; the charioteer recalled (point back); the four virtues (point ahead to Hall IX as the hall). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a struggle between reason and passion (Hercules at the crossroads). *Music:* none.
4. **The sun and the Good.** *Finding:* The highest form, the Good, is like the sun: it makes things knowable and gives them being, and is 'beyond being'. *Read:* *Republic* VI.507b–509c. *Compare:* James 1:17 and 1 John 1:5 (point back if used in *Light, stone and glass*). *Lecture:* the Good and God; Plotinus and the One (our own words, MacKenna 1917–30 partly PD); 'Platonic' theism. *Learn:* new `1-john-1-5` if not used. *Look:* the sun, a sunrise landscape. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
5. **The just city.** *Finding:* Plato's Republic imagines a city ruled by philosopher-kings, with guardians sharing property and families: an ideal that has inspired and horrified readers. *Read:* *Republic* V.473c–e and VII.519c–521b (the return to the cave). *Compare:* Acts 4:32 (point back). *Lecture:* the guardians, the noble lie, women as guardians; Plato's Sicilian adventures. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* an ideal city. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: Aristotle and Popper.** *Finding:* Aristotle said the forms explain nothing; Karl Popper said the Republic is a blueprint for tyranny; both charges must be faced. *Read:* Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.9 (against the forms). *Compare:* Popper, *The Open Society and Its Enemies* (1945; our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength; the third man argument; the replies (Plato's own *Parmenides*; Popper's critics). *Debate:* Plato; Aristotle; Popper. *Learn:* none. *Look:* a philosopher in thought. *Music:* none.
7. **Plato baptised: the verdict.** *Finding:* Augustine said the Platonists had seen the homeland from far off but not the way there: Christianity took Plato's sun and gave it a face. *Read:* Augustine, *Confessions* VII.9–10. *Compare:* the myth of Er, *Republic* X.614b–621d (the soul's journey and choice). *Lecture:* Platonism in Christianity (Augustine, Pseudo-Dionysius, Boethius, the Cambridge Platonists, C. S. Lewis's Narnia ending 'all in Plato', our own words); what Christianity rejected (the body as prison). *Learn:* `augustine-our-heart-is-restless`. *Look:* St Augustine. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.

**Objection:** session 6, Aristotle's and Popper's critiques.

**Traditions:** Platonism's influence on Orthodox (Dionysius), Catholic (Augustine) and Protestant thought, side by side.

**Voices:** Karl Popper; Iris Murdoch, *The Sovereignty of Good*; Julia Annas; Myles Burnyeat; Plotinus (MacKenna, partly PD: confirm volumes before 1929).

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Explain the cave'; 'Is Plato's Republic totalitarian?'; 'What did Augustine take from Plato?'.
