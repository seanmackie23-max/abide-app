**Follows:** a first-year classics paper on Greek historiography.

**Frame:** two inventors of history: Herodotus, who asked why the Greeks and Persians fought and listened to everyone, and Thucydides, who asked what power does to people and trusted only what he could check. The journey ends with Melos: 'the strong do what they can'.

**Real questions:** Who was the first historian? Why is Herodotus called the father of history, and of lies? What were the Persian Wars? What is the Melian dialogue? Is might right? What did Thucydides learn from the plague? How do we know ancient history is true? How is Greek history different from the Bible's history?

**Builds on:** *Democracy and its dangers*; *What is the Bible?* (Luke's method). **Read elsewhere:** Thucydides II.37–45 (*How we got here*), II.65 (*Democracy*), III.36–50 (*Democracy*).

## Sessions

1. **Why did they fight?** *Finding:* Herodotus set out to keep great deeds from being forgotten and to find their causes: history begins as an inquiry (*historia*). *Read:* Herodotus I.1–5. *Compare:* Luke 1:1–4 (point back). *Lecture:* Herodotus of Halicarnassus; the Persian Empire; how he gathered stories. *Learn:* new `herodotus-1-1`. *Look:* a map or Persian art (NGA: flag). *Music:* none.
2. **Call no man happy.** *Finding:* Solon told the richest king alive to call no man happy until he is dead; Croesus learned it on the pyre. *Read:* Herodotus I.29–33 and 86–88. *Compare:* Ecclesiastes 9:11–12 (point back to *Psalms and wisdom*) or Luke 12:16–21. *Lecture:* *hybris* and *nemesis*; providence in Herodotus. *Learn:* new `luke-12-20`. *Look:* a king on a pyre, or a vanitas. *Music:* none.
3. **Free men and a king.** *Finding:* At Thermopylae the Greeks fought as free men under law, not slaves under a master: Herodotus made freedom the meaning of the war. *Read:* Herodotus VII.101–104 (Xerxes and Demaratus) and VII.44–52. *Compare:* Exodus 14 (point back). *Lecture:* Marathon, Thermopylae, Salamis; how reliable is Herodotus? (the 'father of lies' charge and modern archaeology). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a battle scene or Thermopylae. *Music:* none.
4. **A possession for ever.** *Finding:* Thucydides wanted the exact truth, checked against witnesses, so that his work would be 'a possession for all time'. *Read:* Thucydides I.20–23. *Compare:* Herodotus II.123 ('I write what I am told'). *Lecture:* Thucydides' method; the speeches; the Peloponnesian War outline. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scholar or historian at work. *Music:* none.
5. **The plague.** *Finding:* When plague struck Athens, law and religion collapsed: Thucydides recorded how quickly a civilisation forgets itself. *Read:* Thucydides II.47–54. *Compare:* Dionysius of Alexandria's account of Christians in the plague of 260 (Eusebius VII.22). *Lecture:* the plague's medicine (typhus? typhoid? our own words); the moral collapse; Christian care in later plagues (point back to *First Christians*). *Learn:* `psalm-91-1`. *Look:* a plague or sickbed scene. *Music:* `mozart-requiem`.
6. **The case against: the strong do what they can.** *Finding:* At Melos Athens told a small neutral island that justice exists only between equals: Thucydides gives power's case at full strength and lets the reader judge. *Read:* Thucydides V.84–116 (the Melian dialogue). *Compare:* 1 Kings 21 (Naboth's vineyard, point back) or Isaiah 10:5–15. *Lecture:* realism from Thucydides to Hobbes and modern international relations; the objection: is history only power? *Debate:* the Athenians; the Melians; the prophets. *Learn:* `isaiah-2-4` recalled. *Look:* a ruined city. *Music:* none.
7. **What history teaches: the verdict.** *Finding:* The Greeks taught the West to investigate and to see how power corrupts; the Bible added that history has a direction and the weak are seen by God. *Read:* Thucydides VII.84–87 (the destruction of the Athenian army in Sicily). *Compare:* Augustine, *City of God* IV.4 ('without justice, what are kingdoms but great robberies?'). *Lecture:* Greek and biblical history side by side (a table); history as a discipline. *Learn:* new `city-of-god-robberies` (Dods). *Look:* a classical ruin by moonlight. *Music:* `brahms-requiem`.

**Objection:** session 6, the Athenian envoys at Melos.
