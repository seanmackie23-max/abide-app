**Follows:** a paper in Christian ethics (love, sexuality and marriage).

**Frame:** Lewis's four loves as the map: affection, friendship, eros, and charity (point back), with friendship (Aristotle, Cicero, David and Jonathan, Aelred), eros and marriage (Genesis, the vows, Ephesians), singleness and celibacy, then the hardest questions set out fairly, then the verdict. Sets out the traditions on sexuality fairly; needs a pastor's review.

**Real questions:** What are the four loves? What is friendship? Why is friendship so hard as an adult? What is marriage for? What does the Bible say about marriage? What does 'wives submit' mean? Why does the Church care about sex? What about divorce? What do Christians think about same-sex relationships? Is singleness second best? What are the marriage vows?

**Builds on:** *Aristotle*, *Faith, hope and love* (agape), *Psalms and wisdom* (Song of Songs). **Read elsewhere:** Genesis 2 (*In the beginning*, *Dust that wonders*), Song of Songs (*Psalms and wisdom*).

## Sessions

1. **Four loves.** *Finding:* Greek had several words for love; Lewis described four: affection, friendship, eros and charity, each good and each dangerous when it becomes a god. *Read:* point back to 1 Corinthians 13; read Lewis (our own words) beside 1 John 4:19. *Compare:* Plato's ladder (point back). *Lecture:* storge, philia, eros, agape; 'love begins to be a demon the moment it begins to be a god' (short quotation). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a family scene. *Music:* none.
2. **Friendship.** *Finding:* Aristotle said the best friendships are between good people who love each other for their goodness, and that no one would choose to live without friends. *Read:* *Nicomachean Ethics* VIII.1–4. *Compare:* Cicero, *On Friendship* 20–27. *Lecture:* friendships of use, pleasure and virtue; loneliness today (figures, cited). *Learn:* `one-swallow` recalled; new none. *Look:* two friends. *Music:* none.
3. **Spiritual friendship.** *Finding:* The Bible's great friendship is David and Jonathan; a medieval abbot, Aelred, wrote that 'God is friendship'. *Read:* 1 Samuel 18:1–4 and 2 Samuel 1:25–27. *Compare:* Aelred (our own words) and John 15:12–15. *Lecture:* friendship in the monasteries; Jesus's friends; modern readings of David and Jonathan fairly. *Learn:* `john-15-13` (if new elsewhere). *Look:* David and Jonathan. *Music:* none.
4. **What marriage is for.** *Finding:* The Christian tradition saw marriage as a covenant for the good of the spouses, children and society, and a sign of Christ and the Church. *Read:* the BCP Solemnization (the preface and vows). *Compare:* Mark 10:2–12. *Lecture:* Augustine's three goods; Jewish marriage; the vows explained line by line; divorce in the traditions side by side. *Learn:* new `bcp-marriage-vow` (PD). *Look:* a wedding. *Music:* none.
5. **Mutual submission.** *Finding:* 'Wives, submit' is preceded by 'submit to one another', and husbands are told to love as Christ gave himself up: a radical text in its world, a contested one in ours. *Read:* Ephesians 5:21–33. *Compare:* Aristotle on the household (point back) and Plutarch's *Advice to Bride and Groom* (Babbitt 1928: Frank Cole Babbitt died 1935: qualifies) 33. *Lecture:* complementarian and egalitarian readings side by side; domestic abuse is never sanctioned. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a married couple. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: does the Church have anything to say about sex?** *Finding:* Modern critics say Christian sexual ethics are repressive, harmful to gay people and out of date; the traditions disagree among themselves; the case must be set out fairly. *Read:* 1 Corinthians 7:1–9 and 32–35 (Paul on marriage and singleness). *Compare:* Mill, *The Subjection of Women* (1869) ch. 2. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength; the traditions' positions on sexuality side by side (Catholic, Orthodox, conservative and progressive Protestant), with their reasons and their critics, in our own words; singleness and celibacy as vocations. *Debate:* a progressive and a traditional Christian position, and a secular critic. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **Love that lasts: the verdict.** *Finding:* All the loves are good and need charity to save them; friendship, marriage and singleness are each ways of learning to love as God loves. *Read:* Song of Songs 8:6–7 (point back to memory). *Compare:* Shakespeare, Sonnet 116 (memory). *Lecture:* practices for friendship and marriage. *Learn:* `shakespeare-sonnet-116`. *Look:* an elderly couple. *Music:* `ubi-caritas`.

**Objection:** session 6.

**Traditions:** marriage, divorce, contraception and sexuality side by side (Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, evangelical, progressive Protestant), fairly.
