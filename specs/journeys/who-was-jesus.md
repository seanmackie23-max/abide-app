**Follows:** a first-year paper on the figure of Jesus (the historical Jesus and Christology).

**Frame:** a trial with four witnesses: the world he was born into, what he said about himself, what he did, and what the prophets said to expect. The last week, the cross and the resurrection now belong to the next journeys; this one asks who he claimed to be.

**Real questions:** Did Jesus exist? When and where was he born? Did he say he was God? What does 'Son of Man' mean? What does 'Christ' mean? Did he do miracles? Did he fulfil prophecy? Was he just a good teacher? What do Muslims and Jews think of him?

**Builds on:** the Old Testament hall (the promise, the kings, the servant of Isaiah 53 in *Kings and prophets*). **Read elsewhere:** Josephus and Tacitus (*What is the Bible?*); Luke 2 (*Time made holy*); Mark 1 (current *Who was Jesus?* session 1: keep, re-shaped); Mark 15, 1 Corinthians 15 and John 20 move to *The last week and the cross*; Pliny moves to *The first Christians*.

## Sessions

1. **The world he was born into.** *Answers:* When and where? What was Judea like under Rome? Why Greek? *Finding:* Jesus was a Galilean Jew under Roman rule in a Greek-speaking empire, born around 6–4 BC: everything he said must be heard there.
   *Read:* Mark 1:1–45 (kept from the current journey). *Compare:* Matthew 2:1–12 (the Magi; Matthew 1:22–23 is read in *The whole story*: point back), set beside Luke's (pointing back).
   *Lecture:* Herod, Augustus and Quirinius (the census problem, fairly); Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealots; the hope of a Messiah; Greek and Aramaic. *Debate:* how much of the birth stories is history?
   *Learn:* `lords`. *Look:* the Nativity or the Magi. *Music:* `o-come-emmanuel`.
2. **What he said about himself.** *Answers:* Did Jesus say he was God? What does 'Son of Man' mean? *Finding:* Jesus rarely said 'I am God' in those words, but he forgave sins, called himself the Son of Man of Daniel 7, and spoke with an authority no prophet claimed.
   *Read:* Mark 2:1–12 and Mark 14:53–65. *Compare:* Daniel 7:9–14.
   *Lecture:* Son of Man, Son of God, Messiah, 'I say to you', 'Abba'; John's 'I am' sayings and how they differ from Mark's; the criteria historians use. *Debate:* high Christology from the start (Bauckham, Hurtado) or a later development (Ehrman), our own words.
   *Learn:* `matthew-11-28`. *Look:* Christ healing or teaching. *Music:* `jesu-joy`.
3. **Who do you say I am?** *Answers:* What does 'Christ' mean? Why did he tell people to keep quiet? *Finding:* Peter called him the Messiah; Jesus accepted it and at once redefined it as suffering, which no one expected.
   *Read:* Mark 8:27–33. *Compare:* Psalms of Solomon 17 (the expected warrior Messiah, tr. Ryle and James, 1891).
   *Lecture:* messianic hopes in the first century; the 'messianic secret' (Wrede, in our own words); Caesarea Philippi. *Debate:* did Jesus think he was the Messiah?
   *Learn:* `philippians-2-5`. *Look:* the Calling of Peter, or Christ giving the keys. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.
4. **What he did: miracles.** *Answers:* Did he do miracles? What were they for? *Finding:* Friends and enemies agreed Jesus did deeds of power; the Gospels present them as signs of the kingdom, not proofs on demand.
   *Read:* Mark 4:35–41 and John 6:1–15. *Compare:* the Talmud's charge that Jesus 'practised sorcery' (Sanhedrin 43a), as hostile evidence.
   *Lecture:* miracle-workers of the time (Honi, Hanina; Apollonius in Philostratus, Conybeare 1912); healing and exorcism as the best-attested kind; what the historian can and cannot say (Hume is answered in Hall VII). *Debate:* history, legend or signs?
   *Learn:* `psalm-46`. *Look:* Christ stilling the storm, or the loaves and fishes. *Music:* `it-is-well`.
5. **Prophecy fulfilled?** *Answers:* Did he fulfil the prophecies? How did his followers read the Old Testament? *Finding:* Jesus read his mission from Isaiah; his followers read the whole Old Testament as pointing to him, and some of their readings are stronger than others.
   *Read:* Luke 4:16–21 (Isaiah 61 in Nazareth); Matthew 5:17–20 is read in *The Sermon on the Mount*. *Compare:* Isaiah 61:1–3.
   *Lecture:* kinds of fulfilment (prediction, pattern, echo), pointing back to the Paine session in *The whole story* and Isaiah 53 in *Kings and prophets*; Jewish objections (the Messiah was to bring peace). *Debate:* Christian and Jewish readings side by side.
   *Learn:* `isaiah-40-8`. *Look:* Christ in the synagogue, or Isaiah. *Music:* `messiah`.
6. **The case against: just a good teacher?** *Answers:* Was he just a good moral teacher? Liar, lunatic or Lord? *Finding:* 'Just a good teacher' is the hardest view to hold once his claims are heard; but Lewis's trilemma has a fourth option, legend, which has to be answered on the evidence.
   *Read:* the Jefferson Bible's ending (Thomas Jefferson, *The Life and Morals of Jesus of Nazareth*, 1820, the last page) as the 'good teacher' view in practice. *Compare:* John 10:22–39.
   *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Jefferson, Tolstoy's Jesus, the modern sceptics' 'legend' (Ehrman, in our own words); C. S. Lewis's 'liar, lunatic or Lord' (Mere Christianity Book 2, our own words) and its critics (the fourth L, legend; John Hick). *Debate:* teacher, prophet (Jewish and Muslim views), Lord.
   *Learn:* `john-3-16`. *Look:* Christ among the doctors, or a portrait of Christ. *Music:* `sacred-head`.
7. **The verdict.** *Answers:* So who was he? What do Jews and Muslims say? *Finding:* The evidence shows a Jewish prophet who claimed an authority belonging to God and died for it; whether he was right turns on what happened next, which is the next journey.
   *Read:* John 1:1–14 is read in *Psalms and wisdom*; use Philippians 2:5–11. *Compare:* the Qur'an on Jesus, Sura 19:16–36 (Rodwell) — moved here from *One way among many?* only if that journey drops it; otherwise Josephus (point back).
   *Lecture:* Jesus in Judaism, Islam and Christianity side by side; what the creeds later said (point ahead to the hall, not to a session). *Debate:* none; the verdict.
   *Learn:* `philippians-2-5`. *Look:* Christ blessing. *Music:* `holy-holy-holy`.

**Objection:** session 6, 'just a good teacher' and the legend hypothesis, in Jefferson's and modern sceptics' terms.

**Traditions:** Jewish, Muslim, Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant views of who Jesus was; the virgin birth.
