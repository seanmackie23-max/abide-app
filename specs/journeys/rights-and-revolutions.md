**Follows:** a first-year paper in modern political thought.

**Frame:** two revolutions and their arguments: the American (rights from the Creator, government by consent, powers checked because people are not angels) and the French (rights from reason and the general will), with Burke between them and Wollstonecraft asking 'and women?'. Where do rights come from: God, nature, the state or agreement?

**Real questions:** Where do human rights come from? What is natural law? What did Locke say about rights? Why does the Declaration say 'endowed by their Creator'? What is the social contract? Why did the American and French Revolutions turn out so differently? What did Burke think? Who was Mary Wollstonecraft? Are rights Christian? Can there be rights without God?

**Builds on:** *Rome* (natural law), *The rule of law*, *Toleration* (Locke's Letter), *The New World* (Vitoria). **Read elsewhere:** Locke's Letter (*Toleration*).

## Sessions

1. **Life, liberty and property.** *Finding:* Locke argued that all people are created equal and free by God, with rights government exists to protect. *Read:* Locke, *Second Treatise* II.4–15. *Compare:* Genesis 1:27 (point back) and Richard Hooker, *Laws* I.8 (Locke's source). *Lecture:* Locke's life; the Glorious Revolution; property and labour. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of a philosopher (Locke: NGA? flag). *Music:* none.
2. **Endowed by their Creator.** *Finding:* The Declaration of Independence grounded equality and rights in the Creator and government in consent. *Read:* the Declaration, whole. *Compare:* Psalm 82:1–4 (God judges the rulers). *Lecture:* Jefferson's drafts; 'self-evident'; slavery and the Declaration (point ahead to *The end of slavery* as the hall); Lincoln's use of it. *Learn:* new `declaration-self-evident`. *Look:* a portrait of a Founder (NGA: Gilbert Stuart). *Music:* none.
3. **If men were angels.** *Finding:* Madison designed a government on the assumption that people are not angels: power must check power. *Read:* *Federalist* 10 and 51. *Compare:* Jeremiah 17:9 or Romans 3:23. *Lecture:* the Constitution; the Calvinist realism behind checks and balances; the Bill of Rights. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a civic building or Washington. *Music:* none.
4. **Liberty, equality, fraternity.** *Finding:* The French Declaration (1789) proclaimed rights from nature and reason and sovereignty from the nation; the Terror followed within four years. *Read:* the Declaration of the Rights of Man; Rousseau, *Social Contract* I.1–8. *Compare:* Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom. *Lecture:* the general will; dechristianisation (the Cult of Reason); the Vendée. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a revolutionary scene. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: rights as nonsense on stilts?** *Finding:* Burke warned that abstract rights cut loose from history and faith would destroy liberty; Bentham called natural rights 'nonsense upon stilts'; both question where rights come from. *Read:* Burke, *Reflections* (on the real rights of men; society as a partnership of the dead, the living and the unborn). *Compare:* Bentham, *Anarchical Fallacies* (1843, PD) on 'nonsense upon stilts'. *Lecture:* the objection at full strength, from right (Burke) and from utilitarian left (Bentham); MacIntyre's 'there are no such rights' (our own words). *Debate:* Burke; Bentham; Paine (*Rights of Man*, PD). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of Burke. *Music:* none.
6. **And women?** *Finding:* Wollstonecraft argued that if rights rest on reason, women have them equally, and that a Christian education of women was a matter of justice. *Read:* Wollstonecraft, *Vindication* Introduction and ch. 2. *Compare:* Galatians 3:28 (point back). *Lecture:* Wollstonecraft's life and faith; Abigail Adams's 'remember the ladies' (1776); the long road to suffrage. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a portrait of a woman writer. *Music:* none.
7. **Rights without God? The verdict.** *Finding:* The founders of modern rights grounded them in a Creator; whether they can stand without that ground is the question the twentieth century tested. *Read:* Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), preamble and arts. 1–3 (our own words; short quotation). *Compare:* Psalm 8 (point back). *Lecture:* Jacques Maritain and the UDHR; Nicholas Wolterstorff, *Justice: Rights and Wrongs* (our own words); Tom Holland's thesis (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
