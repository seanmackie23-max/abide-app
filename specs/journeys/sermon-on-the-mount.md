**Follows:** a first-year paper on the ethics of the New Testament.

**Frame:** a new law given on a mountain, like Sinai: who is blessed (5:1–16), how the old law is fulfilled (5:17–48), how to pray and give (6), and how to build on rock (7). Each session takes one block, then the objection, then the verdict.

**Real questions:** What is the Sermon on the Mount? What are the Beatitudes? Did Jesus abolish the Old Testament law? Does 'turn the other cheek' mean never resisting? Is it meant literally? Can anyone actually live this? What does 'do not judge' mean? Is Christianity a 'slave morality'? How did Martin Luther King use it?

**Builds on:** *Who was Jesus?*; *Exodus and the law* (Sinai, Leviticus 19). **Read elsewhere:** Matthew 5:1–16 is read in *A good life* (point back; begin at 5:17); the Lord's Prayer as text is in *How to pray* (here, its place in the Sermon only); Matthew 25 in *Human dignity*.

## Sessions

1. **A new Sinai.** *Answers:* What is the Sermon? Where and to whom? What are the Beatitudes? *Finding:* Matthew presents Jesus as a new Moses giving the law on a mountain, and opens with blessing, not command.
   *Read:* Luke 6:20–26 (Luke's sermon on the plain, with woes). *Compare:* Augustine, *Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount* I.1–4 (the beatitudes as seven steps).
   *Lecture:* Matthew's five discourses; Moses typology; Luke's and Matthew's versions; the Beatitudes recalled from *A good life*. *Debate:* one sermon or a collection?
   *Learn:* `matthew-the-beatitudes`. *Look:* the Sermon on the Mount. *Music:* `dear-lord-and-father`.
2. **You have heard it said.** *Answers:* Did Jesus abolish the law? What are the six antitheses? *Finding:* Jesus does not cancel the law but presses it inward: from murder to anger, adultery to lust, oath to plain truth.
   *Read:* Matthew 5:17–37. *Compare:* the rabbinic 'fence around the Torah' (*Pirkei Avot* 1:1, Taylor 1897).
   *Lecture:* fulfilment; the six antitheses; Jewish parallels (Hillel, Akiva, pointing back to *Exodus*); divorce in the traditions. *Debate:* stricter than the law, or the law's true meaning?
   *Learn:* `matthew-22-37`. *Look:* Moses with the tablets, beside Christ teaching. *Music:* `byrd-four-voices`.
3. **The other cheek.** *Answers:* Does 'turn the other cheek' mean never resisting evil? Love your enemies: really? *Finding:* The hardest teachings, non-retaliation and enemy-love, were meant to be lived, and the Church has argued ever since about how.
   *Read:* Matthew 5:38–48. *Compare:* Tolstoy, *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*, chapter I (Garnett): taken literally.
   *Lecture:* Anabaptists and Quakers; Augustine and just war (pointing ahead only to the hall *The Good Life*, not a session); Walter Wink's reading (our own words); Martin Luther King and non-violence. *Debate:* Tolstoy, Augustine, King.
   *Learn:* `luke-6-31`. *Look:* a scene of mercy to an enemy. *Music:* `precious-lord`.
4. **In secret.** *Answers:* How should we pray, give and fast? Why does Jesus say not to worry? *Finding:* Chapter 6 moves religion from the stage to the inner room: give, pray and fast for God alone, and trust him for tomorrow.
   *Read:* Matthew 6:1–34. *Compare:* Didache 8 (fast on Wednesdays and Fridays, pray the Lord's Prayer three times a day) (Lightfoot, 1891).
   *Lecture:* almsgiving, prayer and fasting in Judaism and the early Church; the Lord's Prayer in the Sermon's centre (point back to *How to pray*); 'consider the lilies'. *Debate:* is 'do not worry' realistic?
   *Learn:* `philippians-4-6`. *Look:* lilies, birds, or a figure at prayer alone. *Music:* `morning-has-broken`.
5. **Rock and sand.** *Answers:* What does 'do not judge' mean? What is the narrow gate? What is the golden rule? *Finding:* The Sermon ends with a choice between two ways and two houses: hearing is not enough, only doing.
   *Read:* Matthew 7:1–29. *Compare:* Hillel's golden rule in negative form (Shabbat 31a, pointing back to *Exodus*) beside Matthew 7:12.
   *Lecture:* the golden rule across cultures (Confucius, Isocrates, Tobit 4:15) and what is new in its positive form; 'by their fruits'. *Debate:* is the golden rule unique to Christianity?
   *Learn:* `luke-6-31` (if not used) or new `matthew-7-24`. *Look:* a house on rock, or the wise and foolish builders. *Music:* `how-great-thou-art`.
6. **The case against: a morality for slaves?** *Answers:* Is Christianity a 'slave morality'? Does it make people weak? *Finding:* Nietzsche saw clearly that the Sermon overturned the ancient world's values; whether that was a sickness or a liberation is the question.
   *Read:* Nietzsche, *The Genealogy of Morals*, First Essay §§7–10 (tr. Horace Samuel, 1913). *Compare:* Matthew 5:3–12 recalled, set against Achilles (pointing back to Homer only if *Homer* precedes; else in our own words).
   *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: master and slave morality, ressentiment, pity as weakness; Tom Holland's *Dominion* (our own words) that Nietzsche was right about the revolution; Max Scheler's reply (our own words). *Debate:* Nietzsche; Holland; King on strength to love.
   *Learn:* `kempis-love-to-be-unknown`. *Look:* a powerful classical hero beside a humble saint. *Music:* `allegri-miserere`.
7. **Can anyone live this? The verdict.** *Answers:* Is it meant literally? Can anyone live this? *Finding:* The traditions read the Sermon as law, as ideal, as mirror and as the life of the kingdom; all agree it is meant to be lived with grace, not achieved alone.
   *Read:* Augustine, *Our Lord's Sermon on the Mount* I.1–4 if not used in session 1; otherwise Bonhoeffer in our own words (*The Cost of Discipleship*, 'cheap grace'). *Compare:* 1 John 3:16–18 (Romans 12 is read in *The first Christians*).
   *Lecture:* readings side by side: Catholic (counsels and precepts), Lutheran (the mirror of the law), Anabaptist (literal), Reformed, Orthodox; King's 'Loving your enemies' sermon (our own words). *Debate:* the verdict.
   *Learn:* `luke-6-31` if not used; else `matthew-the-beatitudes`. *Look:* the Sermon on the Mount again, or a saint living it (St Martin). *Music:* `ubi-caritas`.

**Objection:** session 6, Nietzsche in his own words: Christian ethics as slave morality.

**Traditions:** counsels of perfection (Catholic), two kingdoms (Lutheran), literal discipleship (Anabaptist, Quaker), Orthodox *theosis*; Jewish readings of the antitheses.

**Voices:** Bonhoeffer, *The Cost of Discipleship*; Martin Luther King, *Strength to Love*; Tom Holland, *Dominion*; Dale Allison; Walter Wink; John Stott.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Did Jesus abolish the law?'; 'Assess Nietzsche's charge'; 'Can the Sermon guide a state?'.
