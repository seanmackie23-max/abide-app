**Follows:** a paper in science and religion (evolution and design).

**Frame:** two books of nature read before and after 1859: Paley's watch on the heath, Darwin's struggle and his tangled bank, the cruelty problem (the wasp), the Christians who welcomed evolution (Gray, Kingsley, Maxwell, Lemaître), and the question that remains: design, chance or law?

**Real questions:** What did Darwin discover? Did Darwin believe in God? What is the watchmaker argument? Does evolution disprove God? Can a Christian accept evolution? What about Genesis? Why is nature so cruel? What is intelligent design? Who was Georges Lemaître? Is the universe fine-tuned?

**Builds on:** *In the beginning* (Genesis and science; Huxley and Origin ch. XV, read), *Faith and the new science*. **Read elsewhere:** Origin ch. XV (*In the beginning*), Descent ch. XIII, XXI (*Why beauty matters*), Descent ch. 4 (*Right and wrong*, Hall IX).

## Sessions

1. **The watch on the heath.** *Finding:* Paley argued that as a watch implies a watchmaker, the eye implies a designer: the argument Darwin learned and loved at Cambridge. *Read:* Paley, *Natural Theology* ch. 1 and 3. *Compare:* Psalm 139:13–16 (point back to memory). *Lecture:* natural theology in England; the Bridgewater Treatises. *Learn:* `psalm-139-you-have-searched-me`. *Look:* a watch, or a naturalist's study. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.
2. **The voyage and the struggle.** *Finding:* Darwin's theory explained design-like features by variation and natural selection over immense time: nature's struggle produces fitted forms without a watchmaker's hand. *Read:* *Origin* ch. III. *Compare:* Malthus's *Essay on Population* (1798) ch. 1 (Darwin's spark). *Lecture:* the *Beagle*; finches and barnacles; Wallace (1858); the evidence since (genetics, DNA). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a naturalist landscape (Heade's hummingbirds are used: other). *Music:* none.
3. **Grandeur in this view.** *Finding:* Darwin ended the *Origin* with the tangled bank and 'grandeur in this view of life', and in its second edition quoted a clergyman who found evolution as noble as special creation. *Read:* *Origin* XIV, last paragraph; Kingsley's letter. *Compare:* Genesis 1:20–25 (point back). *Lecture:* reactions (Wilberforce and Huxley, 1860: myth and record); Frederick Temple; Catholic responses (*Humani Generis*, 1950, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a riverbank or meadow. *Music:* none.
4. **Design and law.** *Finding:* Asa Gray, Darwin's American champion and a Christian, argued that natural selection is how God designs: design through law rather than by tinkering. *Read:* Gray, *Darwiniana*, 'Natural Selection not Inconsistent'. *Compare:* Maxwell, 'Molecules' (the close: molecules as 'manufactured articles'). *Lecture:* theistic evolution; Aquinas on secondary causes (point back to *Aristotle*); Lemaître and the Big Bang (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a starry sky. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: the wasp.** *Finding:* Darwin could not believe a good God would design the ichneumon wasp to eat caterpillars alive; evolution's waste and cruelty is the strongest objection to design. *Read:* Darwin to Gray, 22 May 1860; Darwin's *Autobiography* on religion. *Compare:* Tennyson, *In Memoriam* LV–LVI ('Nature, red in tooth and claw'). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Richard Dawkins (*The Blind Watchmaker*, our own words); natural evil; replies (the free-process defence, John Polkinghorne; Christopher Southgate; our own words). *Debate:* Dawkins; Polkinghorne; Darwin himself. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a predator and prey scene (nature painting). *Music:* none.
6. **Intelligent design and fine-tuning.** *Finding:* Two modern arguments revive design: 'irreducible complexity' in biology (mostly rejected by biologists) and the fine-tuning of physical constants (taken seriously by many physicists). *Read:* Job 38:4–7 (point back). *Compare:* Fred Hoyle on the carbon resonance (our own words). *Lecture:* Behe and the Dover trial (2005); fine-tuning and the multiverse; the god of the gaps. *Debate:* Behe; Francis Collins; Sean Carroll (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* the night sky. *Music:* none.
7. **Room for God: the verdict.** *Finding:* Evolution removed one argument for God (Paley's) but not God; most Christian traditions accept it, and the deeper questions (why there are laws at all, why the universe is intelligible) remain. *Read:* Maxwell's prayer (PD). *Compare:* Hopkins, 'Pied Beauty' (PD). *Lecture:* the traditions' views side by side; creationism; what evolution means for being human (point back to *Dust that wonders*). *Learn:* `hopkins-gods-grandeur`. *Look:* a landscape of abundance. *Music:* `all-creatures`.

**Objection:** session 5, the wasp, in Darwin's and Dawkins's terms.

**Traditions:** Catholic (*Humani Generis*, John Paul II 1996), Orthodox, mainline Protestant, evangelical (old-earth, young-earth) side by side.

**Voices:** Richard Dawkins; Francis Collins, *The Language of God*; John Polkinghorne; Michael Behe; Simon Conway Morris; Janet Browne (Darwin's biographer).

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'Did Darwin refute Paley?'; 'Is natural evil a problem for theism?'; 'Fine-tuning'.
