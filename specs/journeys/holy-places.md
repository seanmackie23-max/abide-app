**Follows:** a paper in religious studies (sacred space and pilgrimage).

**Frame:** two movements: places where heaven touches earth (Bethel, the Temple, the shrine), and the journey to them (pilgrimage). The journey visits the holy place in the Bible, Jesus's challenge to it, the first pilgrims to Jerusalem, Canterbury and Compostela, the Reformers' objection, and the pilgrim today. (Holy *time* is in *Time made holy*.)

**Real questions:** Why are some places holy? What is a pilgrimage? What is the Camino de Santiago? Why do people go to Jerusalem, Rome, Canterbury, Lourdes? Is God more present in some places? What are relics? Did Jesus abolish holy places? Why did the Reformers oppose pilgrimages? Can a non-believer be a pilgrim?

**Builds on:** *Light, stone and glass* (the church as a building), *Time made holy*. **Read elsewhere:** Genesis 28 (*Abraham*), 1 Kings 8 (*Light, stone and glass*).

## Sessions

1. **This is the gate of heaven.** *Finding:* In the Bible certain places become holy because God meets people there: Bethel, Sinai, the Temple. *Read:* Psalm 122 (a pilgrim song) with point back to Genesis 28. *Compare:* Eliade on the sacred and the profane (our own words). *Lecture:* sacred space across religions; Otto's 'numinous' (our own words). *Learn:* `psalm-121`. *Look:* Jacob's ladder or a mountain sanctuary. *Music:* `locus-iste`.
2. **The river from the Temple.** *Finding:* Ezekiel saw a river flowing from the Temple to heal the world: the holy place exists for what flows out of it. *Read:* Ezekiel 47:1–12. *Compare:* Revelation 22:1–2 (point back). *Lecture:* the Temple's geography; holiness as contagious. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a river landscape or a fountain of life. *Music:* none.
3. **Neither on this mountain.** *Finding:* Jesus told a Samaritan woman that true worshippers worship 'in spirit and truth', not only on one mountain: the Christian holy place became the person and the gathered church. *Read:* John 4:19–24. *Compare:* 1 Corinthians 3:16 (point back). *Lecture:* Gerizim and Jerusalem; Christian holy places as memory, not containers of God. *Learn:* new `john-4-24`. *Look:* Christ and the woman of Samaria. *Music:* none.
4. **The first pilgrims.** *Finding:* By the fourth century pilgrims were walking to Jerusalem to see where it happened; a woman named Egeria wrote it all down. *Read:* Egeria, *Pilgrimage* 24–25 and 36–37 (Holy Week in Jerusalem). *Compare:* Jerome's account of Paula (Letter 108). *Lecture:* Helena; the Church of the Holy Sepulchre; the origin of Holy Week liturgy. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Jerusalem or a pilgrim. *Music:* `all-glory-laud`.
5. **When April comes.** *Finding:* In the Middle Ages pilgrimage was the great journey of ordinary life: Canterbury, Compostela and Rome, for penance, healing and adventure. *Read:* Chaucer, *General Prologue* 1–18. *Compare:* the *Codex Calixtinus* on the road to Santiago (our own words). *Lecture:* Becket's shrine; the Camino; relics and indulgences; badges and souvenirs. *Learn:* new `chaucer-whan-that-aprille` (Middle English). *Look:* pilgrims on the road; St James. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: superstition on foot?** *Finding:* Erasmus mocked the pilgrims' relics, and the Reformers destroyed the shrines, saying God is everywhere and grace cannot be earned by walking. *Read:* Erasmus, *A Pilgrimage for Religion's Sake* (1526) (tr. Nichols 1849 / Bailey). *Compare:* the Injunctions of 1538 ordering the removal of shrines (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: relic fraud, indulgences, commercialism; the Reformation's iconoclasm (Duffy, MacCulloch, our own words). *Debate:* Erasmus; a medieval pilgrim; a Protestant. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a relic or shrine. *Music:* none.
7. **The pilgrim today: the verdict.** *Finding:* Pilgrimage has returned, even among people with little faith; the Christian answer is that every journey can become one if it is walked towards God. *Read:* Psalm 84:5–7 ('blessed are those whose hearts are set on pilgrimage') — point back if memory used; read Hebrews 11:13–16 (strangers and pilgrims; point back if used in *Abraham*). *Compare:* George Herbert, 'The Pilgrimage' or Raleigh, 'The Passionate Man's Pilgrimage'. *Lecture:* the Camino's revival (over 400,000 a year, cite); Walsingham, Iona, Lourdes; how to make a local pilgrimage. *Learn:* `psalm-84-how-lovely`. *Look:* a road through a landscape. *Music:* `be-thou-my-vision`.

**Objection:** session 6, Erasmus and the Reformers.
