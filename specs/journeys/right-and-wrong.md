**Follows:** a first-year paper in moral philosophy.

**Frame:** four ways people have answered 'why is this wrong?', set side by side and tested on the same cases: God's command and natural law, virtue (what a good person would do), duty (what reason requires), consequences (what does the most good); then Nietzsche's and Darwin's attack on all four; then the verdict. Opens the hall, before *The four virtues*.

**Real questions:** Where does morality come from? Can you be good without God? What is the Euthyphro dilemma? What is natural law? What is utilitarianism? What is Kant's categorical imperative? What is virtue ethics? Is morality just evolution? Is morality relative? How do I decide what is right?

**Builds on:** *A good life* (Aristotle I.7), *Human dignity* (Kant's humanity formula), *Exodus* (the ten words), *Aristotle*, *The death of God?*. **Read elsewhere:** those passages; Genealogy I §§7–10 (*Sermon on the Mount*).

## Sessions

1. **Is it good because God commands it?** *Finding:* Socrates asked whether the holy is loved by the gods because it is holy, or holy because they love it: the question every theory of morality must answer. *Read:* *Euthyphro* 9e–11b. *Compare:* Exodus 20 (point back). *Lecture:* divine command theory; the dilemma's horns; the theist reply (good is God's nature: Aquinas, Robert Adams, our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* Socrates (point back) or Moses with the tablets. *Music:* none.
2. **Written on the heart.** *Finding:* Natural law says the basic goods (life, knowledge, friendship, God) are known to everyone, and the first precept is 'do good and avoid evil'. *Read:* Aquinas, *Summa* I–II q.94 a.2. *Compare:* Romans 2:14–15 (point back) and C. S. Lewis's Tao in *The Abolition of Man* (our own words). *Lecture:* natural law from Cicero (point back) to Finnis (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
3. **The kind of person.** *Finding:* Virtue ethics asks not 'what rule?' but 'what would a good person do?'; MacIntyre argued the modern world lost the tradition that made moral language make sense. *Read:* point back to Aristotle; read MacIntyre's thesis (our own words) beside Philippians 4:8. *Compare:* Hume on virtue (point back to *Scotland*). *Lecture:* virtue ethics' revival (Anscombe 1958, MacIntyre 1981, our own words). *Learn:* `by-doing-just-actions`. *Look:* a portrait of virtue. *Music:* none.
4. **Duty.** *Finding:* Kant argued that only a good will is good without limit, and that we must act only on rules we could will everyone to follow. *Read:* Kant, *Groundwork*, First Section. *Compare:* Luke 6:31 (point back) and the golden rule. *Lecture:* the categorical imperative; lying to the murderer at the door; Kant and Christianity. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
5. **The greatest happiness.** *Finding:* Mill argued that actions are right as they tend to promote happiness; utilitarianism is powerful, simple, and troubling in hard cases. *Read:* Mill, *Utilitarianism* ch. II. *Compare:* John 11:49–50 (Caiaphas: better one man die for the people). *Lecture:* Bentham, Mill, Singer (our own words); the transplant case; rule-utilitarianism. *Debate:* Kant or Mill? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: only evolved feeling?** *Finding:* Darwin traced the moral sense to social instinct; Nietzsche called morality a weapon of the weak; together they argue conscience is evolved feeling and custom, with no authority. *Read:* Darwin, *Descent of Man* ch. IV; Nietzsche, *Genealogy* I §§10–17. *Compare:* Lewis, *Abolition of Man* (our own words: 'men without chests'). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: evolutionary debunking (Sharon Street, Richard Joyce, our own words); relativism; replies (moral realism; Lewis; the argument from reason). *Debate:* Nietzsche; Darwin; Lewis. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.
7. **How do we know? The verdict.** *Finding:* Each theory sees something true: rules matter, character matters, persons matter, outcomes matter; the Christian tradition holds them together in love of God and neighbour, and grounds them in a good God. *Read:* Matthew 22:37–40 (point back to memory). *Compare:* Micah 6:8 (point back). *Lecture:* the four theories as one table; how to decide in practice (conscience, counsel, Scripture, prayer). *Learn:* `matthew-22-37`. *Look:* none. *Music:* none.

**Objection:** session 6.

**Traditions:** Catholic natural law, Protestant divine command and Reformed ethics, Orthodox theosis, Jewish halakha side by side.

**Voices:** C. S. Lewis, *The Abolition of Man*; Alasdair MacIntyre; Elizabeth Anscombe; Peter Singer; Sharon Street; Robert Adams.

**Closing:** short answers; exam: 'The Euthyphro dilemma'; 'Kant or Mill?'; 'Can evolution explain morality?'.
