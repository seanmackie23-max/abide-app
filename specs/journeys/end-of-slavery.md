**Follows:** a paper on the history of slavery and abolition.

**Frame:** a puzzle: slavery existed in almost every civilisation, and the Bible was quoted to defend it; yet the first mass movement to abolish it began among Christians in Britain and America. The journey hears the enslaved (Equiano), the slave-ship captain turned clergyman (Newton), the student who could not let it go (Clarkson), the parliamentarian (Wilberforce), the critics who say Christians were the problem, and the verdict.

**Real questions:** Why did slavery end? Did the Bible support slavery? Who started the abolition movement? Who was Olaudah Equiano? Who was William Wilberforce? Did Britain profit from slavery? Was abolition about religion or economics? What did John Newton write? Did Christians defend slavery? Is there still slavery today?

**Builds on:** *How we got here* (Galatians, Philemon, Douglass), *Human dignity*, *Exodus*, *The New World*. **Read elsewhere:** Philemon (*How we got here*), Douglass (*How we got here*, *Human dignity*, *Exodus*), Galatians 3:28.

## Sessions

1. **A world of slaves.** *Finding:* Slavery was universal and taken for granted; a few early Christians, like Gregory of Nyssa, condemned it outright, while most accepted it. *Read:* Exodus 21:16 and 1 Timothy 1:10 (man-stealing condemned). *Compare:* Gregory of Nyssa on Ecclesiastes 2:7 (our own words, short quotation). *Lecture:* slavery in the ancient, Islamic, African and European worlds; medieval decline in Europe; Bathild and the redemption of captives. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a scene of captives freed (St Peter Nolasco?). *Music:* none.
2. **Am I not a man?** *Finding:* Equiano, kidnapped as a child, wrote the first great account of the slave trade by one who survived it, and used the Bible against his enslavers. *Read:* Equiano, *Narrative* ch. 2. *Compare:* Psalm 137 (point back to memory) or Isaiah 61:1. *Lecture:* the Atlantic trade (12.5 million embarked, figures cited); the Middle Passage; Equiano's faith. *Learn:* new Equiano line. *Look:* a portrait of an African man of the period (NGA: confirm). *Music:* `amazing-grace`.
3. **Amazing grace.** *Finding:* John Newton, a former slave-ship captain, wrote 'Amazing Grace' and later a public confession that he had been part of a terrible trade. *Read:* Newton, *Thoughts upon the African Slave Trade*. *Compare:* the hymn 'Amazing Grace' (1779, PD). *Lecture:* Newton's life; how slowly his conscience moved; Cowper's 'The Negro's Complaint'. *Learn:* new `amazing-grace` memory (PD hymn). *Look:* a ship at sea. *Music:* `aretha-amazing-grace`.
4. **The Quakers and the Clapham sect.** *Finding:* Quakers protested first (Germantown, 1688); Clarkson's essay and Wilberforce's evangelical friends turned protest into a national campaign of petitions, boycotts and the kneeling-slave medallion. *Read:* Clarkson, *History* I.7 (Wades Mill); the Germantown Protest. *Compare:* Wilberforce speech of 1789 (a passage not in Worth remembering). *Lecture:* the campaign's methods (the first modern pressure group); 1807 and 1833; the Royal Navy's West Africa Squadron. *Learn:* `wilberforce-1789`. *Look:* Wilberforce (portrait exists in Practice) or a campaign scene. *Music:* none.
5. **The case against: Christians made slavery, and economics ended it?** *Finding:* Christian nations ran the Atlantic trade, the Church of England's own SPG owned slaves in Barbados, preachers defended slavery from the Bible; Eric Williams argued abolition came only when slavery stopped paying. *Read:* a pro-slavery sermon (e.g. Thornton Stringfellow, 'A Brief Examination of Scripture Testimony', 1841, PD). *Compare:* Ephesians 6:5–9 and the 'Slave Bible' (1807) that cut the Exodus (our own words). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength: Eric Williams, *Capitalism and Slavery* (1944; our own words), and Seymour Drescher's reply (*Econocide*, our own words); the Codrington plantations; compensation in 1833. *Debate:* Williams; Drescher; Douglass. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a plantation scene (with care). *Music:* `were-you-there`.
6. **Let my people go.** *Finding:* In America the enslaved themselves made the Bible a book of freedom: the Exodus in the spirituals, Douglass's prophetic voice, Tubman's Moses. *Read:* the spirituals 'Go down, Moses' (PD) and 'Steal away'. *Compare:* Exodus 3:7–10 (point back). *Lecture:* the black church; Nat Turner; the abolitionists (Garrison, Stowe); the Civil War and the Thirteenth Amendment. *Learn:* `go-down-moses`. *Look:* a portrait of Douglass or Tubman (Practice portraits). *Music:* `precious-lord`.
7. **Why the West? The verdict.** *Finding:* Slavery was a human universal; the idea that it was a sin requiring abolition grew from Christian convictions about the image of God and the equality of souls, pressed by the enslaved and by believers against other believers. *Read:* Wilberforce, *A Practical View of Christianity* (1797), selected. *Compare:* Galatians 3:28 (point back). *Lecture:* slavery today (ILO figures, cited); David Brion Davis and Tom Holland on why the West (our own words). *Learn:* none new. *Look:* none. *Music:* `amazing-grace`.

**Objection:** session 5, Christian complicity and the economic explanation.
