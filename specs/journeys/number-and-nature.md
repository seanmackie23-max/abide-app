**Follows:** a first-year paper in the history of ancient science and philosophy.

**Frame:** the Greek wager that the world can be understood by reason: from Thales's water and Pythagoras's numbers, through Euclid's proofs and Archimedes' measurements, to Hippocrates' natural causes of disease; then what the Bible said about an orderly world, and whether science needs God.

**Real questions:** Who was the first philosopher? Who was Pythagoras? What did Euclid prove? What did Archimedes discover? Did the Greeks know the earth was round? What is the Hippocratic Oath? Why did science begin in Greece? Did Christianity stop Greek science? Does science need God?

**Builds on:** *In the beginning* (Genesis and science); *Is there a God?* (Psalm 19). **Read elsewhere:** Genesis 1 (several journeys).

## Sessions

1. **Everything is water.** *Finding:* Around 600 BC, in Ionia, Thales and his successors began to explain the world by natural causes rather than myths. *Read:* Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.3–5. *Compare:* Genesis 1:1–2 (point back), as a different kind of answer. *Lecture:* Thales, Anaximander, Heraclitus, Parmenides; the Ionian miracle. *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a philosopher with globe or instruments. *Music:* none.
2. **All is number.** *Finding:* Pythagoras's school discovered that music and the world follow mathematical ratios, and saw number as divine. *Read:* Diogenes Laertius VIII.1–36 (Yonge). *Compare:* Wisdom of Solomon 11:20. *Lecture:* the Pythagorean theorem's history; the music of the spheres; the scandal of irrational numbers. *Learn:* new `wisdom-11-20`. *Look:* a musician or astronomer. *Music:* `spem-in-alium` (ratios).
3. **Proof.** *Finding:* Euclid built all of geometry from a few definitions and postulates: the model of certain knowledge for two thousand years. *Read:* Euclid, *Elements* I, definitions 1–23, postulates 1–5, propositions 1 and 47 (Heath). *Compare:* Lincoln studying Euclid to learn what 'demonstrate' means (his 1860 autobiographical note, our own words). *Lecture:* axioms; the parallel postulate and non-Euclidean geometry. *Learn:* none. *Look:* a geometer. *Music:* none.
4. **Give me a place to stand.** *Finding:* Archimedes measured, calculated and built, joining mathematics to the physical world. *Read:* Archimedes, *The Sand-Reckoner* (Heath). *Compare:* Eratosthenes measuring the earth (Cleomedes, our own words). *Lecture:* the round earth (Aristotle's evidence, Eratosthenes); the Antikythera mechanism; why the Greeks did not industrialise. *Learn:* none. *Look:* an astronomer or Archimedes. *Music:* none.
5. **First, do no harm.** *Finding:* Hippocratic medicine refused to blame the gods for disease and bound doctors by an oath to the patient's good. *Read:* *On the Sacred Disease* 1–5 and the *Oath* (Adams). *Compare:* Luke 10:30–37 (the Good Samaritan; or point back). *Lecture:* the Oath's clauses (abortion, euthanasia, pointing ahead to *Hard cases* as the hall); Galen; Christian hospitals (Basil's Basiliad). *Learn:* new Hippocratic line (Adams). *Look:* a doctor or physician. *Music:* none.
6. **The case against: did Christianity kill Greek science?** *Finding:* The popular story says Christians burned the library and ended science; historians say the decline had other causes and that monks, Muslims and medieval universities preserved and extended Greek science. *Read:* Gibbon, *Decline and Fall* ch. XXVIII on the Serapeum (391). *Compare:* Hypatia in Socrates Scholasticus VII.15 (NPNF II.2). *Lecture:* the objection at full strength (Gibbon, Draper and White, Carl Sagan's *Cosmos*, our own words); replies (David Lindberg, Ronald Numbers, our own words); John Philoponus. *Debate:* conflict thesis or myth? *Learn:* none new. *Look:* a library or a scholar. *Music:* none.
7. **Does science need God? The verdict.** *Finding:* Greek reason trusted the world to be orderly; the biblical belief in a rational Creator gave later science a reason to expect laws and to test them. *Read:* Plato, *Timaeus* 27c–30c. *Compare:* Psalm 19 recalled; Kepler's 'thinking God's thoughts after him' (point ahead to Hall VI as the hall). *Lecture:* Whitehead's argument in *Science and the Modern World* (1925, our own words); objections. *Learn:* `psalm-19-1`. *Look:* the night sky. *Music:* `haydn-creation`.

**Objection:** session 6, the conflict thesis, in Gibbon's and Draper's terms.
