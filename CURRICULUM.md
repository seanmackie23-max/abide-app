# The curriculum: what Rose Window teaches, and why

Rose Window's learning journeys, taken together, trace the argument of the West from Genesis to the present: its two roots (Athens and Jerusalem), Rome's law and order, the Church's thousand-year synthesis, the Renaissance and Reformation, the new science, the Enlightenment, and the great critics who followed. The test for every author and journey is simple: is it foundational to the West? Writers who shaped it from outside the faith belong where they were part of the foundation and the faith had to answer them, Hume, Darwin and Nietzsche among them, stated fairly. Movements that set out to replace the West's foundations, such as Marxism, are not part of the curriculum.

The plan lives in `content/halls.json`: each hall's `journeys` (written) and `later` (to be written). Each planned journey has a `question`, its set `texts` (out of copyright, usable verbatim), its `voices` (still in copyright, so in our own words only) and sometimes a `note` on why it is there. When you write a journey, follow `JOURNEYS.md` and move it from `later` into `journeys`.

## The halls

Nine halls laid out as a cathedral and walked in order, so that each builds on the ones before: the two Testaments, the three roots meeting at the crossing, the history in order, then the great questions and the good life at the altar. An orientation journey, *How we got here*, comes first.

| Hall | Place | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| I The Old Testament | west door | Can it be trusted; the whole story; Genesis, Abraham, Exodus and the law, kings and prophets, psalms and wisdom; how to read the Bible |
| II Jesus and the New Testament | nave | Who Jesus was, the Sermon on the Mount, the last week, the cross and the resurrection, the first Christians, how Christianity parted from Judaism, what happens at church, the end of the story |
| III Athens | nave | The Greeks: Homer, tragedy, democracy, the historians, number and nature, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Epicureans and Stoics |
| IV Rome and the First Church | the crossing | Where the roots meet: Cicero, Virgil and Roman law; honour and duty; persuasion; Athens meets Jerusalem; the martyrs; Augustine; the fall of Rome |
| V The Middle Ages | north transept | Monks, reason and revelation, universities, cathedrals, music, the church year, holy places, chivalry, the Crusades faced honestly, the rule of law, Dante, the mystics |
| VI Reformation and Renaissance | south transept | The Reformation, the King James Bible, the New World and the rights of man, toleration, Shakespeare, the poets, the new science, Pascal |
| VII The Modern World | choir | Rights, the end of slavery, Scotland's Enlightenment, miracles, Darwin, the death of God, Dostoevsky, the twentieth century, modern theology, the return of belief, the world church, in defence of the West |
| VIII The Great Questions | choir | After the whole story: God, the Creed, the human person and human dignity, evil, suffering, Job, death, other faiths |
| IX The Good Life | apse | Right and wrong, the virtues, faith, hope and love, the sins, love and marriage, work and money, the poor, forgiveness, hard cases, beauty, prayer, silence, discernment, and the last journey, Come and see |

Every hall faces its hardest charge squarely (slavery, the Crusades, conquest, the wars of religion, the twentieth century), because the West's own principles are what condemned those wrongs and corrected them. Each hall ends with a short 'make the case' essay: defend one idea from the hall and answer the strongest objection to it. The questions people actually search for (Is there a God? Why do we suffer? What is the Bible? How should I live?) run through the halls as threads.

## How it compares with a theology degree

Rose Window is not a degree and must never claim to be one or name a university in the app. But it is written at that depth, and its coverage was checked against first-year theology at Oxford (Introduction to the Study of the Bible, The Figure of Jesus through the Centuries, Religion and Religions), Cambridge (David, Jesus and the origins of the Gospel, Christianity and the transformation of culture, the Question of God, philosophy of religion, ethics, contemporary and world religions) and Edinburgh (Biblical Studies, History of Christianity, World Christianity, Religious Studies, Theology and Ethics).

Three gaps against those courses are now in the plan: **doctrine** (*The Great Questions*, Hall IV), **how the Bible was written and read critically** (*What is the Bible?*, Hall I), and **other faiths and the world church** (*One way among many?*, Hall IV, and *The world church*, Hall VII). Rose Window goes beyond a theology degree in joining it to the classics, the arts and spiritual practice. It cannot offer languages, full degree-scale reading or a real tutorial; the tutorial question (and, when switched on, the AI guide) is the nearest thing.

Hall by hall, in order, starting with the two Testaments (✓ written to the playbook):

II Jesus and the New Testament: Who was Jesus? (rewrite; 'just a good teacher') · The Sermon on the Mount (Nietzsche) · The last week and the cross (did he rise?) · Why the cross? · The first Christians (did Paul invent Christianity?) · One root, two branches (did the New Testament teach contempt for Jews?) · What happens at church · The end of the story (failed prophecy).

Each source is read once: outside evidence (Josephus, Tacitus) lives in What is the Bible?, Galatians 3 and Romans 4 in Abraham, the cross and resurrection in The last week, Pliny in The first Christians. The details are in `content/halls.json` (`later` and `rewrite`).
