# The curriculum: what Rose Window teaches, and why

Rose Window's learning journeys, taken together, trace the argument of the West from Genesis to the present: its two roots (Athens and Jerusalem), Rome's law and order, the Church's thousand-year synthesis, the Renaissance and Reformation, the new science, the Enlightenment, and the great critics who followed. The critics are part of the story, not left out: you cannot understand faith today without Marx, Nietzsche, Freud and Darwin, stated fairly.

The plan lives in `content/halls.json`: each hall's `journeys` (written) and `later` (to be written). Each planned journey has a `question`, its set `texts` (out of copyright, usable verbatim), its `voices` (still in copyright, so in our own words only) and sometimes a `note` on why it is there. When you write a journey, follow `JOURNEYS.md` and move it from `later` into `journeys`.

## The halls

Laid out as a cathedral, walked from the west door to the apse:

| Hall | Place | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| I The Great Story | west door | The Bible as one story, and how it came to be |
| II The Great Questions | nave | God, doctrine, suffering, death, other faiths, science, doubt |
| III Know Thyself | nave | Desire, love, pride, evil, guilt and forgiveness |
| VI Athens, Rome and Jerusalem | transept | The history of the West's ideas, from Homer to modern theology |
| VII The City | transept | Conscience, rights, power, war, family, the Scottish Enlightenment, the world church |
| IV The Noble Life | choir | The virtues and the vices |
| VIII Beauty | choir | Art, architecture, music, poetry, the novel, the moral imagination |
| V The Inner Life | apse | Prayer and practice |

Each hall's first journey is its way in; later ones go deeper (foundation, intermediate, advanced, like the years of a degree).

## How it compares with a theology degree

Rose Window is not a degree and must never claim to be one or name a university in the app. But it is written at that depth, and its coverage was checked against first-year theology at Oxford (Introduction to the Study of the Bible, The Figure of Jesus through the Centuries, Religion and Religions), Cambridge (David, Jesus and the origins of the Gospel, Christianity and the transformation of culture, the Question of God, philosophy of religion, ethics, contemporary and world religions) and Edinburgh (Biblical Studies, History of Christianity, World Christianity, Religious Studies, Theology and Ethics).

Three gaps against those courses are now in the plan: **doctrine** (*What Christians believe*, Hall II), **how the Bible was written and read critically** (*How the Bible came to be*, Hall I), and **other faiths and the world church** (*One way among many?*, Hall II, and *The world church*, Hall VII). Rose Window goes beyond a theology degree in joining it to the classics, the arts and spiritual practice. It cannot offer languages, full degree-scale reading or a real tutorial; the tutorial question (and, when switched on, the AI guide) is the nearest thing.
